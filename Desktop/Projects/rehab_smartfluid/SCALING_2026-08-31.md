## 1. The question

Every shear-thickening damper model in the device literature is quasi-steady: it
reads viscosity off a measured steady flow curve at the instantaneous shear rate.
That is legitimate only when the microstructure equilibrates much faster than the
mechanical event the device exists to resist.

In a dense suspension the microstructure does not relax on a *time*. It relaxes
on a *strain*. So the question is whether a gait event contains enough strain.

## 2. The groups

The frictional contact fraction `f` obeys

    d f / d gamma = (f_ss(sigma) - f) / gamma_0

with Wyart-Cates supplying `f_ss(sigma) = exp(-(sigma*/sigma)^beta)`,
`phi_J(f) = (1-f) phi_0 + f phi_m` and `eta = eta_s (1 - phi/phi_J)^-n`. Stress is
algebraic given `f`, so `f` is the only state variable.

Nondimensionalising leaves five groups, and one of them is the point:

| Group | Meaning | Value here |
|---|---|---|
| `A = gamma_dot tau / gamma_0` | **strain available, in units of the clock** | **0.19 to 16** (§3) |
| `Lambda = eta_s gamma_dot / sigma*` | solvent stress in units of onset stress | order 1 by construction |
| `phi/phi_0` | packing, gauge-invariant form | 0.63 to 0.88 |
| `phi_m/phi_0` | frictional packing, gauge-invariant form | 0.84 |
| `n`, `beta` | divergence exponent, activation sharpness | 2, 1 |

Only the *ratios* of the three packing fractions appear. Wyart-Cates is exactly
covariant under `(phi, phi_m, phi_0) -> c (phi, phi_m, phi_0)`, so `phi_0` is a
gauge choice, not a design knob, and sweeping it re-measures algebra. This is
checked to 1e-12 in `test_gauge_covariance_holds_to_machine_precision`.

**The quasi-steady assumption is the single statement `A -> infinity`.**

## 3. A for real gait, with the arithmetic

Critical shear rate band for the published STF prosthetic foot, measured:
`gamma_dot = 10` to `80 1/s` (Smart Mater. Struct. 2020).

Event duration. The loading response spans 2% to 12% of the gait cycle, and a
cycle at normal walking speed is about 1.0 s with a 0.6 s stance, so weight
acceptance is `tau ~ 0.100 s`. A stumble or impact event is shorter; `tau ~ 0.030 s`
is used below as a stand-in and is **not sourced**, unlike the other two numbers.

Strain clock, measured from Lin et al. (2015) shear-reversal transients in
`dst_fit/STRAIN_CLOCK_2026-08-28.md`: `gamma_0 = 0.5` to `1.6`. Not the 0.2 that
drop-impact solvers borrow from corn starch.

Walking, weight acceptance:

    A_min = 10 * 0.100 / 1.6 = 0.625
    A_max = 80 * 0.100 / 0.5 = 16.0

Stumble:

    A_min = 10 * 0.030 / 1.6 = 0.1875
    A_max = 80 * 0.030 / 0.5 = 4.8

**A is order one across the design band, and for a stumble it does not reach 10
anywhere in that band.** The quasi-steady limit is not attained in the event a
fall-protection device exists for.

## 4. What the shortfall costs

Peak stress delivered, divided by the steady stress a quasi-steady design would
have specified. Computed by `scripts/run_design_map.py`, panel (a).

| `A` | phi = 0.40 | 0.46 | 0.50 | 0.52 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.19 | 0.779 | 0.441 | 0.163 | **0.053** |
| 0.30 | 0.793 | 0.462 | 0.178 | 0.059 |
| 1.00 | 0.868 | 0.592 | 0.292 | 0.123 |
| 3.00 | 0.968 | 0.862 | 0.709 | 0.549 |
| 10.0 | 1.000 | 0.999 | 0.999 | 0.999 |

Two readings, and the second is the one that matters.

**Quasi-steady is safe above `A ~ 10`**, for every packing tested. That is a clean
threshold and it is worth designing to.

**The shortfall amplifies with packing fraction.** At `A = 0.3` a device
specified quasi-steady delivers 79% of its design stress at `phi = 0.40` and
**5.9%** at `phi = 0.52`. The error is not a uniform correction: it grows as the
material approaches jamming, which is exactly the direction a designer moves in
to get a stronger effect. **The assumption fails hardest where the device is most
attractive.**

## 5. The other constraint, from the same model

Largest steady shear rate a packing can sustain before shear jamming, panel (b):

| phi | 0.40 | 0.46 | 0.50 | 0.52 | 0.54 = phi_m | 0.56 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gamma_dot_max` (1/s) | 95646 | 31248 | 7822 | 1960 | **7.8** | 4.1 |

The ceiling falls through the gait band at `phi = phi_m` and drops by a factor of
250 between 0.52 and 0.54. Above `phi_m` the material jams *below* the rates it
would ever see in walking, so it is not a damper, it is a solid.

**The two constraints squeeze from opposite sides.** Raising `phi` strengthens
the thickening but deepens the transient shortfall (§4) and closes the reachable
rate window (§5). The usable window here is roughly `phi = 0.40` to `0.52`, and
inside it the designer is trading effect size against how much of that effect
actually arrives during the event.

Neither constraint is expressible in a Bouc-Wen or Bingham damper model, because
neither contains `phi`. That is the layer-2 claim in the design document, made
concrete.

## 6. What would falsify this

- **A measured force-displacement loop on an STF damper that matches its own
  steady flow curve at `A < 3`.** That would say the strain clock is much shorter
  than 0.5, or that the device geometry supplies strain the 0-D estimate misses.
- **`gamma_0 << 0.2` for a silica/PEG STF.** `A` scales as `1/gamma_0`, so a
  clock ten times shorter moves every column of §4 to the right by a decade and
  the effect disappears. The 0.5 to 1.6 bracket is measured on silica and PMMA in
  a different solvent, and its transfer to PEG is an assumption, not a result.
- **Rate ceiling above the gait band at `phi > phi_m`.** Would mean the
  divergence exponent or `phi_m` is badly wrong for these materials.

## 7. Limitations, stated rather than buried

1. **The material is not a fit to anything.** `eta_s = 0.0703 Pa.s` is *solved*
   so the lubricated stress equals `sigma*` at 100 1/s, placing the fixture at
   the onset rather than in a dead regime. Absolute stresses are therefore
   arbitrary; the ratios in §4 and §5 are the result. It is a corroboration, not
   an input, that 0.07 Pa.s is PEG-like rather than water-like.
2. **`gamma_0 = 1.0` in the map**, mid-bracket. `A` scales inversely with it, so
   §4's columns shift by a factor of 3.2 across the measured range.
3. **Constant-rate step, not a gait waveform.** This is the harshest case for
   quasi-steady; a real ramp accumulates strain more gradually. Layer 1 replaces
   this in phase 1.
4. **The comparison is against the lowest steady branch.** Where the flow curve
   is S-shaped, `steady_stress` returns the low-stress solution, so §4
   *understates* the shortfall.
5. **0-D homogeneous shear.** Athani et al. (arXiv:2408.15130) show transient
   stresses approaching shear jamming do not diverge the way steady state
   predicts and that hydrodynamics matters there. A 0-D model cannot see it.
6. **Wyart-Cates has no microstructure variable**, so it cannot describe flow
   reversal, and gait reverses direction every cycle. `dst_fit`'s own reversal
   work found the post-reversal minimum sits below the model's frictionless
   floor on every record. This is a known wrong region, not an untested one.
7. **The stumble duration, 30 ms, is unsourced.** The 100 ms weight-acceptance
   figure and the 10 to 80 1/s band are both sourced.
