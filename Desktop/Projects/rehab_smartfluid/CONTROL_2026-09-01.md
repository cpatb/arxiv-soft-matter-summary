# Layer 3: what a semi-active smart-fluid element can actually be commanded to do

**Date:** 2026-09-01 · **Code:** `layer3/semiactive.py` · **Tests:** 6 pass
**Study:** `scripts/run_control_study.py` (§3 below is closed form and independent of it;
the tracking panel was still running when this was written)

The design document claims layer 3 is "genuine control theory rather than a slider"
because a semi-active element is dissipative only. That is true and it is not the
interesting part. The interesting part is what the strain clock does to the
actuator.

---

## 1. Three properties of this plant

Modelling the field as moving the onset stress `sigma*`, which is what it
physically does in an MR fluid, the plant is

    df/dt = gamma_dot (f_ss(sigma; sigma*) - f) / gamma_0,    sigma = eta(f) gamma_dot
    F     = eta(f) gamma_dot S

**The actuator has no instantaneous authority.** The control appears nowhere in
the force. It enters only through `df/dt`, so the relative degree from field to
force is one. Clipped-optimal and skyhook laws are written for an actuator whose
damping switches immediately; this one cannot. Checked in
`test_field_has_no_instantaneous_force_authority`.

**The lag is a strain, so the bandwidth depends on speed.** Linearising about a
steady operating point gives a first-order system with

    tau_c = gamma_0 / gamma_dot,        omega_c = gamma_dot / gamma_0

The actuator gets faster the harder you shear it. Verified against an exact
first-order step response at vanishing packing, where the stress feedback is off
and the closed form is available: the response reaches `1 - 1/e` at `t = tau_c`.

**At rest the actuator is dead.** `gamma_dot -> 0` sends the bandwidth to zero.
With no relative motion the microstructure cannot be driven anywhere at all,
whatever the field. **A controller cannot pre-position this device before an
impact.** It has to earn its state during the event. This is the discriminating
negative in the test file: a conventional damper model, whose coefficient is a
direct function of the command, passes trivially where this plant must not move.

## 2. The unification

The number of actuator time constants available in an event of duration `tau` is

    tau / tau_c = gamma_dot tau / gamma_0 = A

the same activation number that governs the passive shortfall in layer 2.

**`A` is simultaneously the material's strain budget and the actuator's control
authority budget.** When `A < 1` the passive material has not finished thickening
and the active controller has not completed one response, for the same reason and
by the same arithmetic. Layers 2 and 3 do not fail independently; they fail
together, and one number predicts both.

This is asserted rather than admired, in
`test_activation_number_counts_actuator_time_constants`.

## 3. The bandwidth result, closed form

Actuator bandwidth `omega_c/2pi` over the published 10-80 1/s critical band and
the measured 0.5-1.6 strain clock:

| `gamma_dot` (1/s) | `gamma_0 = 1.6` (slow clock) | `gamma_0 = 0.5` (fast clock) |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.99 Hz | 3.18 Hz |
| 20 | 1.99 Hz | 6.37 Hz |
| 40 | 3.98 Hz | 12.73 Hz |
| 80 | 7.96 Hz | 25.46 Hz |

Against what a gait event demands:

- **Weight acceptance, 100 ms → 10 Hz** (sourced: loading response is 2-12% of a
  ~1.0 s cycle). The worst corner of the design space is **10.1x too slow**. The
  best corner clears it with 2.55x margin.
- **Stumble, 30 ms → 33 Hz** (**not sourced**, a stand-in). The best corner of the
  entire published design space is **1.31x short**. No point in it tracks a
  stumble.

**Read the second bullet carefully before using it.** The 1.31x shortfall is
marginal and it rests on an unsourced 30 ms. At 40 ms the demand falls to 25 Hz
and the best corner just clears. The honest statement is that stumble tracking is
borderline-to-impossible across the published space and that pinning the stumble
duration would settle it. It is the cheapest remaining literature question in this
project.

## 4. What this means for the product

The design document proposed a semi-active device as the more interesting option
and the passive STF device as the safer one. This inverts that.

**Over most of the published design band a semi-active controller is slower than
the event it is controlling.** Where `A < 1` the field cannot complete a single
response before the event is over, so the device behaves as whatever passive
element its initial state happened to correspond to. Paying for sensing,
electronics, power and an Össur-adjacent patent position buys authority the
physics does not deliver there.

Three consequences, in decreasing confidence:

1. **The passive STF route is not the compromise, it is the honest reading of the
   bandwidth.** Where the actuator cannot act in time, a well-chosen passive
   formulation is not a lesser device, it is the same device without the
   electronics.
2. **Semi-active earns its cost only at high shear rate and short strain clock**,
   the top-right corner of §3. That corner is a material-selection target, not a
   control-design target: it is reached by choosing `gamma_0`, not by writing a
   better controller.
3. **Any semi-active design must be evaluated at `A`, not at frequency alone.**
   A controller specified in Hz against a plant whose bandwidth is set by shear
   rate will be specified in the wrong units.

## 5. Limitations

1. **`sigma*` as the control input is a modelling choice.** An MR field also adds
   a magnetic yield stress directly, which this does not represent. The relative
   degree argument survives that (a yield stress still acts through the
   microstructure), the quantitative bandwidth may not.
2. **Linearisation about a steady operating point.** The step-response check is
   exact only at vanishing packing, where the stress feedback is off. With
   feedback on, the response is not first order and `tau_c` is an estimate.
3. **The demanded force is defined by the steady curve at full field.** That is
   the specification a quasi-steady designer writes, which is the point, but it
   is not an optimal demand.
4. **Prescribed velocity, no human in the loop.** The device changes gait, which
   changes shear rate, which changes bandwidth. That coupling is layer 1's job and
   is absent here.
5. Everything inherits `SCALING_2026-08-31.md` §7, in particular that Wyart-Cates
   cannot describe flow reversal, and gait reverses every cycle.
