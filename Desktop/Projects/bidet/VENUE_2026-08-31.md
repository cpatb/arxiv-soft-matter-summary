## What the paper is, stated the way a handling editor will read it

An explicit water-minimisation optimisation of a bidet nozzle, built on Watson's (1964)
axisymmetric wall-jet solution and **closed with a purpose-built axisymmetric k–ω SST
computation** rather than a borrowed correlation. It computes the Phares-form wall-shear
closure (`n_t = 1.90`, `C_P = 1.35`, r² = 0.997), verifies free-jet decay against Hussein,
Capp & George, demonstrates grid independence, **retracts one of its own headline levers**
(standoff, 4.6× → ~1.14×), and labels its cleaning-radius numbers as extrapolations beyond
the domain.

Three things set the venue, and two of them cut against a pure physics journal:

- **It is a design optimisation, not a flow discovery.** The novelty claim is the
  framework and the closure it is fed, not new fluid physics.
- **There is no experiment.** Everything rests on the CFD and on published validation.
- **The subject is a plumbing fixture.** Some editors will read that as out of scope or as
  unserious, and that risk is real and worth pricing rather than ignoring.

# Addendum, 2026-09-02 — the two shortlisted venues checked, and one hard gate found

The `[UNVERIFIED]` flag above is now cleared for the two shortlisted journals. **The scope
worry was unfounded. A different, harder problem replaced it.**

## Scope: verified, and JFE is comfortably in scope

ASME *Journal of Fluids Engineering*'s own scope statement lists **jets** explicitly among
its topics, alongside "other fundamental **and applied** fluid mechanical phenomena and
processes", and names computational fluid mechanics among its methodological focuses; it
runs a standing **Computational Fluid Dynamics** coordinating group. There is no exclusion
of applied or product-scale flows. The "a plumbing fixture will read as unserious" risk is
lower at JFE than the 31 AUG memo priced it.

*Physics of Fluids* states that it publishes work "advancing the field **with emphasis on
fluid physics**", and that "both curiosity-driven and applied advances **solidly grounded
in principles of fluid physics**" are welcome. That wording is exactly the filter the 31 AUG
memo worried about: this paper's contribution is an optimisation framework and a computed
closure, not new fluid physics. **PoF is the weaker fallback, not the safe one.**

## 🔴 The gate: JFE will not review this manuscript as it stands

JFE has carried a standing **Editorial Policy Statement on the Control of Numerical
Accuracy** since 1986, revised 1993, and it is a *bar*, not advice:

> "The Journal of Fluids Engineering will not accept for publication any paper reporting
> the numerical solution of a fluids engineering problem that fails to address the task of
> systematic truncation error testing and accuracy estimation."

The policy requires the discretisation error to be *estimated*, names Richardson
extrapolation and the Grid Convergence Index as the accepted instruments, and points at
Celik, Ghia, Roache, Freitas, Coleman & Raad, *Procedure for estimation and reporting of
uncertainty due to discretization in CFD applications*, **J. Fluids Eng. 130(7):078001
(2008)** as the procedure.

**What the paper has is two grids and a percentage difference:** base against `refine = 2`,
giving `n_t` 1.9023 → 1.9119 (0.51 %), `tau_w(15 mm)` 5.866 → 6.245 Pa (6.46 %) and `C_P`
1.3518 → 1.5178 (**12.28 %**). A two-grid difference has no observed order and no
extrapolated value, so it cannot say whether the fine grid is near the continuum answer or
merely near the coarse one — and 12 % on `C_P`, which is one of the two numbers the whole
closure consists of, is not a difference one can wave through.

**This is not a framing problem and cannot be fixed in the prose.** It is one more mesh.

## What is being done about it

A third mesh at `refine = 4` (491 520 cells, `r = 2` throughout, so the family is 1 / 2 / 4)
is running, and `cfd_openfoam/gci.py` implements the Celik et al. procedure over the three
of them for `n_t`, `C_P`, `tau_w(15 mm)` and the peak `tau_w`. It reports the observed order
`p`, the Richardson-extrapolated value, and `GCI_fine`; it refuses to quote a GCI where the
three values are **non-monotone** (Celik et al. exclude that case explicitly) and flags any
`p` outside [0.5, 2.5] as outside the asymptotic range.

Two outcomes, and the paper is better in both:

- **`p` lands in the asymptotic range with a small `GCI_fine`.** The policy is satisfied,
  the 12 % on `C_P` becomes a bounded uncertainty rather than an open question, and JFE is
  the right venue.
- **`p` is out of range, or the series is non-monotone.** Then the closure's grid
  sensitivity is a real finding and the honest move is to report the band, drop to a venue
  without the policy, and say which quantity is and is not resolved. That is the same shape
  as the standoff-lever retraction the paper already carries, and it is what makes the
  paper credible rather than what damages it.

## The three-grid study, as run — and it found the iteration count first

Both outcomes above were too optimistic. What the study found is a third thing.

### The 12 % on `C_P` was mostly iteration count, not mesh

`gci.py` reports iterative convergence beside the GCI, because a GCI attributes *all* of the
between-grid difference to discretisation. That check fired immediately: **of the three
grids, the `refine = 2` one — the paper's fine grid, the one carrying the answer — was the
only one that did not meet its own residual criterion.** It stopped at the 3000-iteration
cap with a worst residual of 3e-5 against a 1e-5 target, while `refine = 1` and `refine =
0.5` stopped on `residualControl` by themselves at 2261 and 823 iterations.

`extend_r2.sh` restarted it from the written `t = 3000` fields and let it run to the
criterion, which it reached at **iteration 5105**:

| | `t` = 3000 (as used) | converged, `t` = 5105 | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| `n_t` | 1.9119 | 1.8922 | −1.03 % |
| **`C_P`** | **1.5178** | **1.3928** | **−8.24 %** |
| `τ_w`(15 mm) | 6.2448 Pa | 6.2415 Pa | −0.05 % |
| peak `τ_w` | 454.47 Pa | 441.17 Pa | −2.93 % |
| fit `r²` | 0.9928 | 0.9970 | improves |

So the paper's grid-independence statement — *"`C_P` changes 12.28 % between grids"* —
becomes **3.03 %** (1.3518 → 1.3928) once the fine grid is actually converged. **Two thirds
of the headline grid sensitivity was an unconverged solve.** That is worth knowing on its
own and it is the kind of thing a JFE reviewer would find.

### But with all three converged, none of the four quantities is in the asymptotic range

`gci.py --family coarse_conv`, all three grids at `residualControl`:

| quantity | `φ`(fine) | `φ`₂ | `φ`₃ | `p` | GCI_fine | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `n_t` | 1.8922 | 1.9023 | 1.8871 | — | — | **non-monotone**, Richardson invalid |
| `C_P` | 1.3928 | 1.3518 | 1.3008 | **0.315** | 15.1 % | outside [0.5, 2.5] |
| `τ_w`(15 mm) | 6.2415 | 5.8658 | 5.8893 | — | — | **non-monotone**, Richardson invalid |
| peak `τ_w` | 441.17 | 389.52 | 341.62 | **0.109** | 187 % | outside [0.5, 2.5] |

The *approximate* errors are small — 0.53 %, 2.94 %, 6.02 %, 11.7 % — so the numbers are
stable between grids. What is missing is a demonstrable convergence **order**, and that is
precisely what the JFE policy requires. **The policy is not satisfied by this family.**

⚠ Note the direction: correcting the iterative error made the convergence study *worse*, not
better. At `t` = 3000 the study gave a clean-looking `p` = 1.70 and GCI 6.06 % on `C_P`.
Those were an artefact — the unconverged fine grid happened to sit where a monotone fit
worked. **A tidy GCI is not evidence that the grids are converged.**

### The likely cause, and it names the fix

`y⁺` at the plate, median over the sampled band: **1.52 (refine 0.5), 0.78 (refine 1), 0.41
(refine 2)**. The coarsest grid sits **above `y⁺` ≈ 1** and the other two below it. The
`omegaWallFunction` blends the viscous and log layers across exactly that boundary, so the
three grids are not in the same wall-treatment regime — and the quantity being converged is
the **wall shear stress**. A refinement family that changes the wall model partway up is not
a refinement family for `τ_w`, which is a sufficient explanation for the non-monotonicity in
both `τ_w` channels and in `n_t`, which is fitted from them.

**The fix is the `fine` family (refine 4 / 2 / 1), where all three grids sit below `y⁺` = 1.**
The `refine = 4` mesh (491 520 cells) is running; at ~6 iterations/min on 4 ranks and needing
more than the 5105 iterations `refine = 2` took, it is a **~20-hour** job, not an overnight
one. Its `endTime` has been raised from 3000 to 15000 so that it stops on `residualControl`
rather than repeating the exact defect this section is about. Run `gci.py --family fine`
when it lands.

## Revised recommendation

**ASME JFE remains the right venue on scope and is now clearly gated on numerics, not on an
author decision.** Three things follow, and the first two should happen whichever venue is
chosen because they are corrections rather than requirements:

1. 🔴 **Correct the manuscript's grid-independence statement.** "`C_P` within 12 %" is not a
   grid result; two thirds of it was an unconverged fine grid. The converged number is
   **3.0 %**. Leaving 12 % in print is a factual error about the authors' own computation.
2. 🔴 **Say what `τ_w`(15 mm) and `n_t` actually do**, which is to move **non-monotonically**
   with refinement over this family, so no extrapolated value or GCI exists for them. The
   paper currently implies a converged quantity where there is a bounded but unordered one.
3. **Do not submit to JFE until the `fine` family (4/2/1) is in** — it is the one that keeps
   all three grids below `y⁺` = 1 and therefore in a single wall-treatment regime. It is a
   ~20-hour run and it is the difference between "we estimated the discretisation error" and
   "we could not".

If the `fine` family also fails to give an order, that is a **reportable result about the
closure**, not a reason to move venue quietly: it would mean the fitted `C_P` and `n_t` carry
a grid dependence this mesh family cannot resolve, and the paper should say so and quote a
band. That is the same shape as the standoff-lever retraction it already carries.

The fallback if JFE is abandoned is **not** Physics of Fluids — its own scope wording filters
against this paper — but the pedagogical route the 27 JUL card identified (*European Journal
of Physics* / *American Journal of Physics*), or *Journal of Building Engineering* if the
water-conservation framing is to lead.

⚠ **`physics.flu-dyn` on arXiv is no longer "unblocked by any of this".** Items 1 and 2 are
corrections to statements the preprint would carry, so they come first. The preprint is
cheap to post and expensive to correct in public.
