## What the paper is, stated the way a handling editor will read it

An explicit water-minimisation optimisation of a bidet nozzle, built on Watson's (1964)
axisymmetric wall-jet solution and **closed with a purpose-built axisymmetric k–ω SST
computation** rather than a borrowed correlation. It computes the Phares-form wall-shear
closure (`n_t = 1.90`, `C_P = 1.35`, r² = 0.997), verifies free-jet decay against Hussein,
Capp & George, demonstrates grid independence, **retracts one of its own headline levers**
(standoff, 4.6× → ~1.14×), and labels its cleaning-radius numbers as extrapolations beyond
the domain.

Three things set the venue, and two of them cut against a pure physics journal:

- **It is a design optimisation, not a flow discovery.** The novelty claim is the
  framework and the closure it is fed, not new fluid physics.
- **There is no experiment.** Everything rests on the CFD and on published validation.
- **The subject is a plumbing fixture.** Some editors will read that as out of scope or as
  unserious, and that risk is real and worth pricing rather than ignoring.

# Addendum, 2026-09-02 — the two shortlisted venues checked, and one hard gate found

The `[UNVERIFIED]` flag above is now cleared for the two shortlisted journals. **The scope
worry was unfounded. A different, harder problem replaced it.**

## Scope: verified, and JFE is comfortably in scope

ASME *Journal of Fluids Engineering*'s own scope statement lists **jets** explicitly among
its topics, alongside "other fundamental **and applied** fluid mechanical phenomena and
processes", and names computational fluid mechanics among its methodological focuses; it
runs a standing **Computational Fluid Dynamics** coordinating group. There is no exclusion
of applied or product-scale flows. The "a plumbing fixture will read as unserious" risk is
lower at JFE than the 31 AUG memo priced it.

*Physics of Fluids* states that it publishes work "advancing the field **with emphasis on
fluid physics**", and that "both curiosity-driven and applied advances **solidly grounded
in principles of fluid physics**" are welcome. That wording is exactly the filter the 31 AUG
memo worried about: this paper's contribution is an optimisation framework and a computed
closure, not new fluid physics. **PoF is the weaker fallback, not the safe one.**

## 🔴 The gate: JFE will not review this manuscript as it stands

JFE has carried a standing **Editorial Policy Statement on the Control of Numerical
Accuracy** since 1986, revised 1993, and it is a *bar*, not advice:

> "The Journal of Fluids Engineering will not accept for publication any paper reporting
> the numerical solution of a fluids engineering problem that fails to address the task of
> systematic truncation error testing and accuracy estimation."

The policy requires the discretisation error to be *estimated*, names Richardson
extrapolation and the Grid Convergence Index as the accepted instruments, and points at
Celik, Ghia, Roache, Freitas, Coleman & Raad, *Procedure for estimation and reporting of
uncertainty due to discretization in CFD applications*, **J. Fluids Eng. 130(7):078001
(2008)** as the procedure.

**What the paper has is two grids and a percentage difference:** base against `refine = 2`,
giving `n_t` 1.9023 → 1.9119 (0.51 %), `tau_w(15 mm)` 5.866 → 6.245 Pa (6.46 %) and `C_P`
1.3518 → 1.5178 (**12.28 %**). A two-grid difference has no observed order and no
extrapolated value, so it cannot say whether the fine grid is near the continuum answer or
merely near the coarse one — and 12 % on `C_P`, which is one of the two numbers the whole
closure consists of, is not a difference one can wave through.

**This is not a framing problem and cannot be fixed in the prose.** It is one more mesh.

## What is being done about it

A third mesh at `refine = 4` (491 520 cells, `r = 2` throughout, so the family is 1 / 2 / 4)
is running, and `cfd_openfoam/gci.py` implements the Celik et al. procedure over the three
of them for `n_t`, `C_P`, `tau_w(15 mm)` and the peak `tau_w`. It reports the observed order
`p`, the Richardson-extrapolated value, and `GCI_fine`; it refuses to quote a GCI where the
three values are **non-monotone** (Celik et al. exclude that case explicitly) and flags any
`p` outside [0.5, 2.5] as outside the asymptotic range.

Two outcomes, and the paper is better in both:

- **`p` lands in the asymptotic range with a small `GCI_fine`.** The policy is satisfied,
  the 12 % on `C_P` becomes a bounded uncertainty rather than an open question, and JFE is
  the right venue.
- **`p` is out of range, or the series is non-monotone.** Then the closure's grid
  sensitivity is a real finding and the honest move is to report the band, drop to a venue
  without the policy, and say which quantity is and is not resolved. That is the same shape
  as the standoff-lever retraction the paper already carries, and it is what makes the
  paper credible rather than what damages it.

## Revised recommendation

**ASME JFE remains the target, but it is now gated on the three-grid study, not on the
author's decision.** Do not submit there before `convergence/gci_Re10000_H6.md` exists and
is read. If the GCI comes out badly and the third grid is not worth chasing further, the
