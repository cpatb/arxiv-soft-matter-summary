## What the paper is, stated the way a handling editor will read it

An explicit water-minimisation optimisation of a bidet nozzle, built on Watson's (1964)
axisymmetric wall-jet solution and **closed with a purpose-built axisymmetric k–ω SST
computation** rather than a borrowed correlation. It computes the Phares-form wall-shear
closure (`n_t = 1.90`, `C_P = 1.35`, r² = 0.997), verifies free-jet decay against Hussein,
Capp & George, demonstrates grid independence, **retracts one of its own headline levers**
(standoff, 4.6× → ~1.14×), and labels its cleaning-radius numbers as extrapolations beyond
the domain.

Three things set the venue, and two of them cut against a pure physics journal:

- **It is a design optimisation, not a flow discovery.** The novelty claim is the
  framework and the closure it is fed, not new fluid physics.
- **There is no experiment.** Everything rests on the CFD and on published validation.
- **The subject is a plumbing fixture.** Some editors will read that as out of scope or as
  unserious, and that risk is real and worth pricing rather than ignoring.

## The shortlist

Fluids Engineering** | Design-and-optimisation CFD is its centre of gravity, and it is the venue whose review will *want* the grid-independence and convergence tables this paper already has. | **Physics of Fluids** | Impinging jets and wall-jet closures are routine there; broad readership; faster. | The optimisation framing is the paper's contribution and PoF will read it as engineering; the fixture framing is a bigger risk here. |
| **Int. J. Heat and Fluid Flow** | Impinging jets are a staple; a computed closure is a normal contribution. | Wants heat transfer more often than not, and this paper has none. |
| **J. Building Engineering** / **Building and Environment** | The water-conservation framing is squarely in scope and the fixture is an asset rather than an embarrassment. | The fluid mechanics will get a shallower review, which is a waste of the paper's strongest part. |

**Recommendation: ASME Journal of Fluids Engineering**, with Physics of Fluids as the
fallback if turnaround matters more than fit. JFE is the venue where "we computed the
closure instead of borrowing it, and we retracted a lever when the computation disagreed"
is the paper's selling point rather than a curiosity.

**Post to arXiv `physics.flu-dyn` first**, whichever is chosen. Nothing in the paper is
scoop-sensitive and the preprint is free.
