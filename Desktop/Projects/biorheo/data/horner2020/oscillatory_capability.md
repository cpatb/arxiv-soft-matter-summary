## What actually decides it, and what does not

**Item 7's own question is not the binding one.** It asked whether the LAOS sheets have enough samples per cycle; they have 20 to 1571, over 3.0 to 63.6 cycles, against a 4-6 sample rise in the step sheet. Sampling is not the constraint and the item cannot be closed on that ground. Three other things close it.

**C4: the workbook stores no phase.** The Frequency Sweep and Amplitude Sweep sheets carry a **storage modulus only** -- there is no loss modulus, no phase angle, and no tan(delta) anywhere in the file. A lag and a second timescale are separated chiefly by PHASE: a dead time theta contributes a phase -w*theta that grows without bound and leaves the modulus alone, while a second Debye process contributes a bounded phase excursion with a matching dispersion in the modulus. With only |G'| the two candidates are being compared on the channel where they agree most.

**C3: linearity is never demonstrated.** The Read Me says the frequency sweep ran at "a strain amplitude of 10" and the Amplitude Sweep's own axis starts at 9.953 % and runs to 2986 %. The unit of that 10 is ambiguous in the source -- the Amplitude Sweep is labelled in %, while the Read Me says the LAOS sheet labels are dimensionless -- so both readings are carried here and **neither is guessed**:

- **10 % (Amplitude Sweep's own unit)**: an LVE plateau containing that strain would have to be shown from below, i.e. the amplitude sweep would have to reach 5 % or lower. It stops at 9.953 %. **FAILED**.
- **10 dimensionless = 1000 % (the LAOS label unit)**: an LVE plateau containing that strain would have to be shown from below, i.e. the amplitude sweep would have to reach 500 % or lower. It stops at 9.953 %. PASS.

**Neither reading gives a linear measurement, and that is measured, not argued.** Under the first, the amplitude sweep's very first point IS the frequency sweep's operating point, so nothing below it was measured -- and G' is still RISING there, in both ramps, on 9 of 15 donors, so that point is on a flank rather than on a plateau. Under the second, the frequency sweep sits at ~1000 % strain, where G' has fallen to a median 0.207 of its own maximum (worst case 0.540) -- roughly two decades in STRAIN past where G' peaks, with the modulus down by a factor of about five, so it is nonlinear by the source's own data. **The conclusion does not depend on which reading is right**, which is why the ambiguity is reported rather than resolved. Only the C3 pass/fail arithmetic differs between them, and the rule fixed in advance requires every reading to pass.

**C1: the population is not item 2's population.** The step-rise result is on all 20 donors. The Frequency Sweep has 13/20, the LAOS sheets have 12/20 on at least one sheet and only 10/20 on every sheet. Donors **ABCDFQRT** have no LAOS data at all. An oscillatory answer would speak for a different set of people than the finding it is meant to explain.

## What a discriminating measurement would need

Not in this workbook, and worth writing down rather than working around:

1. A **phase or loss channel** on the same donors -- G''(w) or tan(delta), or the raw stress waveform aligned to the strain so one can be computed.
2. An **amplitude sweep that reaches into the linear region**, decades below the strain the frequency sweep uses, so that the linear signatures apply at all.
3. Either that, or a **step experiment sampled fast enough on the rise**. Item 2's rise carries 4-6 samples; a lag of order 0.1-1 s against a tau of 1-2.7 s needs roughly an order of magnitude more, which is a request to the instrument, not to the analysis.

**Both sweep sheets are two concatenated ramps over the same axis**, not one sweep: the Amplitude Sweep is [26, 26] points from 9.953 % and the Frequency Sweep is [20, 20] from 0.5 rad/s. That matters mechanically -- a maximum taken over the whole column lands in whichever ramp happens to be larger and mixes two runs, so every number above is computed per ramp -- and it raises a question the workbook does not answer. At the lowest strain the two ramps of the amplitude sweep differ by a median 83% (worst 432%). **That is far too large to call them repeats**; they are more likely two conditions (the Read Me says the amplitude sweep shown is 'representative' and was run at 2 Hz, without saying what the second ramp is). Either way it does not move the C3 verdict, whose decisive fact needs no ramp reasoning at all: nothing below 9.953 % strain was measured, on either ramp.
