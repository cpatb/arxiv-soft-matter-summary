# Does the identifiability verdict predict multi-start seed scatter?

Generated by `scripts/multistart_seed_scatter.py`; every number reproduces by running it. Each of 20 donor curves is fitted five times with seeds 0-4, changing nothing but the optimiser's random starts.

**Provenance.** The `carreau_yasuda` column and the four-family maxima were an EXPLORATORY pass taken before any criterion was written -- they suggested the hypothesis and cannot also test it. The per-donor `casson`, `cross` and `herschel_bulkley` columns are the held-out half. Both criteria are graded on all four families and the split is stated so the exploratory half can be discounted.

**G9.2 FAILED and its bar was not moved.** The violation is the Herschel-Bulkley fit on donor G: `tau_y` moves 4.149 under nothing but a change of seed, while its verdict is `bounded_only` -- which is neither of the two verdicts G9.2 asked about, and, more to the point, is not one the export refuses. G9.3 is the stricter replacement: it asks about every record the export would ACCEPT, at a bar two orders lower. It FAILS on 1 record(s): [('herschel_bulkley', 'G', 4.1485973932768605)]. None of them is in a shipped family.

## What the two spreads separate

A fit can go wrong under a change of seed in two different ways, and the pair of columns tells them apart. If the OBJECTIVE moves, some starts are landing on worse optima and the fit is under-converged. If the objective does NOT move while the PARAMETERS do, the starts are landing on equally good optima at different places -- a flat direction, which is what `degenerate` and `not_identifiable` are for.

Across all 80 (model, donor) pairs the objective moves by at most **6.50e-10** relative, i.e. not at all. Every parameter spread below is therefore the second kind: flatness, not a failed search. That is the whole result -- the multi-start count is not measuring convergence here, it is sampling a flat direction, and the identifiability layer already names which records have one.

**The converse is NOT claimed.** Nothing here says every flagged record must move: a parameter can be unidentifiable along a direction these particular starts never explore, and the profile likelihood would still be right to flag it. Only the two one-sided criteria above are tested.

**Consequence for the registry.** A record whose parameters move under a change of seed ships whatever `seed=0` returned. That is reproducible -- the seed is fixed and recorded -- but it is not a measurement, and it is exactly the set the export already refuses without `allow_unidentified=True`.
