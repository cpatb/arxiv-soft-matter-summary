## Reading

The steady curve alone leaves alpha and t_r degenerate (tests/test_structural.py); the rise samples carry tau on its own, so the pair is separated by construction whenever tau's profile closes inside the box. What the gate tests is whether the separated values are physically sensible (t95 at the observed peak time) and whether a single exponential is the shape of the rise at the sample noise. Caveats: the steady parameters' uncertainty is not propagated; the first sample at 1 s may include the instrument's own rate transient; the decay after the peak is excluded on the thesis's syneresis reading, which is not tested here.

Result of the first run (2026-09-05): G1 passed on every donor and G2 failed. The timescale is right -- the model's approach to steady state lands where the peak is -- but a single exponential is not the shape of the rise at the sample noise: the fitted rise misses by 1.1-11.5 % of its own amplitude (median 3.4 %) where the tail noise is 0.04-1 mPa, and the sign pattern is systematic. Neither post-hoc variant changes that, and the data-anchored one removes the steady fit's misfit at 1 1/s from the residual and still fails, so the failure is in the kinetics, not inherited from the steady curve. The rise is less concave than an exponential between the same endpoints (nearer to linear in t), which first-order kinetics with one structure variable cannot produce. What would: a lag or a second timescale (two-structure or strain-dependent kinetics), or an instrument transient at 1 s that the drop-first variant does not fully remove. Untested here.
