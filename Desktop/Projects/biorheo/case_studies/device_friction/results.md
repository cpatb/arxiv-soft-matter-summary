# The term that defaults to zero

Pre-registration: [`prereg.md`](prereg.md), written before any number here. Generated by `run.py`; every number below is printed by that script.

`injection_force` takes `friction: float = 0.0` and no case study in this repo supplies it, so every force this project has published is the hydrodynamic term alone. That is labelled correctly on every page. What had never been checked is which CONCLUSIONS drawn from those forces depend on the friction being zero.

## What survives by construction, and what does not

With `F = h + f` and `f` a constant, ordering, force DIFFERENCES and deterministic-interval separability are all invariant -- checked as arithmetic in `results.json`, and not counted as gates, because a check that cannot fail decides nothing. Two things are not invariant, and they are the whole study: a RATIO of two forces moves toward 1 as `f` grows, and separability collapses under a friction that VARIES from syringe to syringe, which a constant cannot touch.

## Q1 -- a constant friction, and the 7.1x

The mAb page reports that the median formulation needs **7.1x** the plunger force of the median blood donor (1.98 N against 0.28 N). That ratio is a property of the friction being zero:

| constant friction `f` (N) | median force ratio |
|---|---|
| 0 | 7.05x |
| 0.25 | 4.20x |
| 0.5 | 3.17x |
| 1 | 2.33x |
| 2 | 1.74x |
| 3 | 1.52x |
| 5 | 1.32x |

It first falls below 3.0 at `f` = **0.575 N**, below 2.0 at **1.44 N** and below 1.5 at **3.19 N**. The force DIFFERENCE, 1.7 N, is untouched at every `f` -- so the statement that survives any friction is *this formulation needs about 1.7 N more than blood*, not *it needs 7.1 times as much*.

**No friction magnitude is asserted here.** The table is the sensitivity; where a particular device sits on it is answered in the section below, from a source that resolved to a DOI in a lookup, and it is attributed there rather than asserted.

## Q2 -- the number a formulation screen actually needs

A device friction that varies syringe to syringe with SD `s` is the term that can destroy separability, and no page mentions it. Convention (named, not derived): device friction SD s added in quadrature to the mAb measurement SD; for blood, each hull side widened by 2s. Named, not derived.

| `s` (N) | mAb pairs separable | blood pairs separable |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 92.3 % (1525/1653) | 18.9 % (36/190) |
| 0.005 | 91.7 % | 12.1 % |
| 0.01 | 90.9 % | 10.0 % |
| 0.05 | 77.0 % | 0.0 % |
| 0.1 | 61.1 % | 0.0 % |
| 0.5 | 1.4 % | 0.0 % |

**The deliverable is the threshold, not a verdict.** The mAb screen falls below 50 % separable pairs at `s*` = **0.1397 N** and below 10 % at **0.3254 N**. A threshold needs no external number to be correct; a literature or datasheet friction only says where on this curve a given syringe sits.

## P4 -- the same friction damages the two cohorts very differently

At `s` = 0.05 N the mAb cohort's median interval half-width grows **4.56x** (22.5 -> 102 mN) and the blood cohort's **3.33x** (42.9 -> 143 mN). The mechanism is that the mAb interval is a pure measurement repeatability -- a median 0.65 % of the force -- while the blood interval already carries a parameter uncertainty of a median 29.1 %. **The tighter the fluid measurement, the more of the answer the device owns.**

**P4 is REFUTED, and the defect is in the pre-registration rather than in either number.** The prediction was `mab_growth > 3` AND `blood_growth < 1.3`; the first holds and the second does not (3.33x). It also compared two arms under two different OPERATIONS -- quadrature on the mAb side, a linear `2s` added to each hull side for blood. The verdict and the numbers above stand as graded. Repeating it with the same operation on both arms (half-width -> hypot(half-width, 2s) on BOTH arms) gives blood 2.54x against the mAb 4.56x, so the mAb cohort is the more exposed by 1.80x -- right in DIRECTION and wrong in SIZE, since the bar implied a separation nearer 3.5x. POST-HOC. Not a pre-registered gate. Reported because the pre-registered comparison was not like-for-like.

## Where a real device sits on that curve

The threshold above needs no external number. This section only places a device on the curve, and it uses one that **resolved to a DOI in a lookup this session** (Funke et al., J. Pharm. Sci. (2016), doi:10.1002/jps.23945), never one from memory. It is a **1 mL glass prefilled syringe with a staked needle; NOT the syringe either case study specifies, whose 2.3 mm barrel radius is a CHOICE**.

- **Glide (sustaining) force 1.3-1.5 N** — 1.3-1.5 N glide, from a trade article with NO DOI in the same survey; treated as UNVERIFIED and reported only as a bracket. This is the comparable term, because the case studies compute a STEADY flow, so glide is the comparable term; breakloose is the start transient the module docstring already excludes. At it the 7.05x ratio is **2.07-1.95x**.
- **Breakloose 3.0-9.0 N** — ~9 N siliconised / ~3 N silicone-free (Funke 2016, RESOLVED). At it the ratio is **1.18-1.52x**.

**The Q2 side has no comparator at all, and that is a result.** the survey resolved friction MEANS and no friction SD or syringe-to-syringe repeatability for any device. s* is therefore a threshold with nothing published to compare it against -- the quantity that decides whether a formulation screen is decidable is unpublished.

## What this does not say

- Nothing here measures a syringe. No friction magnitude is quoted.
- The shipped forces are not wrong; they are the hydrodynamic term, correctly computed and correctly labelled.
- Friction is treated as fluid-independent. That assumption is what makes it additive and therefore what makes the ordering claims survive; if it failed, even the ordering would be at risk and this study could not tell.
- The two cohorts' intervals are different objects (a parameter hull and a measurement SD) and are never compared with each other here.
