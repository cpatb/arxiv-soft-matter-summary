# Twenty blood donors through a 27G needle at 1 mL/min

Generated by `run.py`. Casson closed form, derived and anchored against the package's independent quadrature in `derivations/casson_tube_flow.md`. Needle: nominal 27G regular wall, inner diameter 0.21 mm, length 12.7 mm. Syringe barrel radius 2.3 mm. The geometry is a choice, not a measurement (see the script docstring); the ISO tolerance band is swept below.

## The pre-registered question

**20 of 20 records are flagged `extrapolated`.** The prediction, written before the run, was 20 of 20, on the grounds that the apparent wall rate is about 26x the top of every record's 700 1/s envelope.

The count is the boring part. The DISTANCE is the result: the true wall shear rate is 1.42 decades past the top of the fitted envelope, so the Casson parameters are being asked to predict a regime 26x beyond anything the rheometer saw. Casson has no high-rate structure to get wrong -- it tends to a constant `eta_c` -- but that is an assumption of the FORM, and the form is already known to misfit these donors (3 runs of residual sign against 9 expected). Extrapolating a misfit model 1.42 decades is the honest description of what a needle calculation on this data is.

That distance is the SAME for every donor to three figures (the wall rate spans only 1.0026x across the 20), and that is not a coincidence: for a Newtonian fluid the wall shear rate is `4Q/(pi R^3)`, independent of viscosity, and these donors are effectively Newtonian in this geometry (next section). **How far a device extrapolates its own rheology is therefore a property of the DEVICE, not of the patient** -- it is fixed by flow rate and lumen before any fluid is chosen, and it can be computed at design time.

That last sentence needs its scope stated, because it is demonstrated only within the Casson family, which structurally CANNOT express extra shear-thinning above yield -- it saturates to a constant `eta_c`. For a genuinely shear-thinning family the true wall rate is larger than the Newtonian one by an O(1) factor that depends on the fluid: a power law gives `(3n+1)/(4n)`, which is 1.25x at n = 0.5 and 2x at n = 0.2. So the DECADE is a device property; the third significant figure is a property of the Casson form. A second mechanism helps here too and should be named: the envelope [0.1, 700] 1/s is identical for all 20 donors, being the rheometer protocol rather than a per-donor fit, so both the numerator and the denominator of the distance are device or protocol quantities.

## The yield stress does not matter here

`x = tau_y / tau_w` runs 1.63e-05 to 0.000168 across the 20 donors, so the Casson bracket `1 - (16/7)sqrt(x) + (4/3)x - x^4/21` differs from 1 by 3 % at worst. Setting `tau_y` to zero and refitting the pressure changes the plunger force by 0.92 % to 2.9 %. In this geometry blood is a Newtonian fluid of viscosity `eta_c`, and the donor-to-donor force spread ranks with `eta_c` at Spearman 0.995 and with `tau_y` at 0.170. **The parameter the published Casson tables argue about is the one this device cannot see.** That is not a criticism of the tables -- tau_y is what matters in the microcirculation, at the rates the rheometer measured -- it is a statement about which measurement a needle calculation needs.

The pre-registered estimate for that bracket deviation was 0.5 %, and it was wrong by 6x: the measured worst case is 3 %. The estimate had been carried over from the derivation's own table, which uses a 20 mm needle and different pressures. The conclusion holds at the larger number; the estimate did not.

## The gauge is not a radius

Force scales as `R^-4` at fixed flow rate -- measured here, not assumed: a log-log fit over +/-10 % in inner diameter gives d(log F)/d(log R) = -3.9723, against -4 exactly for Poiseuille -- the small difference is the Casson bracket, which is not quite 1. (This file's own docstring previously said `Q ~ R^3 at fixed dp`, which is wrong twice: Poiseuille gives `R^4` at fixed `dp`, and this study fixes `Q`, so the operative relation is `R^-4`. Corrected 2026-09-05 after a referee pass; the computed numbers never used the wrong scaling, only its stated justification did.)

The consequence needs no standard and no tolerance table. The whole donor-to-donor force spread across these 20 people is 2.28x, and because `F ~ R^-4` an inner-diameter error of only **23.0 %** reproduces it exactly. Excluding the three donors the source study itself flags as atypical (F, K, N) the spread falls to 1.49x, which a 10.5 % diameter error reproduces. **A needle whose bore is uncertain at the ten-percent level carries more uncertainty in plunger force than the biological variation between twenty people.** That statement is exact arithmetic on the fitted records; nothing in it depends on a manufacturing standard.

### What was retracted here (2026-09-05)

An earlier version of this file said: *ISO 9626:2016 allows an inner diameter of 0.184-0.241 mm for regular-wall 27G*, computed a 2.92x force ratio across it, and concluded that the manufacturing tolerance outweighs the donor spread. **That is withdrawn.** A referee pass found that 0.184 mm is a regular-wall figure while 0.241 mm is a THIN-WALL one, so the pair is not one product's tolerance, and the standard itself is not available offline to check. Under the narrower regular-wall-only reading (0.184-0.21 mm) the ratio is 1.69x, which is SMALLER than the 2.28x donor spread -- the conclusion reverses. Both readings are printed below and both are tagged [UNVERIFIED]; the section's claim has been rebuilt on the `R^-4` scaling above, which does not need the standard.

There was a second problem with the original comparison even if the band had been right: an ISO tolerance is a manufacturing bound over a whole population, while 2.28x is the max/min of a sample of 20, a statistic whose expected value grows with sample size. The two are not commensurate and were presented as if they were.

## What is not in these numbers

The module docstring lists the omissions: entrance and exit losses, the barrel's own pressure drop, the kinetic term, the start transient, wall slip. One of them is NOT negligible relative to the effect this study calls negligible, and saying `Re` is small is the wrong comparison -- it compares the term with `dp`, not with the term being ranked. The entrance (Hagenbach) loss is about `1.08 rho U^2 / 2` = 132.5 Pa at the mean velocity U = 0.4812 m/s that Q and R fix for every donor, which is 0.43-0.99 % of the pressure drop -- the same size as the 0.92-2.9 % yield-stress contribution called negligible above. On no donor does the entrance term exceed that donor's own yield contribution, though they are the same order. It does not change the conclusion -- both are small, and the yield stress is still the one being asked to carry a device claim -- but the correct statement is that this calculation resolves neither, not that it resolves one and not the other. The entrance length is 0.196-0.447 mm against a 12.7 mm needle.

And the corrections do not point the same way. Fahraeus-Lindqvist -- a cell-depleted layer at the wall that lowers the apparent viscosity of blood in tubes below roughly 300 um [UNVERIFIED: no citation resolved for that threshold in this session] -- lowers the force; the entrance loss raises it. This lumen is 210 um, inside the first regime, so it is a real downward correction, but calling these numbers `upper bounds` was counting only the downward one and is withdrawn. Quantifying either needs measurements the Horner dataset does not contain: tube viscometry at this diameter for the first, a two-length needle test for the second.

One number in this file is not computed here: the `3 runs of residual sign against 9 expected` quoted above comes from `data/horner2020/model_comparison.md`, and it is the value for 18 of the 20 donors, not all of them (donor D has 8 runs, z = -0.52, and is not flagged structured).

## How much of this is uncertain, and can two patients be told apart?

Pushing each Casson parameter through its 95 % profile interval gives a force hull a median **29.1 %** of the force wide; the covariance-sampled joint interval is a median **25.2 %**. Per donor, in `uq.md`.

**The two are different objects and neither is simply the better number.** The hull moves one parameter at a time to each end of its own interval, so it is WIDER than the joint region it is drawn from. The sample IS a joint region but assumes the Wald normal, which is the approximation a profile likelihood exists because it distrusts.

**These donors cannot be ranked pairwise at 95 %.** Only **36 of 190** donor pairs (18.9 %) have disjoint hulls, against a pre-registered bar of 50 %. The bar was not moved.

Ranked by force, two donors are reliably separated only once they are **17 places apart out of 20**. So the extremes are distinguishable and the middle of the distribution is not.

**What that does and does not overturn.** The arithmetic on this page is untouched -- the donor spread is 128 % and the family-choice effect is 12.5 %, so the spread is still the bigger term. What it does not license is the PAIRWISE reading: *which patient the blood came from matters more* is a claim about telling two patients apart, and that compares one donor's interval with another's rather than a range with an effect size. **Quote the ordering of the extremes; do not rank the middle.**

**Which parameters the force is insensitive to.** `tau_y` on **20 of 20** donors (`|d ln F / d ln tau_y|` at worst 1.48e-02, against an `INSENSITIVE` threshold of 0.05); the force is very nearly proportional to `eta_c` alone (worst `|d ln F / d ln eta_c|` = 0.995). That is a THIRD and independent route to the section above -- a finite-difference sensitivity of the force solver, touching neither the stress ratio nor the zeroing test -- and all three agree.

The family term is **not** added into these intervals, and neither is the needle bore. See the section below and `uq.md`.

## The FAMILY the record was fitted to moves these forces by more than the yield stress does

Every donor on this page also has a `cross` and a `carreau_yasuda` record fitted by the same code to the SAME 16 points. Pushed through this same needle at this same flow rate (`crosscheck.md`, gate pre-registered in `crosscheck_prereg.md` before the run), they miss this page's Casson force by up to **30.18 %** and **33.02 %** respectively, with medians -11.19 % and -13.42 % -- both families predict a LOWER force. That is an order of magnitude more than the 2.94 % the yield stress contributes (that study reads this one's own `yield_share_max`, so the two cannot drift), and it is not a numerical artefact: the closed form and the quadrature agree to 1.02e-12 on this page's own records.

**It is NOT the extrapolation, and the first version of this section said it was.** At the needle the force ratio does track the ratio of the families' high-rate plateaux (to 3.19 %), so the obvious reading was that reading the fits 1.42 decades past the data is what breaks them. One line refutes it: the same two fits differ by up to 41.96 % at rates INSIDE the measured envelope, against 30.54 % at the needle, and the worst rate is the BOTTOM of the envelope. The families disagree everywhere on the curve because Casson misfits it -- median `rmse_log` 0.146 against Cross's 0.030, and `residual_structure` flags 19/20 donors on Casson against 2/20 on Cross. The needle is simply one rate at which that misfit is read.

**What survives and what does not.** The RANGE comparison survives: 30.18 % is well inside the 127.9 % donor-to-donor spread, so across this cohort the patient axis carries more of the force than the family axis does, and the yield stress is still invisible in this geometry. **The PAIRWISE reading of that does NOT survive** -- an earlier version of this sentence said *which patient the blood came from still matters more*, which is a claim about telling two given patients apart, and item 14 measured it directly: only 36 of 190 donor pairs have disjoint 95 % force intervals, and two donors must be 17 places apart out of 20 to be reliably separated. See the section above. The PRECISION does not: a force from this page is a Casson force, and reading the same donor's curve through a BETTER-fitting family -- Cross's median `rmse_log` is 0.030 against Casson's 0.146 -- moves it by tens of per cent. That asymmetry matters: this is not two equally good descriptions differing, it is the worse-fitting one being the one this page ships, because it is the one the source study publishes. The comparison is not vacuous -- a Newtonian null misses by 271.1 %, a 8.98x separation, so the needle has not simply washed out the difference between every model.
