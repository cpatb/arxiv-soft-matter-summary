# 03 — Adjacent industrial markets for a dense-suspension / yield-stress / drop-impact / high-speed-imaging / free-surface-CFD shop

Date: 2026-09-06/07. Method: ~40 web searches + page fetches (WebSearch/WebFetch). Every factual
claim carries a URL. Several job boards and publishers (Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter, Indeed, RSC,
Converting Quarterly) returned HTTP 403 on fetch, so for those only the page TITLE / search
snippet is available and is marked "(snippet only)". Market-size figures from report vendors are
marked "(vendor number)". "Not found" means I searched and found nothing, not that it does not exist.

## Summary

1. The one adjacent market with a quantified, dated, non-vendor cost-of-problem is **battery electrode slurry/coating**: Fraunhofer FFB (2024-10-17) puts gigafactory scrap at 15–30 % in early years, ~10 % after five, at ~€30 k/day per percentage point; a 2025 RSC review and a 2025 AIChE J paper both frame slurry rheology as the lever on coating window and defects; the UK's £17.9 m Nextrode programme (2019–2025) made "slurry rheology at different mixing scales" a headline workstream and its successor programme is £452 m (2026–2030).
2. **No job posting anywhere titled "rheologist" or "non-Newtonian CFD" was found in any of the ten markets.** Rheology appears as one required skill inside process-engineer, coatings-chemist and materials-scientist roles. Glassdoor listed 176 "rheology" jobs in the US (July 2026), Indeed 168 (snippet only) — across all industries combined.
3. Small (1–10 person) expert shops demonstrably exist and win paid work in three of these markets: mining tailings (Rheological Consulting Services, AU; Paterson & Cooke's labs), industrial inkjet (Alchemie Technology, Smart Chemistry, ImageXpert), and agrochemical spray (Silsoe Spray Applications Unit, with a CropLife Europe-commissioned contract report on the record). That is the strongest evidence that the *form* of business is viable; none of those shops sells Basilisk/OpenFOAM free-surface simulation, which no consultancy was found selling at all.
4. Drop impact + high-speed imaging is directly bought in exactly two places found: agrochemical spray retention/drift (2025 Soft Matter and J. Colloid papers, Lubrizol-style drift-reduction-adjuvant patents) and inkjet drop-watching (commercial dropwatcher equipment, 2025–26 papers on satellite formation and 1024-nozzle monitoring).
5. Shear thickening (the founder's PhD topic) is a live, costly problem in **CMP slurries** (agglomerate-induced scratches, papers 2012–2013) but the buyers (Entegris, Fujifilm Electronic Materials) hire in-house PhDs and publish little; no evidence of external contracting found.
6. **3D concrete printing** is exactly yield-stress/buildability physics and is hiring (ICON materials technician, July 2026; Indeed ~60 "concrete 3D printing" jobs), but the market-size numbers are vendor hype spanning 13x (USD 0.48 bn vs 6.29 bn for 2025) and the admixture makers (Cemex D.fab with COBOD, Sika, Heidelberg) already own mix design.
7. **Paints/coatings, cosmetics, and food (IDDSI, plant-based)** have rheology fully commoditised: half a dozen contract rheology labs (Rheology Lab UK, Rheology Solutions AU, RTS, IEH, Intertek, NETZSCH) sell shear-sweep/yield-stress tests; the differentiated question (drop impact, splash, free-surface simulation) is rarely asked there.
8. **Mining tailings / drilling / cementing** is the most mature buyer of yield-stress engineering, with named incumbents, API/ISO standard test protocols, and ~32 Halliburton drilling-fluids-engineer postings on Glassdoor; it is a real but crowded and geography-bound market (AU, ZA, CL, CA).
9. **Consumer splash products** (bidets, toilets, urinals, diapers) show sporadic academic interest (PNAS Nexus 2025 splash-free urinal, TOTO anti-splash bidet patent) and no evidence of external hiring; absorbent testing is standardised (NWSP/ISO 17190) and done in-house or by generic contract labs.
10. Ranking by (evidence x fit x feasibility): battery slurries > agrochemical spray > inkjet/dispensing of particle-loaded fluids > 3D concrete printing > tailings/paste > CMP > coatings > cosmetics > food > consumer splash > drilling/cement > diapers.

## a. Battery electrode slurries and coating

**Who buys.** Cell manufacturers' process/coating engineering; coater and mixer OEMs; binder/conductive-additive suppliers; dry-electrode and silicon-anode start-ups. Automate America's 2026 careers note describes gigafactory process roles as needing "slurry mixing, coating rheology, electrolyte handling" and quotes $95–150k for process engineers (recruiter marketing) https://automateamerica.com/blog/battery-ev-manufacturing-automation-careers-in-2026 .

**Does the physics change answers.** Yes, and the industry says so in its own words:
- Fraunhofer FFB white paper press release (2024-10-17): "Scrap rates of 15 to 30 per cent in the first few years are common in battery cell production"; after five years "the reject rates are still high at around ten percent"; "Each percentage point costs around 30.000 € per day and around ten million euros per year"; at 30 % reject "costs of around 900.000 € per day" https://www.ffb.fraunhofer.de/en/press/news/the-ramp-up-of-a-gigafactory-in-battery-cell-production.html (fetched and quoted verbatim).
- RSC Energy Advances 2025 review "Battery electrode slurry rheology and its impact on manufacturing": high viscosity limits coating speed and builds pressure, elasticity causes coating instabilities, low viscosity causes slump https://pubs.rsc.org/ya/article/4/1/84/879627 (fetch 403; snippet).
- AIChE J 2025 (Chen et al.) builds a micro-rheological model to predict the slot-die coating window from slurry formulation, stating that the formulation-to-rheology relation is hard to determine experimentally https://aiche.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/aic.18800 .
- Trade press: Converting Quarterly, "Slurry rheology and the scale-up challenge for cell manufacturers" (fetch 403; title only) https://convertingquarterly.com/slurry-rheology-and-the-scale-up-challenge-for-cell-manufacturers/ ; Precision Die Systems on slurry rheology vs slot-die https://www.precisiondiesystems.com/post/understanding-slurry-rheology-in-battery-manufacturing-and-its-impact-on-slot-die-coating-process ; infinityPV https://www.infinitypv.com/news/slot-die-coating-in-battery-production-why-slurry-rheology-matters .
- LiCAP (dry-electrode vendor) document: wet-coating scrap "5 % during normal operations", up to 30 % at ramp (vendor number) https://licaptech.com/static/media/electrodescrap.5e1c3d847c60b902c21f.pdf .
- Niobia AI (vendor): defect rates "5–8 % to below 0.5 %" with vision inspection (marketing) https://niobia.ai/blog/ai-vision-inspection-electrode-coating-calendering .

**Budget signals.** UK Nextrode programme £17.9 m (2019-10-01 to 2025-09-30), whose UKBIC collaboration explicitly focuses on "how electrode slurry rheology changes when mixed at different scales" https://www.faraday.ac.uk/research/lithium-ion/electrode-manufacturing/ and https://www.faraday.ac.uk/success-stories/ukbic-case-study-1/ . Successor Battery Innovation Programme: £452 m announced June 2025 for April 2026–March 2030 https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S3050660326000025 . Instrument vendors are selling into it: Rheonics inline viscosity/density for slurry lines https://rheonics.com/solutions-item/battery-production-slurry-mixing-and-coating/ ; Waters/TA "Discovery Core" rheometer aimed at routine QC (2024 press release) https://s205.q4cdn.com/962852172/files/doc_news/2024/10/1/New-Waters-Discovery-Core-Rheometer-Brings-the-Power-of-Rheology-to-Routine-Quality-Control-Testing-2024.pdf .

**Dry electrode.** Tesla reported both 4680 electrodes now dry-processed (Rigaku blog summarising the FY2025 update; Musk called it "incredibly difficult") https://rigaku.com/industries/environment-and-energy/batteries/blog/tesla-dry-electrode-manufacturing ; Tesla opening Giga Berlin to start-ups on the 4680 bottleneck (TechTimes, 2026-07-09) https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320029/20260709/tesla-opens-gigafactory-berlin-startups-crack-4680-battery-bottleneck.htm . Dry electrode is powder rheology / PTFE fibrillation / calendering, i.e. granular-paste mechanics rather than suspension rheology — adjacent to the DEM skill, not the shear-thickening one.

**Suppliers.** In-house labs at every major; Rheology Lab UK sells battery-slurry testing https://www.rheologylab.com/articles/electronic/battery-electrode-slurry/ ; Fraunhofer FFB and UKBIC are public pilot lines; no independent "coating-window modelling" consultancy found.

**Small-shop feasibility: 3.** The pain is quantified and the physics is his (dense suspensions, yield stress, free-surface coating bead which Basilisk can resolve). Against: no posting titled rheologist; Korean/Chinese/Japanese incumbents do this internally; US buildout access requires being on site; Rheonics and TA are selling hardware, not answers. The wedge is start-ups and OEMs that cannot hire a rheologist.

## The three I would bet on and why

1. **Battery electrode slurry / coating-window physics for start-ups and OEMs.** The only market with a dated, non-vendor cost-of-problem (Fraunhofer: €30 k/day per scrap point), public money explicitly aimed at "slurry rheology at scale" (Nextrode → £452 m successor), and 2025 papers whose stated gap (formulation → rheology → coating window) is exactly dense-suspension rheology plus a free-surface coating-bead problem Basilisk can resolve. Sell to the layer that cannot hire a rheologist: dry-electrode, silicon-anode, sodium-ion and solid-state start-ups; coater and mixer OEMs; binder/CNT suppliers.
2. **Agrochemical spray retention / adjuvant physics.** The one market where drop impact + high-speed imaging is bought as such (CropLife Europe → SSAU contract report; adjuvant patents built on droplet-spectrum measurements; 2025 papers claiming 5x retention gains from impact control). SSAU proves a small UK contract lab is paid for it; the founder adds non-Newtonian impact simulation and audited calibration, which SSAU does not advertise.
3. **Jetting and dispensing of particle-loaded fluids (inkjet, DIW, dispensing).** Smallest of the three but the one with the most small-shop precedent (Alchemie, Smart Chemistry, ImageXpert process development), and a live 2025–26 literature on satellites, particle-loaded filaments and rheology-driven waveforms. Best run as a service line inside bet 1 or 2, not as the company.

Runner-up: thickened tailings — real, boring, paid, but geography-bound and owned by P&C/RCS.

## Evidence AGAINST each

**Battery.** No posting titled rheologist or coating-physics scientist was found; rheology is one line in process-engineer specs at $88–150k, which says buyers value it as an employee skill, not a consulting line. The majors (CATL, LGES, Samsung SDI, Panasonic) do this in Asia with internal labs. Rheonics/TA/Waters sell the measurement; Fraunhofer and UKBIC give the scale-up advice as publicly funded pilot lines, i.e. free-ish competition. Dry electrode is granular/PTFE mechanics, not suspension rheology. The Fraunhofer cost figures are for total scrap, not the coating-attributable share; LiCAP's 5 % coating share is a vendor's number. US gigafactory build-out pace in 2025–26 was not researched here (open question).

**Agrochemical.** Zero application-technology job postings surfaced at Syngenta/Bayer/BASF/Corteva in 2025–26. SSAU already occupies the small-contract-lab slot in the UK and majors have in-house wind tunnels and high-speed cameras. Regulatory spend goes to field trials and drift classification (laser diffraction, per the patents), not to impact physics. The 2025 "5x retention" claims are academic (MIT-style) and may never become products.

**Inkjet/dispensing.** Small market; the equipment vendors (ImageXpert, Meteor) bundle jettability advice with hardware; established consultancies have 10+ years of printhead relationships. No 2025 job posting for a fluid physicist at Xaar/Dimatix/Meteor was found. Printed-electronics volumes are modest.
