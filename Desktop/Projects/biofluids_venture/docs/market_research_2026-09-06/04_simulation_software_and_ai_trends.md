# 04 — Where simulation software is going (2025–2026) and what a 1–3 person expert shop should build

Research date: 2026-09-06/07. Method: ~36 web searches and page fetches; every factual claim carries a URL; vendor and analyst-database numbers are flagged as such. Items I could not confirm are marked **not found**. Third-party revenue/headcount estimators (Latka, Owler, Tracxn, Growjo, ZoomInfo) are unaudited scrapes and are labelled "(estimator)".

---

## Summary

1. Physics-AI surrogates are where the money is: PhysicsX ($300M Series C at ~$2.4B, June 2026), Neural Concept ($100M Series C, Dec 2025), Luminary Cloud ($72M Series B, Sep 2025), all with NVIDIA's NVentures on the cap table. They sell *geometry-to-field* prediction for aero/thermal/structural design exploration in automotive, aerospace and defence.
2. Every one of those companies trains on Newtonian, single-phase, RANS-style CFD. None mentions non-Newtonian fluids, rheology, free-surface or yield-stress flow; the two vendor posts that discuss failure (Luminary, June 2026; NVIDIA, May 2026) locate it in turbulence-closure bias and "silent" non-physical outputs, and both ask domain experts to supply the diagnostics. They are neither a threat nor (yet) a customer for a rheology layer; they are a market that has not opened.
3. Data-scarce, out-of-distribution regimes are the documented weak point of neural surrogates (NeurIPS ML4CFD retrospective; PDEBench-style OOD studies), and 79% of ML-for-PDE papers claiming to beat classical solvers used weak baselines (McGreivy & Hakim, Nat. Mach. Intell. 2024). A rheometer flow curve with 16–54 points is the data-scarce regime.
4. Agentic CFD is real and fast: Foam-Agent 2.0 reports 88.2% execution success on 110 OpenFOAM tutorials with Claude 3.5 Sonnet; OpenFOAMGPT 2.0 claims 100% reproducibility over 450 runs. Their benchmarks are OpenFOAM tutorials (Poiseuille, porous media, aero) and their hardest residual failure class is "solver/model mismatch" (12.5% recovery). None benchmarks a non-Newtonian case or checks whether the material parameters were right. The agent needs something to call.
5. There is no fluid equivalent of Veryst/PolymerFEM's PolyUMod + MCalibration (solids). The closest is a UK lab (Rheology Lab / Centre for Industrial Rheology) selling "modelling-ready rheology data" as a measurement service with no uncertainty or identifiability output. Instrument-vendor software (TA TRIOS) fits Carreau-Yasuda/Cross/Herschel-Bulkley etc. but stops at parameters; nothing exports to a solver or checks the solver reproduced the curve. This gap is confirmed by absence, not by a positive source.
6. Material-model businesses that survived were bootstrapped by one expert and sold on tokens/seats to solver users (PolymerFEM: founder-run 2020–2024, then founder joined Ansys as Fellow); the data-platform ones (Citrine $81M raised, Materials Zone $7.5M, Albert Invent $45M+, Uncountable $27M Series A 2025) sell enterprise R&D SaaS to chemicals companies, not solver inputs.
7. Open-source-plus-services CFD is viable at 5 people (CFD Direct maintains 1.6M lines of OpenFOAM; Foundation target €500k/yr) and at 30–50 (Flow Science, Engys), but the OpenFOAM customer base is 71% companies with >1000 employees, so a solo shop sells to big-company engineers, not to peers.
8. UQ/calibration tooling has not made money: UQLab and Dakota went fully open source; SmartUQ is ~$1.8M revenue on 15 people after $8.2M raised (estimator). Differentiable CFD (JAX-Fluids, PhiFlow) remains academic. Calibration is a feature, not a company.
9. The durable layer in every prior cycle was validated data plus a credibility trail: ASME V&V 40 / NAFEMS VVUQ courses are still being sold in 2026, Granta MI sells "company-approved proprietary data plus reference data", and NVIDIA's own 2026 position is "don't yet trust the model, test the physics".
10. Implication: build the thing the agent and the surrogate both have to call and cannot fake — a cited, uncertainty-carrying, solver-verified material-model registry for complex fluids — and sell it as a per-record/per-seat product plus a calibration service, not as a solver, a surrogate, or a general "AI for rheology" platform.

**Where they are known to fail (their own words, plus the literature):**

Rheology/material properties: not mentioned. ([luminary.ai](https://luminary.ai/resources/why-physics-ai-models-need-to-be-anchored-to-reality/))
- NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo blog (29 May 2026), title "Don't Yet Trust the Model, Test the Physics": "A model can achieve a low MSE while still violating important physical constraints"; asks domain experts to contribute "edge cases and diagnostics that reveal failure modes standard loss curves miss". ([nvidia.github.io](https://nvidia.github.io/physicsnemo/blog/2026/05/29/physicsnemo-cfd/))
- Ansys SimAI docs: the model "will always generate a result"; user must read a confidence score and add high-fidelity cases where confidence is low ([ansyshelp](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/corp/v261/en/opti_ug/opti_ug_simai_training.html)).
- NeurIPS 2024 ML4CFD competition retrospective: industrially relevant data scarcity means ~200 training samples (AirfRANS) or 100 (NASA CRM); OOD generalisation across geometry is "a core challenge" ([NeurIPS D&B](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/f268ca663c202d012594a4b94bba5c1d-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf)). OOD studies in the small-data regime show "uneven generalization across coefficients, resolutions, time horizons, and physical regimes" ([arXiv 2601.08404](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08404)).
- McGreivy & Hakim, Nat. Mach. Intell. 6, 1256 (2024): 60/76 (79%) of ML-for-fluid-PDE papers claiming to beat standard solvers compared against a weak baseline; outcome-reporting and publication bias widespread ([nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00897-5)).
- Non-Newtonian ML: the work is academic and 2025-dated — GNN free-surface power-law flow ([Phys. Fluids 37, 123114](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/pof/article-abstract/37/12/123114/3374653/Graph-neural-network-modeling-of-free-surface)), PINN inference of viscoplastic models from velocity fields ([arXiv 2506.17681](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.17681)), the PNAS perspective on SciML for complex fluids ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2304669120)). No commercial product found.
- Industry attitude: at GM, "accuracy is not considered a huge concern at the design stage" because certification uses the wind tunnel ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/large-physics-models-design-engineering)). That is exactly the attitude a regulated biofluids/medical-device customer cannot take.

**Threat or customer?** For the rheology layer: neither today. They do not touch complex fluids, and when they do, the data-scarcity and "learns the solver's bias" problems bite hardest where the constitutive model is itself uncertain. A surrogate for a non-Newtonian process would need (a) a validated material model to generate training data and (b) a credibility trail for the regulator. That is an input they would buy or an integration they would allow; it is not something PhysicsNeMo/SimAI produce.

What they do not have, from their own failure taxonomies:

- Foam-Agent's ten error classes are syntax, missing files, BC name mismatch, BC type, **solver/model mismatch (12.5% recovery, "hardest residual class")**, convergence, mesh, geometry, unsupported workflow, unrecoverable reasoning. There is no class for "physically wrong material parameters", because the benchmark cannot detect it: ground truth is the tutorial's own output ([2505.04997](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04997)).
- The benchmarks are OpenFOAM tutorials. Tutorials carry fixed `nu`, `BirdCarreau` coefficients etc. that were chosen to run, not measured. An agent that reproduces them has learned the tutorial's number, not the fluid.
- OpenFOAMGPT sets temperature to 0 and injects documentation via RAG to stop "hallucinated directives" — the documentation it retrieves for `BirdCarreau` says which keys exist, not what values a given blood or Carbopol sample takes ([CFD Direct user guide](https://doc.cfd.direct/openfoam/user-guide-v13/transport-rheology)).
- The biorheo project's own record (memory: "OpenFOAM parameter names collide, swapped"; `CrossPowerLaw` calls the time `m` and the exponent `n`) is exactly the class of error an LLM will make silently and a tutorial-based benchmark will never catch.

**Is "the thing the agent calls" defensible?** The agents are converging on MCP as the interface (Foam-Agent 2.0, Luminary). An MCP tool that returns *a cited material record with intervals and a solver-verified export* is defensible in the same way a test oracle is: the agent cannot generate it, and a wrong one is invisible until validation. It is not defensible if it is merely "a Python function that fits Carreau" — any agent can write that in a minute. The defensible part is the registry content, the identifiability verdicts, and the round-trip verification, i.e. the provenance.

Net: the rheology-software field is academic packages plus instrument-vendor fitting; the money in adjacent "AI materials" is going to enterprise R&D platforms for chemicals companies. No one sells identifiability + solver export + verification.

### Q6. Inverse problems, calibration and UQ tooling

- **Differentiable CFD**: JAX-Fluids (TUM; compressible two-phase; scales to 512 A100s), JAX-CFD (Google), PhiFlow (TUM) — all academic; a 2024 review notes differentiable simulators "have not been widely adopted by other research" ([GitHub JAXFLUIDS](https://github.com/tumaer/JAXFLUIDS), [arXiv 2407.05560](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05560)). No commercial product found.
- **UQLab** (ETH): went fully open source BSD-3 with v2.0 (2022), includes Bayesian calibration ([uqlab.com](https://www.uqlab.com/license)). **Dakota** (Sandia): LGPL, free ([dakota.sandia.gov](https://dakota.sandia.gov/license/)). **UQpy**: open source (not re-verified this pass).
- **SmartUQ**: $1.8M revenue (2024) on 15 people, $8.22M raised (estimators) ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/smartuq), [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/smartuq)). That is the commercial ceiling found for a standalone UQ vendor after a decade.
- Bayesian inverse design with neural operators is a 2026 arXiv topic, not a product ([arXiv 2605.26059](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.26059)).

Implication: profile intervals, identifiability verdicts and Bayesian calibration are *necessary* content of a material record but do not sell on their own; the buyer pays for the answer with a credibility trail, not for the UQ engine.

### Q7. What durable value looks like

- **V&V and credibility frameworks are still sold in 2026**: NAFEMS is running "Verification, Validation and Uncertainty Quantification in Engineering Simulation" courses in 2026 built on ASME V&V 40, AIAA CFD guide, NASA-STD-7009 ([NAFEMS 2026](https://www.nafems.org/events/nafems/2026/verification-and-validation-in-engineering-simulation-online-02/)); ASME V&V 40 (medical devices) ties required credibility to decision risk ([ASME](https://www.asme.org/codes-standards/find-codes-standards/assessing-credibility-of-computational-modeling-through-verification-and-validation-application-to-medical-devices)). No AI-surrogate-specific standard was found; the frameworks are being applied to surrogates by analogy.
- **The AI vendors themselves now sell validation**: NVIDIA's May 2026 PhysicsNeMo-CFD post and Luminary's June 2026 "grounding" post both say the model is only as good as the experimental anchor and the expert-supplied diagnostics (Q1 sources). PhysicsX's argument that AI accuracy "can supersede simulation accuracy with the right data" rests on *incorporating experimental measurements* ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/large-physics-models-design-engineering)).
- **Material data businesses outlived several cycles** by owning validated, cited, company-approved data with integrations (Granta MI, 25 years; MatWeb since the 1990s; Digimat through three acquirers). The AI-formulation platforms are all <10 years old and VC-dependent.
- **Market context**: analysts put simulation software at ~$20–27B (2024/2025) growing 10–13% CAGR (analyst-house estimates, methodology not audited: [MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/simulation-software-market-263646018.html), [Precedence](https://www.precedenceresearch.com/simulation-software-market)); Synopsys claims a $31B TAM post-Ansys ([Synopsys](https://news.synopsys.com/2025-07-17-Synopsys-Completes-Acquisition-of-Ansys)). Consolidation (Synopsys–Ansys, Siemens–Altair, Keysight–ESI, Cadence–Hexagon D&E) means the incumbents are now four conglomerates; niche physics is bought, not built, in such firms.
- **Practitioner view of what LLMs commoditise**: setup, input-file generation, geometry scripting, boilerplate post-processing; what they amplify is scenario design and interpretation, and "the more faithfully a simulation model represents reality, the more complex it becomes" ([J. Intell. Manuf. 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10845-025-02732-z), [arXiv 2503.18178](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18178)). The clarification-benchmark literature (SCICONVBENCH, 2026) shows the residual hard part is *task formulation*, i.e. asking the right physical question ([arXiv 2605.18630](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18630)).

## Future-proof product shapes for a 1–3 person expert shop (ranked)

**1. The cited, verified complex-fluid material registry, sold per record and per seat, with an MCP/API front end.**
Reasoning: it is the one artefact in this survey that nobody sells (Q3, Q5), that every agent and surrogate needs and cannot generate (Q2, Q1), and that matches the only business shape that outlived multiple cycles (Granta, MatWeb, Digimat — validated data plus integrations). The moat is content and provenance (DOI, fit objective, envelope, identifiability verdict, corrected intervals, solver round-trip hash), not code. Pricing analogue: MatWeb $99.95/yr for solids reference; Granta MI enterprise for "company-approved" data. Start free/open for the public-literature records (blood, Carbopol, cornstarch), charge for private-record hosting, solver export packs, and a credibility report.
Counter-argument: databases are slow to reach critical mass, the public-literature supply of good rheometry is thin (the biorheo Horner audit shows how much of it is protocol-contaminated), and a large vendor (Synopsys/Ansys Granta) could add a rheology table in a quarter. Mitigation: the value is not the table but the verdicts and the round-trip; ship those as the thing Granta does not do.

**2. Calibration-as-a-service with a V&V 40-style credibility report, for regulated biofluids / medical-device CFD.**
Reasoning: the customer who cannot say "accuracy is not a concern at the design stage" (GM's attitude, Q7) is the regulated one. ASME V&V 40 is a medical-device standard; NAFEMS still sells VVUQ training in 2026. Rheology Lab already sells "modelling-ready data" without uncertainty (Q3), so the demand exists and the credibility layer is the upgrade. A 1–3 person shop can deliver a report per project (rheometer data → identifiable model → exported dictionary → verified solve → intervals on the engineering quantity, e.g. needle force) using the existing package, overnight, agentically.
Counter-argument: services do not scale and are exactly what LLMs threaten to commoditise; revenue is lumpy; medical-device sales cycles are long. Mitigation: each engagement feeds product 1 (with consent), and the report template becomes a product.

**3. The validation harness for physics-AI in complex fluids: "the physics tests the surrogate has to pass".**
Reasoning: NVIDIA (May 2026) and Luminary (June 2026) both explicitly ask domain experts for edge-case diagnostics; PhysicsNeMo-CFD is config-driven YAML benchmarking with L2/drag/lift metrics and nothing non-Newtonian. A package of analytic anchors (Poiseuille/Casson tube flow closed forms, yield-surface checks, envelope guards, quantization-aware gates) that plugs into PhysicsNeMo-CFD or SimAI confidence scoring is small, expert-only work, and positions the shop as the reviewer rather than the competitor.
Counter-argument: the surrogate vendors have not entered complex fluids yet, so the customer may be 2–4 years away; open-source harnesses earn reputation, not revenue. Mitigation: build it as the test suite of product 1 (it largely exists in biorheo already) and publish it; charge when a vendor wants a certified run.

**4. The fluid counterpart of MCalibration: a desktop/CLI product for engineers who own a rheometer and a solver, sold on solver tokens.**
Reasoning: PolymerFEM proves the shape — one expert, founder-run, sold per solver seat to Abaqus/Ansys/COMSOL users, exited to Ansys. The fluids gap is confirmed by absence. Instrument vendors fit but do not export or verify.
Counter-argument: fitting is the most commoditised step in the table above; the desktop-tool market is being absorbed into the four conglomerates; the OpenFOAM installed base is large-company and small in count (0.1% of CAE installs). Success here likely looks like "acquired by an instrument vendor or a CAE major", which may be the intended outcome but is not 5–10-year independence.

**5. Open-source-core plus paid support for a non-Newtonian OpenFOAM/Basilisk toolkit (Engys/CFD Direct shape).**
Reasoning: proven viable at 5–50 people; the shop already has the solver knowledge.
Counter-argument: it competes with rheoTool (free) and with the maintainers' own branches; needs a community before revenue; at 1–3 people the support load is the product. Lowest rank because the artefact it sells (solver code) is the one agents are getting best at writing.

## Things NOT to build

- **A general-purpose CFD agent or "OpenFOAM copilot".** Foam-Agent, OpenFOAMGPT, MetaOpenFOAM, FoamGPT, Luminary MCP, Simcenter Copilot already exist and are funded or open source; the hard part they leave behind is the material truth, not the file generation.
- **A neural surrogate / foundation model for non-Newtonian flow.** Data-scarce, OOD-fragile (Q1), and 79% of the literature it would be benchmarked against uses weak baselines; the funded players have $100M+ and NVIDIA on the cap table. If a surrogate is ever needed, be the one who generates and certifies its training data.
- **A standalone UQ or Bayesian-calibration tool.** UQLab and Dakota are free; SmartUQ is ~$1.8M/yr after ten years and $8M raised.
- **A formulation-AI / ELN platform.** Albert Invent, Uncountable, Citrine, Materials Zone are funded and enterprise-sold; rheology is one column to them.
- **An "AI predicts rheology from composition" product.** No identifiable data supply, and the RhINN academic line has produced no product in five years.
- **Hourly CFD consulting as the business.** Contested even on forums; the customer base is large firms; it is the first thing agents erode.
- **Anything whose only moat is a Carreau fit.** TRIOS ships nine flow models; pyRheo is free.
