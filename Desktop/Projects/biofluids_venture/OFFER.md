# The offer: an injectability calibration study

Draft, 2026-09-07. This is the thing being sold: one fixed-price study, one deliverable,
one fluid. It is written to be used on a call and to be checked against the code, so every
capability claim here points at a file that exists and every gap is named.

Ranked first in `docs/market_research_2026-09-06/00_SYNTHESIS.md` §3. The evidence for the
gap is there; this file is the offer, not the argument for it.

---

## 1. In one sentence

*You send a viscosity curve and a device; you get back the injection force with a
confidence interval, a statement of which parameters the answer does not depend on, and
the distance in decades between where your data stops and where your needle runs.*

## 2. What arrives

A single page. Four worked examples of exactly that page are in
`../biorheo/case_studies/deliverable_example/`, generated by
`biorheo.apps.report` rather than written by hand, and regenerated by a test.

| # | on the page | why it is not standard |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the force, **with an interval, and the interval named** | it is a one-at-a-time profile push-through hull, which is *wider* than the joint 95 % region it is drawn from. The two are not interchangeable, and the page says which it is quoting, every time |
| 2 | **which parameters the answer does not depend on** | by finite difference on the force itself. On blood in a fine needle the force goes as `eta_c^0.995` and is insensitive to the yield stress, so effort spent measuring a yield stress does not move that answer |
| 3 | **where your data stops and the device starts**, in decades | every blood example here is an extrapolation of 1.42 decades and says so above the fold, not in a footnote |
| 4 | **`d ln F / d ln R`**: is a Newtonian `R^-4` sizing rule safe for *your* fluid? | measured, not assumed. Blood in a fine needle: `-3.98`, and the rule is right to within **1.24 %** on all 20 donors tested. A 0.5 wt% microgel: `-2.31`, and the rule over-predicts the force at a 30 % finer bore by **82 %**. Same page, opposite verdicts, and only a measurement separates them |
| 5 | a **derating factor** against your force budget | the flow rate the budget allows at the fit, against the flow rate it allows at the top of the parameter interval. Across the registry that ratio runs 0.12 to 0.97, so the fit uncertainty alone can cost a factor of 8 in deliverable flow rate |
| 6 | **provenance**: DOI, licence, and whether the data may be used commercially | so the number can be traced out of the report and back to a curve |

Optionally, at no extra work: an OpenFOAM `transportProperties` dictionary for the same
fitted law, with a round-trip report showing the solver reproduces the record. That layer
is built (`biorheo.solver.openfoam`, six of seven constitutive families) and it found
something worth telling a buyer. **The export is exact and the solve is not.** At high
Bingham number `simpleFoam` returned a viscosity 172 % wrong with `rc = 0`, no warning and
a written result.

## 3. What it does not include

Say all of this on the call, not in a schedule at the back.

- **No bench measurement.** No rheometer, no force stand, no imaging. If you have no
  curve, the study cannot start; see §4.
- **No regulatory authoring.** No V&V 40 credibility plan, no submission text, no MDDT.
  That is a separate, later offer, and it is not built.
- **No wall slip.** The calculation assumes no slip at the needle wall. Microgels and many
  biological fluids slip at the kPa wall stresses a fine needle produces, which lowers the
  true force. It is a boundary condition rather than a constitutive extrapolation, so the
  extrapolation flag does not cover it, and detecting it needs two needle radii at the
  same wall stress. That is a measurement, not an analysis.
- **No temperature model.** A cold-chain question can be answered *only if you supply the
  shift*. `biorheo.constitutive.shift` carries a time-temperature superposition
  `eta(gd; T) = a·eta_ref(a·gd)` exactly, and the force calculation, the envelope and the
  report all follow it through. But **`a` comes from you**, either as the ratio of two
  viscosities you measured at the same shear rate, or from your own activation energy.
  There is no activation energy for any fluid anywhere in the package and there will not
  be one: that is a measurement, not a constant to be looked up.

  Two things come with it. The superposition is an assumption and the study reports its
  residual, so if your two curves do not collapse under a single factor you are told,
  rather than handed a confident number. And a shifted curve **loses its error bar**. A
  confidence interval belongs to a fit against measured data, there is no data at the
  shifted temperature, so the shifted result is reported as a point estimate.
- **Not included in the force**: the barrel's own pressure drop, entrance and exit losses,
  the kinetic term, the transient at plunger start, and syringe friction (a property of
  your device, supplied by you if you want it added). These do not all point the same
  way, so the number is not an upper bound.
- **No protein-specific physics.** Aggregation, adsorption, shear-induced damage and
  concentration-dependence are outside the model. The study treats your fluid as a
  generalised-Newtonian fluid described by the curve you send.

## 4. What you send

The study is a day of analysis and a day of writing once this arrives. Most of the
calendar time is waiting for it.

1. **A flow curve**: shear rate and viscosity (or shear stress), as numbers, not a figure.
   Replicates if you have them.
2. **How it was measured**: instrument, geometry, gap, temperature, whether the sweep was
   rate- or stress-controlled, and any pre-shear protocol.
3. **The device**: needle inner diameter and length as *dimensions*. A gauge number is a
   range, not a dimension, and this package will not turn one into the other. Syringe
   barrel inner diameter. Any measured friction or stiction.
4. **The operating point**: target volume and delivery time, or a target flow rate; and
   your force budget if you have one.
5. **Licence position**: whether the curve may be used in a deliverable to you, and
   whether anything may be retained. Default: nothing is retained.

If a curve stops decades below the needle's own shear rate, which is the normal case, the
study still runs and reports the distance. Measuring one point at the device's own rate is
usually the cheapest thing that changes the answer, and the report says so.

## 5. Price

**Fixed price. No hourly billing, no free pilot.** Both are the only failure modes with
documented evidence behind them in the research (`docs/.../05_solo_founder_business_models.md`).

| tier | scope | indicative |
|---|---|---|
| **Screen** | one fluid, one device, one operating point; the page in §2 | $2k to $4k |
| **Study** | one fluid, a device design space (gauges × rates), worst-corner selection and derating; plus the OpenFOAM export and round trip | $6k to $12k |
| **Programme** | several fluids or a formulation series, with a re-runnable script the client keeps | $15k to $25k |

**These prices are inferred, not observed.** The research found *no public price for any
injectability, V&V 40 or credibility-plan service*. The anchors it did find: US CFD studies
$1.5k to $20k fixed; PhD consultant median $200/h (IEEE-USA); specialist day rates $1,200
to $5,000; per-sample contract rheology $170 to $340; university cores rent rheometers to
industry at $43 to $64/h. The first three engagements should be treated as price discovery
and the number recorded.

## 6. The honest state of the capability

What is built and tested (573 automated tests, `../biorheo`):

- registry of cited fluid records with envelopes and DOI-resolved provenance
- constitutive fitting with profile-likelihood intervals and a per-parameter
  identifiability verdict
- needle flow for seven constitutive families, closed-form where one exists
- the deliverable page, with its refusals -- from a fitted record OR from screen data (one viscosity, no curve), which is the shape most client data arrives in
- OpenFOAM export and round trip for six families
- a temperature shift carried exactly through all of the above, on a factor you supply,
  with the superposition's own residual reported beside it
- ten CC BY 4.0 records, of which eight reproduce the source paper's own published
  parameters -- as a viscosity curve over the measured range, to a median 1-7 % and a worst
  19 %, inside the 20 % repeatability those authors state for their own measurements
- **an EXTERNAL anchor for the chain itself, added 2026-09-08.** Until then every tube
  anchor here was analytic -- Buckingham-Reiner, the Casson closed form, Poiseuille, an
  OpenFOAM Couette run against an analytic profile -- so the claim this offer is built on,
  *a model identified from one measurement predicts a different geometry*, had never been
  compared against a measured pressure-driven flow. On a CC BY 4.0 pipe rheometer
  (Koponen et al., *Chem. Eng. Sci.* 2023; 660 rows, three pipe diameters), identifying on
  the 19 mm pipe and predicting the 8.15 mm one -- a factor of **2.33 in radius** --
  reproduces the measured pressure gradient to a **median 18.7 %**, with the same-pipe
  control at **2.4 %**, so the error is extrapolation and not misfit.
  Three things must be said with it, and they are on `biorheo/data/foam_koponen2023/`:
  **it is a foam**, so no number from it transfers to blood or a protein formulation; it is
  **pipe-to-pipe**, not rheometer-to-pipe, because that deposit's rheometer file is the
  base solution rather than the flowing fluid; and its own pre-registered discriminator
  **refuses to let the pass count as evidence about wall slip**, because deleting the slip
  term makes the prediction better. Five of that item's seven registered predictions were
  refuted, and the report says which.

What is **not** built, in the order it blocks revenue:

1. **Almost no commercially-usable reference data.** 115 of the 125 records are
   `commercial_use: false`. The microgel data is CC BY-NC-SA and the blood data comes
   from a workbook that states no licence at all. **This does not block a paid study**,
   which runs on the client's own curve, but until 2026-09-08 it blocked showing anything
   in public.

   Ten records now do carry a commercial licence: eight of DMEM with fetal bovine serum
   and two of a Carbopol microgel at two temperatures, all CC BY 4.0 from deposited Zenodo
   datasets. They are enough for a public method demo and the first eight come with a real
   external validation -- the fit reproduces the source paper's own published parameters,
   as a viscosity curve over the measured range, to inside the 20 % repeatability those
   authors state for their own measurements. (An earlier and stronger-sounding version of
   that claim, "reproduces the published flow indices on 4/4 conditions, worst 0.03", was
   **corrected on 2026-09-08**: it had been graded against the paper's figure-caption
   values rather than its fit-results table, and against the latter the same bar fails.)

   **They are still not enough for an injectability demo.** Cell culture medium measured to
   1 1/s is four to five decades away from what a needle imposes, and the microgel reaches
   1e3 1/s. A licensable *flow curve* that reaches rheometer-to-needle rates is still
   missing -- though the wedge fluid itself is now covered from single-viscosity data; see
   item 3.
2. **No temperature MODEL, and no record with a temperature axis.** The shift layer
   above carries a factor; it does not predict one. One fluid in the registry is measured
   at two temperatures (DMEM + 10 % FBS at 20 and 37 C), and a one-parameter shift does
   NOT collapse that pair -- the two curves differ in the flow index, which no shift can
   move. A client who cannot supply two curves or an
   activation energy still cannot get a cold-chain answer. §3.
3. **A high-concentration protein FLOW CURVE** -- narrowed on 2026-09-08, not closed.
   The wedge fluid is now covered: `case_studies/mab_injectability/` runs the whole chain on
   **58 real formulations of a monoclonal antibody at 159-212 mg/mL**, CC BY 4.0, on the same
   needle and syringe as the blood study. Two things came out of it that a formulation
   scientist can use, and both are measurements rather than claims:

   * at **matched** mAb concentration the best and worst excipient differ by 7.3x in
     viscosity, which through the concentration law is worth about **63 mg/mL of extra mAb
     at the same viscosity**. The raw spread across the table says 3.5x and 39 mg/mL, and
     it is the wrong number, because these formulations are not at one concentration and
     viscosity is exponential in it;
   * the needle extrapolates only **0.74 decades** past what that source measured, against
     1.42 for blood and four to five for the cell-culture medium -- the least-extrapolated
     fluid in this project.

   **What is still missing is the word "curve".** That source verified Newtonian behaviour
   over 960-3329 1/s and then reported **one viscosity per formulation**, so there is no
   flow curve to fit, no confidence interval, and no registry record -- a one-parameter
   model on one point has zero degrees of freedom, which the study demonstrates rather than
   asserts.

   **That shape is now a supported input rather than a limitation.** `build_from_screen`
   produces the deliverable page straight from a measured viscosity and its replicate SD,
   and the page says what it is: the interval is the **measurement's own repeatability**,
   named as such and explicitly not a parameter interval, and the page never calls anything
   fitted. `case_studies/deliverable_example/05_screen.md` is the worked example -- **the
   first licensable page in that set and the only one on the wedge fluid.**
4. **No bench.** By design until an invoice exists.

## 7. How a call ends

One of three ways, and the third is the common one:

- *"Send me a quote for this fluid"*: take the intake list in §4.
- *"We do this in-house"*: ask who owns the credibility argument, and whether anyone puts
  an error bar on the predicted force. That is the question the research could not answer
  from the web, and it is the reason the call is worth taking.
- *"Not now"*: ask who else should be asked, and whether they would want the one-page
  method note. That note is the acquisition channel; cold outreach is the documented
  failure.
