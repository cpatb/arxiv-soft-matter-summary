## 1. ~~Fix `joint.py`~~ DONE (commit c18cc31)

It is rank-deficient by exactly one, verified twice: scaling `(phi, phi_m, phi_0)`
by `c` moves every predicted viscosity by 2e-15, and the Jacobian's smallest
singular value is 1.5e-5 against a largest of 119, null vector
`(phi_0, phi_m, 0, phi)`.

**Why first:** `np.linalg.inv` does not raise on a singular matrix, so N5 prints
`phi_0 = 0.6912 +/- 924.4` and N6 `0.6778 +/- 1110`. Those are arithmetic noise
being presented as uncertainty, in a package whose entire selling point is
honest uncertainty. It is the one thing here that would actively embarrass the
product.

- **Decision: pin `phi_0`, not `phi_m`.** `phi_0` is the gauge (see
  `wyart-cates-phi0-is-gauge` in memory); `phi_m` is the quantity of interest and
  should stay free. Pin it at the per-dataset value already in `ConstantSet`.
- Replace `inv` with `scipy.linalg.pinvh` plus an explicit rank check that
  **raises** rather than warns.
- Fix `n_params` at `joint.py:131`, which counts the flat parameter, so every
  joint AIC is 2 too high.
- Re-run N5 and N6. **Expect the conclusions to survive** (the joint fit loses by
  55 and 568 AIC; a 2-unit correction does not flip either). If a conclusion
  *does* flip, that is the session's headline and everything below waits.
- Add a test that a rank-deficient design raises instead of returning numbers.

none of them can fail:

- N6: "`phi_0` moves the level of `phi_m` and leaves the reduced chi-squared

For N4, promote the **gauge-invariant** version: run the same recovery test on
Guy's data where `phi` is a genuinely known volume fraction. It gives slope
**1.20 / 1.23 with a non-zero intercept**, which no `phi_0` removes. It is a
stronger check and it fails, which is more useful than a passing one that tests
nothing.

## 3. ~~Restrict every series to the curves its authors call steady~~ DONE — **the kill criterion fired**

Result in `STEADY_SUBSET_2026-08-29.md`, gate in `scripts/gate_n8.py`. Reduced
chi-squared falls 20x / 98x / 10x on the three series, and Guy's 404 nm lands at
3.6, close to consistent. The claim survives in weaker form and the spec must be
written around that. Three follow-ups are listed in that file's section 6; the
cheapest and most valuable is a bound-hitting admission rule.

Original text follows.

Two independent routes now say the non-steady curves should come out:

- Richards' 0.46/0.48/0.50 curves are colour-coded in their own deposited
  `Figure1.py` as the shear-jamming/oscillatory regime — time averages of an
  oscillating rate, not steady flow curves.
- `systematic_misfit()` reaches the same exclusion from the instrument side,
  landing on exactly the 56% and 58% curves Guy et al. call unsteady.

Reported effect: Richards goes from reduced chi² **221 to 11.0** and 0/7 to 3/4
within 2σ; Guy from **344 to 36.9**; and on Guy's steady subset the `phi_m` drift
**stops being monotone**.

**That is why this is the strategic item.** The product's headline is "single-curve
`phi_m` is not identifiable, and here is the reference dataset proving it". Part
of the current evidence is a monotone drift across three datasets — and that drift
may live in the curves the source labs already disowned.

Work it as a pre-registered question, written before running:

- **Kill criterion:** if `phi_m` consistency largely recovers on the steady
  subsets, the "not identifiable" claim is *weaker than currently written* and the
  spec must say so.
- **What survives regardless:** the degeneracy mechanism itself. `phi` and `phi_m`
  enter only through `phi/phi_J`; `r(phi, phi_m)` runs to +0.99 and `phi_m − phi`
  collapses to 0.0013. That is algebra plus a measurement, not a curve-selection
  artefact, and it does not depend on which curves are admitted.
- Write the admission rule in code, not by hand at read-time.

**Do not rewrite the spec until this lands.**

## 4. Two smaller statistical debts. ~1 hour.

- **N7 compares two different rulers.** `Profile.wald_width` uses
  `2·sqrt(f_crit)·se`, `BootstrapInterval.wald_width` uses `2·1.95996·se` —
  differing by 4.9% at dof 26 and 13% at dof 5. That is exactly the comparison
  N7's argument turns on. Make them one function.
- **`block_bootstrap` does not warm-start.** It refits each resample with
  `n_starts=1` from a generic start, which is why its iid control comes back
  *narrower* than Wald (0.8–1.0×). A percentile bootstrap that cannot reproduce
  Wald under Wald's own assumption is not calibrated yet.

Also record, without pretending to fix it: refitting each curve on the top and
bottom three-quarters of its stress branch moves `phi_m` by **+0.7 to +12.4 Wald
σ, always positive**. That is a bias, and no resampling of residuals estimates a
bias. So N7's margin against the 9.3–14.1× needed to reconcile the series is a
factor of ~1.5, not ~7.

## 5. The control on G11's method. ~2 hours. First new science.

Lin's `Fig3` is the simulation counterpart: same reversal protocol, but contact
and hydrodynamic stress **evaluated directly** rather than inferred. So `f` can be
read without inverting anything.

Fit the strain clock both ways on the same record — through the Wyart-Cates
inversion, and from the directly evaluated contact fraction — and compare. That
tests the inversion instead of assuming it, which is the one thing G11 currently
takes on faith.

`read_lin_transients` already reads `Fig_3a`. What is missing is the steady fit to
pair it with; `Fig_3c_total.csv` supplies 26 rates. Caveat to carry: the panel's
rates are in units of the simulation's own scale, not 1/s, so its clock is
dimensionless and it is a control on the *method*, not a second measurement of the
same constant.

## Two traps this codebase has sprung more than once

- **A scripted documentation edit can silently match nothing.** Assert the
  replacement count before writing the file.
- **Heredoc Python eats backslashes.** `\N` and `\S` in a Windows path or a
  match string arrive mangled. Use forward slashes, or the Write tool.
