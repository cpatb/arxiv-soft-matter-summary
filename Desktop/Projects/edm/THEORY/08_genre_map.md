# The genre map

Dance music genres are mostly distinguished by four things: tempo, kick pattern, swing, and
sound palette. Once you can hear those four, you can identify almost anything, and the
endless subgenre arguments become much less mysterious.

The two big families are **four on the floor** (a kick on every beat) and **broken beat** (a
syncopated kick with a backbeat snare). Everything below belongs to one or the other.

## Four on the floor

| Genre | BPM | Tells | Start here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Disco | 110 to 125 | Live band, strings, real bass guitar, offbeat hats | Chic, Donna Summer |
| Chicago house | 118 to 127 | 909 or 707 drums, raw, jack tracks, minimal chords | Marshall Jefferson, Phuture |
| Deep house | 118 to 125 | 7th and 9th chords, soft kick, warm pads, jazzy | Larry Heard, Kerri Chandler, Moodymann |
| Acid house | 118 to 130 | TB-303 squelch and slide, high resonance | Phuture, Armando, 808 State |
| Garage (US) | 120 to 128 | Gospel vocals, swung, soulful, organ | Todd Terry, Masters at Work |
| French / filter house | 120 to 128 | Looped and filtered disco samples, heavy compression | Daft Punk, Stardust, Cassius |
| Nu-disco | 115 to 125 | Modern production, disco bass and chords | Todd Terje, Dimitri From Paris |
| Tech house | 120 to 128 | Techno drums with a house groove, minimal, dry | Green Velvet, Hot Since 82 |
| Afro house | 118 to 125 | Live percussion, tribal patterns, chant vocals | Black Coffee |
| Progressive house | 124 to 130 | Very long builds, wide pads, melodic | Sasha, Eric Prydz |
| Big room / festival EDM | 126 to 132 | Enormous supersaw drops, minimal groove | Martin Garrix |
| Techno | 125 to 150 | Often no backbeat clap, industrial, hypnotic, dark | Jeff Mills, Robert Hood, Surgeon |
| Detroit techno | 120 to 135 | Melodic, futurist, string pads, funk underneath | Derrick May, Juan Atkins |
| Minimal techno | 120 to 130 | Almost nothing happening, very slowly | Richie Hawtin, Ricardo Villalobos |
| Trance | 130 to 145 | Supersaw leads, 16th-note bass, huge breakdowns | Paul van Dyk, Above & Beyond |
| Hardstyle / gabber | 140 to 200 | Distorted kick with a pitched tail | Various, at your own risk |
| Ghetto house | 130 to 150 | Bare 909, obscene chants, no chords | DJ Funk, DJ Deeon |
| Juke | 155 to 165 | Ghetto house sped up, chopped vocals | DJ Slugo, Traxman |

## Broken beat

| Genre | BPM | Tells | Start here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electro | 120 to 135 | 808, syncopated kick, robotic, sci-fi | Drexciya, Kraftwerk |
| Breakbeat | 125 to 140 | Sampled funk breaks, no four-on-floor | The Chemical Brothers |
| UK garage / 2-step | 130 to 140 | Heavily swung, skipping kick, chopped vocals | MJ Cole, Artful Dodger |
| Grime | 130 to 145 | Sparse, aggressive, MC-led | Wiley, Dizzee Rascal |
| Dubstep | 138 to 142 | Half-time feel (snare on 3), heavy sub, wobble bass | Mala, Skream |
| Footwork | 155 to 165 | Triplet kicks, extreme repetition, huge space | RP Boo, DJ Rashad |
| Jungle | 160 to 175 | Chopped Amen break, reggae basslines | Goldie, Dillinja |
| Drum and bass | 170 to 176 | Two-step break, sub bass, dark or liquid | LTJ Bukem, Noisia |

Note the gap between about 145 and 155 BPM. Very little lives there, because those tempos
are physically awkward: too fast to dance to at full speed, too slow to feel half-time.

Note also that dubstep at 140 BPM and drum and bass at 175 both feel slow, because their
snare pattern implies a half-time or double-time reading. Tempo alone does not determine
perceived speed.

## The Chicago family tree

Everything below came from within about ten miles of where you live.

    Disco (1970s)
        |
    The Warehouse, Frankie Knuckles (1977 to 1982)
        |
    Chicago house (1984)
        |-- Deep house ------> Larry Heard, then everywhere
        |-- Acid house ------> UK rave, then trance and techno
        |-- Jack tracks -----> Ghetto house -----> Juke -----> Footwork
        |-- Hip house -------> a dead end, mostly
        |
    (exported to New York, London, Ibiza, Berlin)
        |
    Everything else with a four-on-the-floor kick

Detroit techno developed in parallel about 280 miles away and the two scenes fed each other
constantly. House is warmer and more soulful; techno is colder and more futurist. That is
the entire distinction, and it dissolves the moment you look at any specific record.

## Which one should you make?

Start with **Chicago house or deep house**, for four reasons.

The tempo is forgiving. At 122 BPM you have time to hear what you are doing.

The arrangements are simple. A deep house track is a groove, two chords and a bassline.
There is nowhere for production weakness to hide, but there is also very little to learn
before you can make a complete one.

The production standard is achievable. Nobody expects a deep house record to sound like a
mastered festival single. Warmth and groove beat polish.

And it is the local tradition, which is what you said you wanted.

> **Watch out.** Avoid starting with trance, big-room or drum and bass. All three demand a
> level of technical production skill that will make your first six months miserable.
