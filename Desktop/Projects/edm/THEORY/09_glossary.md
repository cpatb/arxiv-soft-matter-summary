# Glossary

Every term you are likely to hear, in one line each. Grouped by area rather than
alphabetised, because you will be looking things up by context.

## Rhythm and timing

**BPM** Beats per minute. The tempo.

**Bar** One cycle of the meter. Four beats in almost all dance music.

**Beat** One quarter note. In house, one kick drum.

**Step** One sixteenth note. A drum machine bar has sixteen of them.

**Downbeat** Beat 1 of a bar.

**Offbeat / upbeat** The "&" halfway between two beats. Where house hi-hats and basslines
live.

**Backbeat** Beats 2 and 4. Where the clap or snare goes.

**Four on the floor** A kick drum on every beat.

**Two-step** A broken kick pattern, characteristic of UK garage.

**Half-time** A pattern that implies half the tempo, usually by putting the snare on beat 3
instead of on 2 and 4.

**Swing / shuffle** Delaying every second subdivision to create a lopsided groove. 50% is
straight, 66.7% is a full triplet feel.

**Quantise** Snapping notes to the nearest grid position.

**Groove / feel** The overall rhythmic character, including swing, velocity and micro-timing.

**Syncopation** Accents placed where the meter does not expect them.

**Polyrhythm** Two rhythmic cycles of different lengths running at once.

**Ghost note** A deliberately quiet hit between the accented ones.

**Velocity** How hard a note is played, 0 to 127. Usually maps to volume and brightness.

**Phrase** A group of bars, usually 4, 8, 16 or 32, forming one unit of the arrangement.

## Arrangement

**Intro** The opening section, usually drums only, designed for the DJ to mix into.

**Outro** The closing section, same idea in reverse.

**Breakdown** A section where the drums drop out and the atmosphere takes over.

**Build / riser section** The tension-increasing section before a drop.

**Drop** The moment everything returns at full force. The payoff.

**Riser** A rising noise or tone used to build tension.

**Impact** A low booming hit marking a drop.

**Fill** A short variation at the end of a phrase that signals a change is coming.

**Loop** A repeating section of music.

**Loop syndrome** The chronic condition of making dozens of loops and finishing no tracks.

**Stem** A submix of one group of elements (all drums, all vocals) exported separately.

**Edit / rework / VIP** Versions of a track: a DJ edit for mixing, a rework by someone else,
a VIP as the producer's own alternate version.

**Dub / instrumental** A version with the vocal removed or heavily reduced.

**Extended mix** The full-length club version, as opposed to the radio edit.

## Harmony

**Semitone** The smallest step in Western music. A ratio of 2^(1/12).

**Interval** The distance between two notes, measured in semitones.

**Scale** The set of notes a piece stays inside.

**Key** A scale plus a home note.

**Root** The note a chord is built on.

**Triad** A three-note chord.

**Seventh / ninth chord** A chord with a fourth or fifth note stacked on, giving the jazzy,
unresolved quality house is built on.

**Inversion** The same chord with a different note at the bottom.

**Voicing** The specific arrangement and spacing of a chord's notes.

**Voice leading** Moving between chords by shifting each note as little as possible.

**Progression** A sequence of chords.

**Vamp** A short progression, often just two chords, repeated indefinitely.

**Roman numerals** Key-independent shorthand for chords: i, iv, V and so on.

**Relative minor** The minor key sharing the same notes as a given major key.

**Pentatonic** A five-note scale in which nothing sounds wrong.

**Monophonic / polyphonic** One note at a time versus several. Basses should be monophonic.

**Camelot wheel** A relabelling of the circle of fifths used by DJs for harmonic mixing.

## Synthesis

**Oscillator** The part that generates the raw tone.

**Waveform** The oscillator's shape: sine, triangle, square, sawtooth, noise.

**Subtractive synthesis** Starting with a rich waveform and filtering harmonics away. The
dominant method.

**Additive synthesis** Building a sound by summing individual sine partials.

**FM synthesis** Using one oscillator to modulate another's frequency at audio rate.

**Wavetable synthesis** Scanning through stored waveforms so the timbre morphs over time.

**Filter** Removes frequencies above or below a cutoff.

**Cutoff** The filter's corner frequency.

**Resonance** A boost right at the cutoff. High resonance is the acid sound.

**Slope** How steeply a filter cuts, in dB per octave. 12, 18 and 24 are common.

**Envelope / ADSR** Attack, Decay, Sustain, Release. How a parameter changes over one note.
Sustain is a level; the other three are times.

**LFO** A low frequency oscillator used as a controller rather than a sound source.

**Modulation** Any parameter controlling another parameter.

**Portamento / glide / slide** Sliding continuously from one pitch to the next.

**Unison / detune** Stacking several slightly out-of-tune copies of an oscillator for
thickness. The supersaw.

**Patch / preset** A saved synth setting.

**Polyphony** How many notes a synth can play at once.

## Sampling

**One-shot** A single sound, triggered whole.

**Sample pack** A commercial collection of one-shots and loops.

**Chop** Cutting a sample into pieces and resequencing them.

**Slicing** Automatically dividing a loop at its transients.

**Time stretch** Changing length without changing pitch.

**Pitch shift** Changing pitch without changing length.

**Vocal chop** A short vocal fragment played as an instrument.

**Break** A drum-only section of an old record, sampled. The Amen break is the famous one.

**Clearance** Legal permission to use a sample from a copyrighted recording.

**Royalty free** Sample material licensed for use in your own productions.

## Mixing and mastering

**Gain staging** Setting sensible levels through the whole signal chain.

**dBFS** Decibels relative to full scale. 0 dBFS is the digital ceiling.

**Headroom** How much space is left before clipping.

**Clipping** Exceeding the ceiling, producing distortion.

**EQ** Equalisation. Boosting or cutting specific frequency ranges.

**Highpass / lowpass** Filters removing lows or highs respectively.

**Compression** Reducing dynamic range so the whole thing can be louder.

**Threshold, ratio, attack, release** The four main compressor controls.

**Sidechain** Using one signal to control a processor on another. Usually the kick ducking
the bass.

**Limiter** A compressor with a very high ratio, used to catch peaks.

**Send / return / bus** Routing a copy of several channels to a shared effect.

**Dry / wet** Unprocessed versus processed signal.

**Pan** Position in the stereo field.

**Mono compatibility** Whether the mix survives being summed to a single channel.

**Phase** The relative timing of waveforms. Misaligned phase causes cancellation.

**LUFS** The standard perceptual loudness measurement.

**True peak (dBTP)** Peak level accounting for what happens between samples. Keep at or
below −1.

**Loudness war** The long-running trend of mastering everything as loud as possible.

**Mastering** The final polishing and loudness stage.

**Reference track** A commercial release you A/B against while mixing.

## Software and hardware

**DAW** Digital audio workstation. FL Studio, Ableton Live, Logic, Bitwig.

**Plugin** Software instrument or effect loaded inside a DAW.

**VST / AU / CLAP** Plugin formats. VST is the Windows standard.

**MIDI** A protocol for note and control data. Not audio.

**Piano roll** The grid editor where MIDI notes are drawn.

**Step sequencer** A grid of on/off buttons for programming drums.

**Automation** Recorded parameter changes over time.

**ASIO** The low-latency Windows audio driver standard. Required for usable performance.

**Buffer size** How much audio is processed at once. Smaller means lower latency and higher
CPU load.

**Latency** The delay between an action and hearing the result.

**Bounce / render / export** Writing the project out as an audio file.

**Freeze** Rendering a track to audio temporarily to save CPU.

**Controller** A hardware device sending MIDI to your DAW.

**Audio interface** External hardware for converting audio in and out of the computer.

## Machines you will hear named

**TR-909** Roland drum machine, 1983. The house and techno standard.

**TR-808** Roland drum machine, 1980. Deep booming kick, cowbell. Hip-hop and electro.

**TR-707** Roland drum machine, 1985. Cheaper, drier, all over early Chicago records.

**TB-303** Roland bass synth, 1981. A commercial failure that invented acid house.

**Juno-60 / Juno-106** Roland polysynths with a famous chorus. The deep house pad sound.

**SP-1200 / MPC** Samplers. The foundation of hip-hop and a lot of house.

**Technics SL-1200** The DJ turntable.

**CDJ** Pioneer's standard club media player.

## Scene and culture

**House** The genre, named after the Warehouse in Chicago.

**Jack** To dance to house. Also a jack track: a stripped, hypnotic, drum-led record.

**Resident** A DJ with a regular slot at one club.

**Set** A DJ's performance.

**Beatmatching** Aligning the tempo and phase of two records.

**Mixing in key** Selecting records with compatible keys.

**Warm-up** The early part of the night, played slower and deeper. A specialised skill.

**Peak time** The busiest, hardest part of the night.

**White label / promo** A limited pressing without full artwork, usually for DJs.

**Crate digging** Searching for records in shops.

**Rave** An event, historically illegal or unlicensed.

**Bootleg / mashup** An unofficial edit combining or reworking existing records.

**Four to the floor** See four on the floor. Same thing, said differently, by everyone.
