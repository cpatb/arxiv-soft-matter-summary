# Drills

Short, repeatable exercises. Each has a time box. Do one at the start of a session as a
warm-up, or use them when you are stuck.

They are grouped by what they train.

## Ears

**Clap the offbeat. 15 minutes.**
Put on a house record. Clap on the "&" between the kicks, not on the kick. Do it for three
full tracks without losing it. Harder than it sounds, and it rewires how you hear the genre.
Progress: do it while walking, or while doing something else.

**Count the phrase. 20 minutes.**
Play a track and count bars out loud in groups of eight. Write down the bar number of every
change: when the bass enters, when the drums drop out, when the drop lands. Check whether
they are all multiples of 8. They will be.

**Name the tempo. 10 minutes.**
Play a track and guess the BPM before checking. Tap along and count beats for 15 seconds,
multiply by 4. Do this ten times. Within a week you will be within a few BPM by feel.

**Identify the drum machine. 10 minutes.**
Play five house or techno records and decide whether the kick is a 909, an 808 or something
sampled. Then load the samples in FL and compare. The 909 click and the 808 sustain become
obvious quickly.

**Reference A/B. 60 minutes.**
Load a commercial track into your project alongside your own. Turn the reference down to
match your level. Switch between them every ten seconds while you mix. Do this for a full
hour. It is the fastest mixing feedback available and it costs nothing.

## Rhythm

**Transcribe a drum pattern. 60 minutes.**
Pick a track. Rebuild its drum pattern in FL by ear, one element at a time: kick first, then
clap, then hats, then percussion. Use only the bundled 909 samples.

This is the highest-value exercise in the folder. Do it once a week for a month and your
drum programming will be unrecognisable.

**Five loops, five tempos. 45 minutes.**
Make five different eight-bar drum loops at 118, 122, 124, 126 and 128 BPM. Notice how the
tempo changes what patterns feel right. Fast tempos want simpler patterns.

**Swing sweep. 10 minutes.**
Take one loop. Set swing to 0%, then 10%, 20%, 30%, 40%. Listen at each. Find where it stops
feeling good. Now you know your own preference and you will set it deliberately instead of
leaving it at zero.

**Velocity only. 20 minutes.**
Take a flat sixteenth-note hi-hat pattern where every hit is identical. Change nothing except
velocity, using the graph editor. Make it groove. No new notes allowed.

**Three against four. 15 minutes.**
Put a percussion sound on every third sixteenth. Let it run for eight bars and hear it cycle
against the bar. Then find a way to make it resolve.

## Harmony

**Two chords, ten ways. 30 minutes.**
Take Am7 and Dm7. Write ten different versions: different voicings, different inversions,
different rhythms, stabs versus pads, different octaves. Same two chords throughout.

**Voice leading. 20 minutes.**
Write a four-chord progression twice. Once with every chord in root position, jumping around.
Once with proper voice leading, moving each note as little as possible. Listen to both. The
difference is larger than you expect.

**Bassline variations. 30 minutes.**
One chord loop, five basslines: root on offbeats, octave bounce, a line using the fifth and
minor seventh, one that plays only on beat 1, and one that syncopates against the kick.

**Transpose. 15 minutes.**
Take a finished chord loop in A minor. Move it to F minor, then C minor, then G minor,
without changing the shape. Confirms you understand that the pattern is what matters, not
the specific notes.

**Steal a progression. 30 minutes.**
Find the chords of a deep house record by ear. Play the bass note first to find the root,
then work out whether the chord above it is major or minor, then whether it has a seventh.
Slow work at first. Gets much faster.

## Sound design

**The 3x Osc hour. 60 minutes.**
The six-step exercise at the end of `THEORY/05_sound_design.md`. Do it once, properly, with
nothing else open.

**One synth, three sounds. 45 minutes.**
Pick one synth. Make a bass, a pad and a pluck from the initialised patch. No presets.

**Acid line. 45 minutes.**
Transistor Bass, sixteenth-note pattern, high resonance. Automate the cutoff across sixteen
bars. Add slide and accent to individual notes. This is a Chicago-specific skill and worth
its own session.

**Recreate a sound. 60 minutes.**
Pick one sound from a record you like. Try to build it from scratch. You will fail, and the
failure tells you exactly which part of synthesis you do not understand yet.

## Arrangement

**Subtract. 45 minutes.**
Take a full eight-bar loop with six or more elements. Copy it across 64 bars. Now only
delete. Build an intro, a breakdown and a build purely by removing things. Add nothing.

**The build. 60 minutes.**
Take any loop. Spend a full hour on just the sixteen bars before the drop. Riser, filter
automation, hat roll accelerating from 1/8 to 1/32, silence in the last half bar, crash on
the downbeat. One hour on sixteen bars.

**Eight variations. 45 minutes.**
Take one eight-bar loop. Make eight versions, each with exactly one thing different. Then
arrange them in sequence. You now have 64 bars that develop.

**Copy an arrangement. 45 minutes.**
Take a record you like. Write down its full structure with bar numbers. Then build your own
track into exactly that structure using your own sounds.

## Finishing

**The three hour track. 3 hours.**
Start from empty. Export something at the three hour mark, whatever state it is in. One
synth, four drum sounds, no preset browsing.

Removes the option of perfectionism, which is the actual obstacle for most people.

**The one-loop track. 90 minutes.**
Make a single two-bar loop. Build an entire five-minute track using only that loop plus
filtering, muting and automation. No new musical material allowed.

Teaches the thing that separates dance music from other genres: how much you can get from
variation alone.

**Finish an abandoned project. 2 hours.**
Open something you gave up on. Do not improve it. Just arrange it, mix it roughly, and export
it. You have two of these from May already.

**The deadline. 1 week.**
Pick a track. Set a date. On that date it gets exported and filed regardless of state. Repeat
until finishing stops feeling like a decision.

## A note on how to use these

Do not do them all. Pick the one that addresses whatever is currently annoying you about your
tracks.

If you do not know what is annoying you, do the reference A/B for an hour. It will tell you.
