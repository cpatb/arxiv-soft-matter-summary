# Polydisperse jamming in a drop-like boundary: specification

## 0. What this is, in one paragraph

A hydrodynamics-free discrete-element toy model of a dense sphere packing held in a
**drop-like (non-rigid) confining boundary** and driven into a wall. It asks which
grain-scale ingredient decides whether the assembly behaves as a **non-inertial jammed solid
that rebounds** or as an **inertial-modulated dilatant hybrid**, and specifically whether the
**width of the particle size distribution** can move that boundary at fixed mean size, which is
what the author has observed on the bench and what no published model addresses.

It is a *toy* model. It does not compute a rheology, it does not resolve the interstitial
fluid, and it is not a competitor to LF-DEM. Its output is a **phase boundary and a structural
account of it**.

---

## 1. Why this is worth building

### 1.1 The observation that motivates it

The author reports, from bench work: **at similar particle size, raising the coefficient of
variation `CV` of the size distribution moves the impacting drop from purely dilatant to
spreading and inertial-dominated for some time.** The samples were matched on the **number
mean**, to within roughly 100 nm.

⚠ **Provenance.** This dataset is not on disk. A search of `Desktop\Projects`,
`Desktop\Papers` and the Obsidian `Status/` tree on 2026-08-31 for polydispersity /
size-distribution / Sauter / `d32` / lognormal found no drop-impact CV data. Every `CV` value
used here is a **bench-note figure, unverified against raw distributions**. Nothing in this
spec may be quoted as agreeing with experiment until the distributions are produced.

### 1.2 The null that had to die first, and how it died

Matching on the *number* mean does not match two samples on the moment the physics reads.
Carman–Kozeny permeability uses the **Sauter mean** `d₃₂ = ⟨d³⟩/⟨d²⟩`, and for a lognormal at
fixed number mean:

```
d32/d10 = (1 + CV²)²          kappa_ratio = (1 + CV²)⁴
```

(analytic; reproduced to 4 decimal places by a 2×10⁷-draw Monte-Carlo, drifting to 3.957 vs
4.000 at `CV = 1` as the third-moment tail converges slowly.)

So raising `CV` raises permeability, and therefore raises the lab's own inertial–dilatant
number `ID ∝ κ ∝ d₃₂²`, **in exactly the direction observed, with no packing physics at all**.
That is mechanism **M1**, and it had to be excluded before a grain-scale model could be
justified.

It was excluded by running the lab's own `impact_stress/inertial_dilatant.py` (`ID()`, [M26]
Eq. 6 verbatim) with `d₃₂` substituted for the Carman–Kozeny diameter, at `φ = 0.44`,
`φ_c = 0.42`, `U₀ = 3 m/s`, `D₀ = 2.5 mm`, `ρ = 1440 kg/m³`:

```
 d10[um]    CV  d32/d10  d32[um]          ID  ID/ID(CV=0)  lamD/r_throat
    0.80  0.00   1.0000    0.800   7.194e-05        1.000          0.484
    0.80  0.30   1.1881    0.950   1.015e-04        1.412          0.407
    0.80  0.80   2.6896    2.152   5.204e-04        7.234          0.180
    1.50  0.00   1.0000    1.500   2.529e-04        1.000          0.258
    1.50  0.30   1.1881    1.782   3.570e-04        1.412          0.217
    1.50  0.80   2.6896    4.034   1.830e-03        7.234          0.096
cornstarch 10 um, same phi/phi_c:  ID = 1.124e-02
```

**The nominal regime boundary is `ID = 1`.** The entire `CV` range sits three to four decades
below it, before and after. A 2.4× or even 7.2× multiplier cannot cross a boundary four
decades away. **The permeability channel cannot produce a transition here, so a structural
explanation is required.**

⚠ Two caveats bound that exclusion and both are live:

1. `ID`'s boundary calibration is a known defect, Eq. 6 returns `6.6e-03` at the Regime-2/3
   transition the paper itself draws, not 1. If `6.6e-03` is the true boundary, `d₁₀ = 1.5 µm`
   at `CV = 0.8` is within **3.6×** and M1 is **not** excluded at that corner.
2. `λ_D/r_throat` = 0.10–0.48 at the 30 nm low-salt bracket, so Carman–Kozeny is marginal for
   these particles regardless (`impact_stress/PORE_PHYSICS.md` §5.2).

Both point at one action outside this project: **get the `ID` boundary calibration settled by a
co-author.** Driscoll is local and is on the paper.

### 1.3 What the field says is open

`Desktop\Papers\STATE_OF_THE_FIELD\OPEN_PROBLEMS_MASTER.md` §3 lists as **contested**:

> "Intrinsic bulk transition against boundary-mediated dilatancy, unresolved *for a free
> surface*, where no rigid wall supplies confinement."

A DEM packing in a drop-like boundary with **no rigid wall** is a direct attack on exactly
that. The confinement is the physics here, not a modelling detail. Checked against §7 (the
graveyard) and §8 (crowded zones): **this is not there.** The nearest live entry is **A5**
(particle *aspect ratio* and the impact jamming onset), which is a shape axis, not a width
axis, and is complementary.

⚠ **NOVELTY CLAIM NARROWED 2026-08-31, see `LITERATURE_2026-08-31.md`.** The check above
was made against a **drop-impact** slate, which is silent on suspension rheology, and the
rheology half of this question is actively worked:

- **Singh, Tyagi & Santra, arXiv:2510.16464 (Oct 2025)** do rigidity/percolation analysis of
  polydisperse shear-thickening suspensions in 2D LF-DEM, and find `φ_c` and `φ_J^μ` vary
  **non-monotonically** with polydispersity and **depend on particle stiffness**.
- **Guy, Ness, Hermes, Sawiak, Sun & Poon, arXiv:1901.02066 (2019)** use bidispersity as a
  deliberate probe of Wyart–Cates.
- The bidisperse-DST line reports that **raising the size ratio weakens thickening, DST → CST**
  *which is the direction of the author's bench observation*. So "polydispersity weakens
  thickening" is **not** a novel claim and must not be presented as one.

**What survives as open:** the free-surface/impact geometry (Phase 4); whether `φ_J^μ` is
non-monotonic in polydispersity in **3D** rather than 2D; separating polydispersity from
**skewness** for *frictional* `φ_J`; and the moment-matching result itself.

**Anchor A5 is demoted accordingly**, monotonic `φ_RCP` rise is asserted only for the
frictionless case, and for `φ_J^μ` the sweep must MEASURE the ordering rather than grade
against an assumed direction.

## 2. Nondimensionalisation, with numbers for the actual experiment

Done before any code, per the lab's standing rule.

### 2.1 Confinement: Weber number

For a drop of diameter `D₀`, the Laplace pressure is `P_L = 4γ/D₀`. The ratio of impact
inertia to capillary confinement is

```
Lambda = rho U0^2 / (4 gamma / D0) = We/4,    We = rho U0^2 D0 / gamma
```

| `U₀` [m/s] | `We` | `Λ = We/4` |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 50.0 | 12.5 |
| 2.0 | 200.0 | 50.0 |
| 3.0 | 450.0 | 112.5 |
| 4.0 | 800.0 | 200.0 |

**`Λ` = 12.5–200.** Surface tension is 1–2 orders of magnitude too weak to confine the grains
against the impact. **During impact the drop-like boundary is nearly free.** This is a result,
not a nuisance: it is precisely the situation the field map calls unresolved, and it means the
confinement that produces dilatancy in a rheometer is *absent here*. The model must therefore
supply confinement from the grains' own inertia and the wall, not from the skin.

### 2.2 Regime: the granular inertial number

`I = γ̇ d / sqrt(P/ρ_p)` is the standard quasi-static (`I ≪ 1`) vs inertial (`I ≳ 0.1`)
discriminator in granular flow, and it maps directly onto the author's own words,
"non-inertial ball" vs "inertial-modulated hybrid". With `P ~ ρ_susp U₀²` at `U₀ = 3 m/s`:

| material | `d` [µm] | `I` at `γ̇ = U₀/D₀` | `I` at `γ̇ = 10⁴ s⁻¹` | `I` at `γ̇ = 10⁵ s⁻¹` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| silica 0.8 µm | 0.80 | 3.96e-04 | 3.30e-03 | 3.30e-02 |
| silica 1.5 µm | 1.50 | 7.42e-04 | 6.18e-03 | 6.18e-02 |
| cornstarch 10 µm | 10.0 | 4.15e-03 | 3.46e-02 | **3.46e-01** |

⚠ **`I` is gauge-dependent and the gauge is unsettled.** On the bulk rate `U₀/D₀` everything is
quasi-static and nothing separates. On the film rate `~10⁵ s⁻¹` cornstarch reaches `I = 0.35`
, genuinely inertial, while silica stays at 0.03–0.06. **The regime separation the author
observes exists only if the relevant rate is the film rate, not the bulk rate.** This is the
same open question the lab already carries as "which Re at 10⁴ s⁻¹", and this project inherits
it rather than solving it. Every `I` quoted must name its `γ̇`.

**And `I` contains `d`, so it inherits the same moment problem as `κ`.** Which moment of the
distribution enters `I` for a polydisperse pack is not settled in the literature. Measuring
that is a genuine output of this work (see §5, Phase 3).

### 2.3 Contact stiffness: the rigid-grain requirement

Hertzian overlap `δ/d ~ (ρU₀²/E*)^(2/5)`; grains must be effectively rigid:

| `E*` [Pa] | `δ/d` |
|---|---|
| 1e7 | 6.999e-02 |
| 1e8 | 2.786e-02 |
| 1e9 | 1.109e-02 |
| 7e10 (silica glass) | **2.028e-03** |

Real silica gives `δ/d ≈ 2e-3`, comfortably rigid. **DEM at `E* = 7e10` is prohibitively stiff
for the timestep**, so runs use a reduced `E*` and the *stiffness must be swept*: `1e8`, `1e9`,
`1e10`, and every conclusion graded at each. A conclusion that moves with `E*` is a
softness artifact. This follows the lab's `MUMAX`-box precedent, a regularisation-like knob
gets bracketed both ways, not tested at two adjacent corners.

### 2.4 The parameter set

| symbol | meaning | values |
|---|---|---|
| `CV` | size-distribution width (lognormal) | 0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.8 |
| `φ` | volume fraction | swept to locate `φ_J` |
| `μ_p` | interparticle friction | 0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0 |
| `E*` | contact stiffness | 1e8, 1e9, 1e10 |
| `Λ` | capillary confinement, `We/4` | 12.5, 50, 200, plus `∞` (free) and 0 (rigid wall) |
| `N` | particle count | 2000 production, 500 cheap, 8000 convergence |

## 3. The three mechanisms, and what each phase decides

| | mechanism | status | decided by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M1** | permeability via `d₃₂` | ☠ **excluded** except the top corner (§1.2) | done, desk |
| **M2** | distance to jamming: broader packs denser, `φ_J` rises, `φ/φ_J` falls | open | **Phase 2** |
| **M3** | force-network structure: at matched `φ/φ_J` *and* matched `d₃₂`, small grains are rattlers and the load-bearing backbone is a subset | open | **Phase 3** |

**M3 is the only one a hydrodynamics-free model is the right instrument for, and it is the
interesting result.** M2 is largely published packing physics and is included to be subtracted.

## 5. Phases

### Phase 1: Machinery and anchors

Build packing generation (lognormal PSD at target `d₁₀`, `CV`), isotropic compression, and the
measurement layer (`Z`, rattler fraction, force network, fabric tensor, stress).

**Nothing proceeds until Phase 1's anchors pass.** See §6.

### Phase 2: `φ_J(CV)`: settles M2

Locate the jamming point by compression/decompression to the pressure→0 limit, at each `CV`
and each `μ_p`. Report `φ_J(CV, μ_p)` with error bars over ≥5 seeds.

**The question it answers:** at the `CV` values the author used, how much does `φ/φ_J` move,
and is that enough to move a regime given `η ∝ (φ_J − φ)⁻²`?

**Pre-registered discriminator, written before the runs:** M2 is *sufficient* if the measured
`Δ(φ/φ_J)` between the author's low-`CV` and high-`CV` samples changes `(φ_J − φ)⁻²` by more
than **one decade**. Below one decade, M2 is a contributing shift but not the explanation, and
Phase 3 carries the result. This threshold is fixed now and will not be moved; if it turns out
to be the wrong *test* rather than a failed one, the replacement must be **stricter**, and both
numbers stay printed.

### Phase 3: Structure at matched `φ/φ_J` and matched `d₃₂`: isolates M3

The core of the project. Hold `φ/φ_J` **and** `d₃₂` fixed, vary `CV`, and measure what is left:

- rattler fraction, and the size distribution *of the rattlers* against that of all grains
- load-bearing backbone fraction, and `Z` of the backbone alone vs `Z` of all grains
- fabric anisotropy of the contact and force networks
- the contact-force PDF, and specifically its exponential tail
- force-chain length distribution and the spanning-cluster geometry
- **which moment of the PSD makes `I` collapse the regimes**, the §2.2 question, and a
  genuine output

**Pre-registered:** a residual `CV` effect after both matchings is M3. If the residual is
smaller than the seed-to-seed scatter, **M3 is refuted and the honest answer is that the
observation is not structural**, that result gets written up as clearly as a positive one.

### Phase 4: The drop-like boundary and impact

Three boundary conditions on the same packings, which is the direct attack on the contested
free-surface question:

- **free**, no confinement at all
- **capillary**, Laplace pressure `4γ/D₀` applied inward on surface grains, at `Λ` = 12.5, 50, 200
- **rigid wall**, the rheometer-like limit

Drive into a rigid wall at `U₀`. Measure network formation vs time, whether a system-spanning
frictional cluster forms before the kinetic energy is spent, and the coefficient of restitution.

⚠ **Scope guard from §2.1:** at `Λ` = 12.5–200 the capillary case is expected to sit close to
the free case. If it does, **that is the finding**, capillary confinement is irrelevant during
impact, and it must be reported rather than tuned away.

### Phase 5: Analysis, figures, documentation

Figures in the lab house style (minimal text on axes, explanation in a sibling `.md`, linear
axes start at zero). One script per figure, byte-identical regeneration checked. Status card
and `lessons.md` updated.

## 7. What would make this project wrong

Stated in advance so it can be checked rather than rationalised.

1. **`I` never separates the regimes at any defensible `γ̇`.** Then the "inertial vs
   non-inertial" framing is wrong and the observation needs a different organising variable.
2. **The residual `CV` effect at matched `φ/φ_J` and `d₃₂` is inside seed scatter.** M3
   refuted; the observation is not structural.
3. **Conclusions move with `E*` across the swept decades.** Then they are softness artifacts,
   as `MUMAX` was for the Basilisk arm.
4. **The capillary boundary is indistinguishable from the free boundary at all `Λ`.** Then the
   drop-like boundary adds nothing over a free packing and the model should say so.
5. **The author's raw distributions, when produced, show the samples were not matched on the
   number mean after all.** Then §1.2 has to be re-run and M1 may revive.

Any of 1–4 is a publishable negative. None of them is a reason to adjust a parameter.

## 8. Standing landmines this project must not step on

- 🔴 **`si_fit` is withdrawn**, `σ* = 44.2 Pa`, `φ_m = 0.5176`, `φ_0 = 0.70` were fitted to
  rheometry retracted 2026-07-29, preset withdrawn 2026-08-26, still shipped by
  `impact_stress/materials.py:lab_silica()`. **Do not calibrate anything here on it.**
- ⚠ **`ID` Eq. 6 does not evaluate to 1 at its own boundary** (§1.2). Use `ID` for ordering.
- ⚠ **`ρU₀²` is the wrong stress gauge for thickening** by ~2 decades; dilatancy responds to
  *shear* stress. Name the gauge before quoting anything as "above `σ*`".
- ⚠ **`φ_m` is not identifiable from a steady flow curve**, a band 6× to 24× the tolerance `φ`
  is held to. This is the argument for computing `φ_J` from the packing here rather than
  importing it.
- ⚠ **Wyart–Cates is exactly scale-covariant in `(φ, φ_m, φ_0)`**, sweeping `φ_0` re-measures
  algebra.
- ⚠ **Cornstarch is outside the Wyart–Cates repulsive class** (Gauthier, Ovarlez & Colin,
  *JCIS* 2023), adhesive, irregular, porous, swelling. **Do not model silica and cornstarch
  with one interaction law and attribute the difference to polydispersity.**
- ⚠ **The rebound observable may be a fracture threshold**, not a jamming one: Slutzky,
  Pelosse, van der Naald & Jaeger, *PRE* **113**, 025410 (2026), brittle fracture above
  `σ_N ≳ 0.1 MPa`, `φ ≳ 0.59`, self-healing, with particle diameter *not* controlling failure.
- ⚠ **LF-DEM is crowded** (Seto, Mari, Singh, Ness). This is not a rheology solver and must not
  become one.
- ⚠ **Cornstarch numbers are not quotable beside silica ones** until D4, the cornstarch
  common-axis calibration, is done.

## 11. Changes made during execution, and why

Recorded as they happened, so the spec is not quietly rewritten to match what was built.

**11.1 Yade is installed userspace, not by `apt`.** `sudo` on this WSL requires a
password unavailable in an autonomous session. Yade `2026.1.0-3build1` was installed by
extracting its 101-package `.deb` dependency closure into `~/opt/yade`. Three things had
to be right, each of which silently broke the import: `PYTHONPATH` points at
`.../yade-double/py`, not `dist-packages`; `flatlib` holds **only** the sonames
`ldconfig -p` cannot already resolve (shadowing the system's self-consistent Qt5 5.15.18
set gave `libQt5OpenGL.so.5: undefined symbol QOpenGLFunctions_1_1::versionProfile`); and
`IPython` is the only missing Python dependency, so it must be installed **alone**:
installing the full stack pulled a numpy 2.5.2 that conflicted with the system numba.
Environment in `scripts/env.sh`. **Fallback ladder in §4 was not needed.**

**11.2 The model runs in dimensionless units.** At the physical scale (d10 = 2 µm,
E = 1e8 Pa) `PWaveTimeStep` gives dt ≈ 1.4e-9 s and one N = 300 cell took over 3 minutes
of 4-thread wall clock, a 150-cell sweep measured in days. Packing observables are
invariant under a change of units, so the model uses `d10 = 1, rho = 1, E = 1` and
controls the reduced pressure `p_hat = p/E` directly. This also turns §2.3's stiffness
sweep into a one-line sweep and removes the temptation to quote a Young's modulus in Pa
as if it were physical, when silica's real 7e10 Pa is unreachable by DEM anyway.

**11.3 The rattler threshold is a swept definition, not a constant.** ⚠ **This changed
an answer.** Iteratively removing grains with fewer than `k` contacts is k-core
decomposition. For a frictional pack at mean Z = 4.86 the **k = 4 core is empty**, the
entire pack reads as rattlers, while a single non-iterated pass removes 15% of it. The
k-core percolation threshold for a random graph at k = 4 sits near mean degree 5.1, so a
marginal frictional pack is on the wrong side of it. The frictionless anchor cell is not
affected (Z = 6.92, rattler fraction 0.001 at every k). So `k` ∈ {2, 3, 4} are all
recorded, `k = 2` (true rattlers, no friction-dependent stability assumption) is primary,
and any conclusion must be graded at all three.

**11.4 N is bounded below by the periodic collider, and the bound binds at the final
density.** Yade needs cell edge > 2.1 × `maxBodySpan`, and the cell *shrinks* under
compression, so sizing only the insertion cell is not enough (N = 200, CV = 0.5 passed at
φ = 0.30 and died on the way to 0.64). `build_and_compress` now checks at
`PHI_DENSE_BOUND = 0.72` and raises with the minimum viable N. At CV = 0.5 with
`truncate = (0.2, 5.0)` that is **N ≥ 1299**, so production runs at N = 1500.

**11.5 The CV ladder stops at 0.5, and everything is reported by REALISED CV.**
Truncation needed to keep the largest grain inside the cell distorts the high-CV end
badly: at a nominal CV = 0.8 the realised CV is 0.72 and the realised `d32` is 21% below
analytic. A nominal 0.8 would not be a 0.8, so the ladder is 0 to 0.5 (realised 0.496 at
the top, `d32` within 1.5%).

**11.6 The production grid was cut from 270 to 144 cells on a measured rate.** The sweep
ran at 0.43 cells/min across 8 workers, 10.5 h for the original grid. Dropped: the
`mu_p = 1.0` column, and the `p_hat = 3e-3` rung, which `analyse_phase2.P_HAT_FIT_MAX`
already excluded from the extrapolation. All four surviving rungs are inside the fit
window, which the free-exponent check needs. The A4 anchor is still testable on
`mu_p` = 0.0 vs 0.3.

**11.7 Phase 3 needs no new simulations, and the reason sharpens M1 vs M3.** A DEM
packing of hard spheres is scale-invariant, so at fixed distribution shape, matching on
`d10` and matching on `d32` are the **same experiment**. M1 and M3 are separated by
construction rather than by matching: M1 lives in the interstitial fluid where an
absolute length enters via `kappa ~ d32^2`, and the packing cannot see an absolute length
at all. **Everything the DEM reports is by definition free of M1.** The remaining
matching condition is mechanical (equal `p_hat`, equal `mu_p`), which the Phase-2 sweep
already spans.

**11.8 Phase 4 aggregates are CARVED from a compressed periodic pack.** The first version
inserted grains into a sphere and compacted them with a central pull applied from Python
between `O.run()` calls. It exploded, COR came back as 2.6e37, for two reasons worth
recording: `ForceResetter` zeroes `O.forces` at the start of every timestep, so a force
added from Python before `O.run(400)` acts for one step in 400 (body forces must come
from a `PyRunner` with `iterPeriod=1`); and random insertion leaves overlaps that the
contact law converts straight into kinetic energy. Carving reuses the validated Phase-2
compression instead. The parent pressure was then lowered to `p_hat = 1e-4`, because at
1e-3 the stored elastic energy disintegrated the free aggregate before it reached the
wall (`Z_initial` = 0.00), and Z is now read **as carved**, before any settling.

**11.9 A limitation that bounds every Phase-4 claim.** The granular inertial number
reduces to `I ~ d/D_agg`, independent of `U0`. An N ~ 1000 aggregate has `D_agg ~ 12 d`,
so `I ~ 0.08`, while a real 2.5 mm drop of 1 µm grains has `d/D0 = 4e-4`. **The model is
~200× more inertial per grain than the experiment and no affordable N fixes that.** Phase
4 compares boundary conditions and CV at matched `I`; it does not predict the
experiment's regime boundary, and no number from it may be quoted as one.
