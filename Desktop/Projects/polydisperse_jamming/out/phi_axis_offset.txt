=== IS THE LAB'S SILICA ABOVE OR BELOW ITS CRITICAL STATE? ===
  lab working point                       phi   = 0.4400
  MEASURED critical state (mu_p=0.3, CV=0) phi_c = 0.5695 +/- 0.0030
  phi - phi_c                                    = -0.1295

  On its face the lab sits 0.13 BELOW the critical state, i.e. permanently
  CONTRACTIVE -- which would mean the solid-like branch the project exists to
  explain cannot happen at all. Something in that comparison is wrong, and this
  module bounds which part.

=== HOW FAR CAN FRICTION PUSH phi_c DOWN? (the only knob a hard-sphere model has)
  Isotropic phi_J against mu_p, from the project's committed A4 ladder:
    mu_p     phi_J       sd  drop from mu=0
    0.00    0.6332   0.0002         +0.0000
    0.30    0.5745   0.0006         -0.0586
    1.00    0.5590   0.0012         -0.0741
    3.00    0.5574   0.0017         -0.0757

  phi_J SATURATES at 0.5574: the step from mu_p = 1 to 3 is -0.0016, i.e. inside the seed scatter. More friction buys
  essentially nothing. So for FRICTIONAL HARD SPHERES there is a FLOOR.

  floor - lab phi = +0.1174
  ⇒ 🔑 NO amount of interparticle friction can bring a hard-sphere critical state
    down to phi = 0.44. The gap that friction cannot close is 0.117 in phi.

=== SO ONE OF THESE MUST BE TRUE, and they are testable against each other ===
  (1) THE LOCAL phi DURING IMPACT IS MUCH HIGHER THAN THE BULK 0.44.
      Reaching the measured phi_c needs a local densification of 1.294x,
      i.e. a volumetric compaction of 23 % at the
      contact. That is large but it is exactly the Status card's own
      next-action #2 ('measure the LOCAL phi reached during impact'), and this
      is the number that measurement has to beat.

  (2) THE MATERIAL IS NOT A FRICTIONAL HARD-SPHERE PACK. 800 nm silica at low
      salt is charge-stabilised: the particles never touch, and a repulsive
      network is mechanically rigid at far lower solid fractions than a hard
      sphere pack. This DEM has NO repulsion, so its phi_c is the wrong
      quantity for that material, and the floor above does not apply to it.

  (3) THE EXPERIMENT'S phi IS SYSTEMATICALLY LOW. The Status card already
      records three irreconcilable numbers on this axis (fronts at 0.42-0.45
      here, first solid patches at 0.47 in Shah et al., a fitted phi_M of
      0.5176). A controlled simulation now adds a FLOOR to that list rather
      than a fourth free number.

=== WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT DO TO THE MECHANISM ===
  DOES NOT TOUCH: the CV CONTRAST. Every claim in MECHANISM_2026-09-08.md is a
  DIFFERENCE at fixed protocol -- dphi_c/dCV = +0.125, the 43x collapse at
  matched phi -- and a common offset in the phi axis cancels out of all of them.

  DOES TOUCH: any statement about WHICH SIDE the lab's silica is on, including
  the critical width CV* and the regime map's assignment of the lab to R1. Those
  need phi_c for THIS material, which this DEM does not measure, and they should
  be read as conditional on resolution (1), (2) or (3).

  🔑 AND IT MAKES ONE CHEAP THING URGENT: option (2) is decidable in this same
  DEM by adding a repulsive contact law and re-running one ladder. That is a
  bigger change than a sweep but a much smaller one than a new experiment, and
  it would say whether a charge-stabilised pack has a critical state anywhere
  near 0.44.

=== CAN OPTION (2) ACTUALLY CLOSE THE GAP? -- an estimate, not a result ===
  Charge-stabilised particles never touch, so the pack jams when the EFFECTIVE
  spheres reach the hard-sphere critical state. For an exponentially screened
  repulsion the separation at which the repulsive force balances the confining
  one is h = lambda_D ln(sigma*/sigma), so

      phi_c_eff = phi_c_HS * [ d / (d + 2 h) ]^3

      salt  lam_D[nm]  sigma/sigma*   h [nm]   d_eff/d  phi_c_eff  vs lab phi
      0 mM         30         1e-02   138.16    1.3454     0.2339    DILATANT
      0 mM         30         1e-01    69.08    1.1727     0.3531    DILATANT
      1 mM         10         1e-02    46.05    1.1151     0.4107    DILATANT
      1 mM         10         1e-01    23.03    1.0576     0.4815 contractive
     10 mM          3         1e-02    13.82    1.0345     0.5143 contractive
     10 mM          3         1e-01     6.91    1.0173     0.5410 contractive
    100 mM          1         1e-02     4.61    1.0115     0.5503 contractive
    100 mM          1         1e-01     2.30    1.0058     0.5598 contractive

  The range spanned is 0.234 to 0.560, and the
  lab's phi = 0.44 falls INSIDE it. So the 0.117 gap that friction cannot
  close IS closable by the double layer, at plausible salt and stress. That is
  the quantitative content of option (2) and it is the main result of this
  section.

  ⚠⚠ WHAT THIS IS NOT. Composing this with the Wyart-Cates friction activation
  is NOT done here, and the naive composition points the WRONG WAY: using the
  FRICTIONLESS phi_c at low stress and the FRICTIONAL one at high stress makes
  phi_c_eff RISE with stress, i.e. the material would become MORE contractive as
  it is loaded harder -- the opposite of shear thickening. Either the composition
  is wrong or one of the two ingredients is being misapplied, and that is not
  settled here. Treat the table above as a REACHABILITY estimate -- 'the double
  layer is big enough to matter' -- and not as a value of phi_c for this
  material.

  🔑 The estimate does something else worth keeping: it makes phi_c a function of
  STRESS, which is precisely the critical-state LINE that poro_impact.py's
  docstring identifies as the closure the transient model is missing. The same
  gap shows up in two places, which is weak evidence that it is a real gap and
  not a bookkeeping error.
