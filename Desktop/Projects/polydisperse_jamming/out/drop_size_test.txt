=== THE DROP-SIZE TEST OF THE DRAINAGE LENGTH ===
  🔴 CALIBRATION, and the first version of this script got it wrong. Each reading
  is a DIFFERENT force ratio, so each has its OWN boundary value; applying (M)'s
  number to (B) and (L) made both look as though cornstarch were always inertial,
  which was an artefact of the mis-calibration and not a refutation of anything.
  Every reading is now pinned to the SAME observation -- [M26] see the transition
  at the top of their range, U0 = 7.2 m/s at D0 = 4.0 mm -- so all
  three agree there BY CONSTRUCTION and the test is entirely about how they MOVE.

  boundary value per reading: (M) 6.6748e-03   (B) 1.0680e+01   (L) 2.6699e-01

   D0 [mm]    U0* (M)    U0* (B)    U0* (L)    (M)/ref  (B)/ref  (L)/ref
      1.00       1.80      28.80       7.20      0.250    4.000    1.000
      2.00       3.60      14.40       7.20      0.500    2.000    1.000
      4.00       7.20       7.20       7.20      1.000    1.000    1.000
      6.00      10.80       4.80       7.20      1.500    0.667    1.000
      8.00      14.40       3.60       7.20      2.000    0.500    1.000

  🔑 THE THREE READINGS MOVE IN THREE DIRECTIONS. Halving the drop diameter from
  4 to 2 mm HALVES the boundary velocity under (M), DOUBLES it under (B), and
  leaves it unchanged under (L) -- a 4x separation between the extremes over one
  nozzle change. The SIGN alone decides it, so the test does not depend on the
  O(1) band thickness, on the ID prefactor, or on where the boundary sits.

  ⚠ (M) and (B) both predict the transition LEAVES the accessible range at one
  end of the ladder -- (M) at small D0, (B) at large -- so the ladder should be
  centred on 4 mm and kept inside about 2-6 mm for the transition to stay
  observable under every reading.

  A competing mechanism built on the suspension VISCOSITY (phi_m rising with CV,
  eta ~ (phi_m - phi)^-2) predicts NO boundary in (U0, D0) at all, so a null
  result is informative rather than a failed experiment.

=== IS IT RUNNABLE ON THE LAB'S OWN SILICA? NO -- and that is worth knowing ===
  silica, D0 = 2.5 mm: U0* = 278 m/s under reading (M) -- unreachable on any bench tower.
  So the drop-size test belongs on CORNSTARCH (or on a larger particle), where
  the boundary is already inside the accessible velocity range. That is a
  feature: it tests [M26]'s construction on [M26]'s own material, and it is the
  cheapest available attack on the drainage length.

  ⚠ WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS INVALID: if changing D0 also changes the impact regime
  in some other way that moves the observed transition -- Weber and Reynolds both
  scale with D0, so the SPREADING behaviour changes even with no rheology change.
  The observable must therefore be the REGIME (solid-like vs re-entrant liquid),
  not the spread factor, and the control is a Newtonian liquid of matched
  viscosity and density run over the same D0 ladder.
