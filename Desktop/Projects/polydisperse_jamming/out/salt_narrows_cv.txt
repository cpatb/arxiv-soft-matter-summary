  d_eff = d + 2h with the same h for every particle, so sigma is unchanged and
  the mean rises: CV_eff = CV * d10 / (d10 + 2h).
  d10 = 800 nm, h = lambda_D ln(sigma*/sigma)

=== ⚠ AND IT POINTS THE OPPOSITE WAY TO THE OTHER SALT EFFECT ===
  The same effective-diameter model has screening move phi_c^eff ITSELF from
  0.23 (0 mM) to 0.56 (100 mM) -- a LARGE effect that pushes the material toward
  the contractive side as salt is added. The narrowing effect above is a SMALL
  effect that makes the CV lever weaker as salt is REMOVED. On the salt axis the
  two are confounded, and separating them needs the CV ladder run AT TWO SALTS
  rather than a single salt series.

  🔑 That is a concrete experimental design: a 2 x 2 of (CV low/high) x (salt
  low/high). The interaction term is the narrowing prediction; the salt main
  effect is the phi_c^eff shift. Neither is identifiable from a one-dimensional
  ladder, which is what the lab currently has.

  ⚠⚠ PROVENANCE: all of this is a consequence of the effective-diameter ESTIMATE,
  which phi_axis_offset.py labels a reachability estimate and NOT a value of
  phi_c. It is a prediction OF THAT MODEL. The measured quantities it uses --
  dphi_c/dCV = 0.1247 and its 0.003 scatter -- are real.
