=== transfer_law.py -- does the polydispersity shift depend on WHERE the pack jams? ===
   The escape clause the whole programme rests on is that dphi_c/dCV transfers
   from a DEM at phi_c ~ 0.59 to a material at 0.40. This tests it.

=== LADDER HEALTH, before any arm is used ===
   phi_J_lin is a straight line through 4 pressure rungs; its se cannot see a
   ladder that is not a straight line. An arm with a NEGATIVE slope is
   inadmissible (phi_J falling as pressure rises), not merely noisy.

=== the candidate transfer laws, each fitted with ONE free constant ===
   dphi_J = K * f(phi_J). K is fitted by weighted least squares in every case,
   so the three models have the SAME number of parameters and their chi2 are
   directly comparable on 1 residual degree(s) of freedom.
   ⚠ 2 healthy arms and one fitted constant leaves 1 residual
     degree of freedom. This can REJECT a badly wrong form but it cannot
     separate two forms that differ by less than the error bars, and below
     it does not.

   model                          K        chi2(1 dof)   p       predictions
   ABSOLUTE     dphi = K           0.0601       1.19   0.275   +0.0601 +0.0601
   FREE-VOLUME  dphi = K(1-phi)    0.1507       1.61   0.205   +0.0554 +0.0641
   RELATIVE     dphi = K*phi       0.0995       7.02   0.008   +0.0629 +0.0572
   measured                                                       +0.0580 +0.0621
   -> best: ABSOLUTE (chi2 = 1.19)

=== the exponent, fitted rather than chosen: dphi_J = K (1 - phi_J)^n ===
   n = 0 is ABSOLUTE (the shift is a property of the size distribution alone);
   n = 1 is FREE-VOLUME. The RELATIVE law is n = -phi/(1-phi) ~ -1.4 here.
   ⚠ NOT FITTED: two parameters on 2 points is zero residual degrees of freedom, so the fit would
     be exact by construction and its 'error' meaningless. The bound below
     is taken from the CHI2 TABLE instead: which named forms survive.
   forms NOT rejected at p > 0.05: ['ABSOLUTE', 'FREE-VOLUME']
   -> the admissible exponent range is the span of the surviving named
      forms: n in [+0.00, +1.00]. That is what the lever is
      worth, and it is a great deal less than three healthy arms would be.
   admissible exponent range: n in [+0.00, +1.00]  -> free-volume (n = 1) is INSIDE, absolute (n = 0) is INSIDE

=== what the bound buys: carrying +0.053 to a material that jams lower ===
   The DEM measures the shift at phi_c = 0.591 (constant pressure, sheared).
   Transferring it to phi_c = X multiplies it by ((1-X)/(1-0.591))^n.
   phi_c   factor at n = 0   at n = +1.00 (2se hi)   at n = +0.00 (2se lo)   ->  dphi_c band
   0.50    1.000            1.223              1.000          [0.0533, 0.0652]
   0.44    1.000            1.370              1.000          [0.0533, 0.0730]
   0.40    1.000            1.467              1.000          [0.0533, 0.0782]
   0.37    1.000            1.541              1.000          [0.0533, 0.0822]
   -> the central (n = 0) transfer is the measured +0.0533 unchanged.

=== a second reading of the same question, from data taken for another reason ===
   phi_0 (frictionless) and phi_mu (frictional isotropic) are two jamming
   points 0.058 apart at CV 0, measured on the SAME seeds with the SAME
   protocol. If the shift tracked free volume, the LOWER one would shift more.
      mu_p 0.0: phi_J 0.6324, shift +0.0580 +- 0.0027
      mu_p 0.3: phi_J 0.5745, shift +0.0621 +- 0.0026
   free-volume would require +0.0580 +0.0672; measured +0.0580 +0.0621
   the free-volume prediction is off by 0.0 2.0 se.

=== WHAT THE 2026-09-13 REVIEW CHANGED, recorded rather than absorbed ===
   (1) The mu_p = 1.0 arm is REFUSED on ladder health, above. It was the arm
       that made the shift look NON-MONOTONIC in phi_J, and that shape was the
       ABSOLUTE law's whole chi2 advantage -- no single-sign power law can
       reproduce a rise then a fall. With it gone the lever is TWO points and
       0.058 wide, not three and 0.073, and 'non-monotonic' is withdrawn.
   (2) COLLINEARITY: friction moves the isostatic contact number Z as well as
       phi_J, and they move together across every arm here. So this test
       separates 'the shift tracks phi_J' from 'the shift does not', and CANNOT
       separate phi_J from Z. The claim 'a property of the size distribution
       rather than of the packing' is NOT established -- only the narrower
       'rather than of phi_J specifically'. The clean lever is PROTOCOL at
       fixed mu_p, which moves phi_J while isostaticity pins Z.
   (3) CHANNEL: the exponent is fitted on ISOTROPIC ladders and applied to the
       SHEARED critical state, and those differ by 2.8 se at the one friction
       where both exist (isotropic +0.0621 +- 0.0026 vs sheared +0.0533 +-
       0.0018). There is no lever at all on the sheared channel -- one point.
   (4) The chi2 table and the exponent bound answer DIFFERENT questions and
       neither may be quoted as the other. The table compares three named,
       motivated forms at equal parameter count; the bound is a bound.
   (5) The RCP polydispersity literature (Farr & Groot 2009 doi:10.1063/1.3276799; Desmond & Weeks 2014 doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.90.022204) is
       FRICTIONLESS BY CONSTRUCTION, so none of it can predict n for a test
       run across friction. Citing it as background for this hypothesis was a
       category error and is withdrawn. It does corroborate the SHAPE of the
       mu_p = 0 ladder qualitatively (convex, accelerating in CV), and this
       project's own secant slope steepens 0.051 -> 0.178 per unit CV over
       CV [0,0.1] -> [0.4,0.5], a 3.5x steepening, consistent with that.
       ⚠ No coefficient is quoted from either paper: the review's PDF read of
       Desmond & Weeks' c2 was ambiguous (0.857 vs 0.0857) and is [UNVERIFIED].

=== WHAT IS AND IS NOT ESTABLISHED ===
   ESTABLISHED: over the 0.073 in phi_J that friction spans, the CV 0 -> 0.5
     shift is constant within its own error. The escape clause the programme
     rests on is SUPPORTED over that range rather than merely assumed.
   NOT ESTABLISHED: anything at phi_c = 0.40. That is an extrapolation of
     0.19 in phi_J against a lever arm of 0.073 -- 2.6 lever arms -- and the
     bound above is what it is worth. Extending the lever is the next campaign,
     and friction cannot do it (phi_J saturates between mu_p 1 and 3).
   NOT TESTED: whether a pack whose phi_J is lowered by SHAPE rather than by
     friction behaves the same. Rolling resistance is the standard DEM proxy
     for non-sphericity, it reaches phi_J ~ 0.50, and phase6_cohesion.py
     already proves this codebase can drive CohFrictMat with momentRotationLaw.
