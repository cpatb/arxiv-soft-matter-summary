=== 5. THE BENCH STATEMENT ===
   At <k> = 2 (half the solid in multiplets), COMPACT aggregates (d_f = 3):
      Lambda = 1.0000 (no trapped fluid, exactly), G = 1.0311, Lambda/G = 0.9698
      -> aggregation moves the suspension AWAY from jamming by 3.0 %.
   The same <k> = 2 with OPEN aggregates (d_f = 1.8, the diffusion-limited value):
      Lambda = 1.9892, G = 1.0638, Lambda/G = 1.8700
      -> aggregation moves it TOWARD jamming by 87.0 %.

   SO THE SIGN OF THE AGGREGATION EFFECT IS SET BY THE AGGREGATE'S OPENNESS,
   NOT BY HOW MUCH IT HAS AGGREGATED. That is falsifiable on the bench: it says
   two suspensions at the SAME measured CV can sit on opposite sides of the
   jamming shift if their aggregates differ in compactness -- and that a CV
   measurement alone therefore cannot predict the rheology.

=== 6. WHY d_f* BARELY MOVES: a first-order expansion, and its check ===
   Near gamma = 1 (d_f = 3), with L1 = <k ln k>/<k>:
       Lambda(gamma) = 1 + (gamma-1) L1 + O((gamma-1)^2)
       G(gamma)      = G0 + (gamma-1) G0' + ...
   so the crossing Lambda = G sits at
       gamma* - 1  =  (G0 - 1) / (L1 - G0')   ~=  (G0 - 1) / L1.
   BOTH G0-1 and L1 grow with aggregation, so their RATIO is nearly invariant.
   That is the explanation for the flat d_f* column, and it is checkable:

      <k>    G0 - 1        L1   (G0-1)/L1  gamma*-1 (exact)  d_f* 1st order  d_f* exact
      1.1    0.0030    0.1306      0.0227            0.0239          2.9334      2.9300
      1.2    0.0061    0.2474      0.0246            0.0260          2.9279      2.9241
      1.5    0.0157    0.5371      0.0293            0.0310          2.9146      2.9098

   throughout'. THAT IS REFUTED BY THE TABLE ABOVE, which this file prints:
      <k>=1.1: +0.0034  <k>=1.2: +0.0038  <k>=1.5: +0.0049  <k>=2.0: +0.0058  <k>=3.0: +0.0011  <k>=5.0: -0.0003  <k>=8.0: -0.0003
   The first-order estimate is ABOVE the exact d_f* on 5 of 7
   rows, worst |deviation| 0.0058 (not 0.005).

   ABOVE is the direction the algebra predicts, and the earlier prose had the
   word wrong while its own mechanism sentence was right. Since
       gamma* - 1 = (G0-1)/(L1 - G0')   and   G0' > 0,
   dropping G0' makes the denominator LARGER, so gamma*_approx < gamma*_exact,
   and d_f = 3/gamma means d_f*_approx > d_f*_exact -- i.e. the first-order
   estimate sits CLOSER TO d_f = 3, which is ABOVE. The two rows that come out
   marginally below (-0.0003) are second order and are not explained by this
   argument; they are reported, not absorbed into it.

   This is an explanation of the FLATNESS, not a replacement for the bisection,
   and every number quoted elsewhere in this file is the exact one.
