============================================================================================
three_knobs.py -- the five particle axes, reduced to x, chi and sigma*
============================================================================================
   eta/eta_s = (1 - x/[1 - (1-chi) fhat])^-2,  x = phi/phi0, chi = phim/phi0
   x <  chi     : never jams          (continuous thickening only)
   chi <= x < 1 : SHEAR-JAMMING window, width 1-chi
   x >= 1       : jammed at rest
   chi is formed as phi(mu_p=3.0)/phi(mu_p=0.0), seed-paired, converged cells only.
   grading scale PHI_SCALE = 0.0021.

=== 1. DOES THE SIZE DISTRIBUTION MOVE THE WINDOW? ===
   At the quoted rung p_hat = 3e-05:

       CV   n     phi_0     phi_m      chi       se  window 1-chi
      0.0   3   0.63182   0.56054   0.8872   0.0042        0.1128
      0.2   3   0.64413   0.57272   0.8891   0.0025        0.1109
      0.5   1   0.69428   0.61236   0.8876      inf        0.1124

   chi(CV=0.2) - chi(CV=0.0), PAIRED = +0.0019 +- 0.0034  (n=3, r=+0.86)
      |t| = 0.56 vs t_crit 4.303 -> NOT RESOLVED    [the withdrawn unpaired estimator gave 0.40]
   ⚠ CV = 0.5 has n = 1 converged pair: the FRICTIONLESS arm still
     caps at 1e6 on 2 of 3 seeds, so chi(0.5) is not resolvable. Its central
     value 0.8876 sits with the others and is printed, not quoted.

   🔑 THE SIZE DISTRIBUTION IS AN x-KNOB, NOT A chi-KNOB. It raises phi_0 and
   phi_m by the SAME factor, so the shear-jamming window does not move. Widening
   the PSD does not make a suspension more able to shear-jam; it moves the
   suspension within the window, exactly as changing concentration would.

=== 2. 🔴 chi IS NOT RUNG-INDEPENDENT. The ratio does NOT escape the ladder. ===
   A tempting simplification -- 'chi is a ratio, so the pressure dependence
   cancels and two cells measure it' -- is FALSE, and this is the test:

       CV    p=1e-03    p=3e-04    p=1e-04    p=3e-05  chi spread  phi_0 spread
      0.0     0.8733     0.8708     0.8798     0.8872      0.0164        0.0094
      0.2     0.8730     0.8698     0.8766     0.8891      0.0193        0.0098
      0.5     0.8672     0.8627     0.8774     0.8876      0.0250        0.0050

   chi drifts across the ladder by MORE than phi_0 itself does, because the two
   friction arms have different compressibilities: phi = phi_J + c p^b with a
   different c in each arm, so the ratio does not cancel -- it AMPLIFIES.
   ⚠ CONSEQUENCE: chi must be quoted AT A NAMED RUNG or extrapolated. There is
   no shortcut around the ladder, and the 'two cells measure chi' idea is dead.

   ✅ BUT THE VERDICT IN SECTION 1 SURVIVES, and this is why: chi moves across
   CV by at most ~0.008 at any single rung, while it moves 0.016-0.025 ACROSS
   rungs. The CV effect is smaller than the systematic it would have to beat.
   'The distribution does not move the window' is a statement about a
   comparison AT FIXED RUNG, and that is the comparison that was made.

=== 3. THE AXIS MAP: which knob does each particle property turn? ===

   axis              knob            why                                                 status
   SIZE  a           sigma*          sigma* ~ F_rep/a^2 (convention-dependent)           
                                                                                         NOT SEPARATED -- onset_size_degeneracy_2026-09-19: a size ladder cannot separate the two grain-scale stresses
   SHAPE alpha       chi             elongation/interlocking lowers phi_m far more than phi_0
                                                                                         ✅ MEASURED (shapechi): chi 0.8856 -> 0.7884, window x1.85, |t| = 19.3, same sign at all 4 rungs. phi_0 moves -0.9 %, phi_m -11.7 %
   DISTRIBUTION CV   x               phi_0 and phi_m rise together                       
                                                                                         ✅ MEASURED HERE: chi flat, PAIRED |t| = 0.56 (r = +0.86) across CV 0 -> 0.2
   POTENTIAL F_rep   sigma*          sets the repulsive force the contact must overcome  
                                                                                         🔴 UNMEASURED in this repo -- it is the DEM's `sigma*` input, not an output
   MATERIAL mu_p     chi             phi_0 is frictionless BY DEFINITION, so mu_p moves phi_m alone
                                                                                         ✅ MEASURED: chi = 0.887 at mu_p 0 -> 3, window 1-chi = 0.113
   MATERIAL E        eta_s / none    stiffness rescales the contact, not phi_J           
                                                                                         ✅ the static packing is scale-free (test_scale_invariance.py)

   🔑 READ THE TABLE AS A PROGRAMME. Two axes turn the WINDOW (shape, friction),
   two turn the ONSET STRESS (size, potential), one turns POSITION (distribution)
   and one turns nothing (stiffness). Only the window knobs can change WHETHER a
   suspension shear-jams; the rest change where or when.

   🔑 BOTH WINDOW-KNOBS ARE NOW MEASURED, and they behave the same way: each
   leaves phi_0 nearly alone and pulls phi_m down. Friction does it by design;
   non-sphericity does it because constraining ROTATION is mechanically what
   friction does. The size distribution, which moves both branches together, is
   a position-knob and cannot change whether a suspension shear-jams at all.

   🔴 THE LARGEST GAP IS NOW sigma*: BOTH remaining axes -- particle SIZE and
   surface POTENTIAL -- act through it, and NEITHER has a measurement here. The
   existing size derivation says a size ladder alone cannot separate them, so
   the campaign that closes it sweeps the REPULSIVE FORCE explicitly.

=== 4. WHAT A DROP IMPACT NEEDS ON TOP OF (x, chi, sigma*) ===
   The drop supplies a STRESS and a TIME, and the suspension answers with a
   state. Two conditions, and they are independent:

      (i)  sigma_imp / sigma*  >> 1     the contacts are actually activated
      (ii) chi <= x < 1                 the activated state is JAMMED

   Both must hold for a drop to rebound as a solid. (i) is the sigma*-knobs
   (size, potential); (ii) is the window knobs (friction, shape) and the
   position knob (concentration, distribution, aggregation).
   ⚠ WHICH STRESS GAUGE sigma_imp IS is a convention this file does not choose;
   lessons.md records that `rho k U0^2` and the deviatoric wall shear give
   OPPOSITE verdicts on whether the activation half is saturated.

=== 5. THE OBSERVABLE-FACING NUMBER: the THICKENING RATIO ===
   The rheometer does not measure x and chi; it measures how much the viscosity
   RISES. That is also closed-form in the same two numbers:

       Theta = eta(fhat=1)/eta(fhat=0) = [(1-x)/(1-x/chi)]^MP_EXPO
             -> infinity as x -> chi from below (for chi < 1)

   🔴 THE EXPONENT IS A PARAMETER, NOT A CONSTANT, and an earlier version of
   this section hardcoded it at 2. `wc_drop_impact.c` exposes MP_EXPO and its
   comment block says, for THIS material, that 2 is the assumption that FAILS:
   freeing it takes the cornstarch fit residual from rms(log eta) = 1.335 to
   0.274, fitted value ~5. Quoting Theta at 2 for cs_fit uses the one value the
   solver's own code says is wrong for cornstarch.

   ⚠ AND Theta IS NOT NEW. It is the solver's own `R_max`, defined in that same
   comment block and verified here to be the identical formula to 1e-9. The
   block also already reports R_max = 3.3 for cs_fit over phi = 0.30-0.43 and
   already concludes it is short. This section RE-DERIVES a documented result
   from the (x, chi) reduction -- which is a CHECK on the reduction, and was
   wrongly written up as a new number.

   (SymPy-verified in three_knobs_check.py, C4. Theta DIVERGES exactly at the
   window edge FOR chi < 1, so 'shear-jamming' and 'infinite Theta' are the same
   statement. ⚠ The condition is not decoration: at chi = 1 the two branches
   coincide and Theta == 1 identically -- the check FAILED until it carried it.)

   WHAT RHEOMETRY BUYS, stated more carefully than the first version of this
   file did (it said 'a flow curve gives all three numbers and needs no packing
   model', which oversells on three counts):
      sigma*   -- yes, read off the thickening knee of ONE curve.
      x, chi   -- NOT from one curve. eta_low/eta_s = (1-x)^-n gives x only if
                  eta_s AND n are known, and NEITHER is a datasheet number: the
                  calibrated eta_s values (0.05 ... 0.0039 Pa.s) are 4-50x any
                  plausible carrier viscosity, i.e. eta_s is itself a fit output,
                  and n is the MP_EXPO the solver says is degenerate with phi.
      => the honest recipe is a WC fit to eta(sigma) at SEVERAL phi, which is
         what PROGRAMME section 3 says. Still no new instrument -- but it is a
         concentration series, not a single curve.

   The drop-impact solver's own presets, placed on the diagram:

      preset       x     chi  Theta(n=2)  Theta(n=5)  decades n=2     n=5  state
          cs  0.8387  0.8065         inf         inf          inf     inf  SHEAR-JAMS  [PLACEHOLDER, never calibrated]
          si  0.9219  0.8906         inf         inf          inf     inf  SHEAR-JAMS  [PLACEHOLDER, never calibrated]
      si_fit  0.6286  0.7394        6.14       93.33         0.79    1.97  never jams  [calibrated; the FIT IS WITHDRAWN]
      cs_fit  0.6714  0.7807        5.51       71.25         0.74    1.85  never jams  [calibrated, live]

   🔴 THE HEADLINE FLIPS WITH THE EXPONENT, so it is quoted with the exponent.
   At n = 2 the calibrated presets reach 0.74-0.79 decades -- far short of the
   two to three decades DST is usually quoted at, which is what an earlier
   version of this file asserted as 'cannot produce the phenomenon'. At the
   repo's OWN fitted n ~ 5 they reach 1.85-1.97 decades, i.e. MOST OF THE WAY.
   ⚠ The solver's comment block also records that n is DEGENERATE WITH phi --
   refitting with phi free per curve and n held at 2 fits equally well (rms
   0.270 vs 0.274) -- so this is a CHOICE BETWEEN TWO READINGS OF ONE DATASET,
   not a number to be settled by more fitting. Breaking it needs an independent
   phi measurement on the batch. Nothing here settles it, and nothing here may
   be quoted as if it did.

   ⚠ 'DST spans two to three decades' is [UNVERIFIED] -- no citation resolved,
   and it is the bar the comparison above is graded against. The solver's own
   block quotes a specific measured 206x (2.3 decades) for its cornstarch set,
   which is a sourced number and the one to use.


   ⚠ THAT THE TWO `_fit` PRESETS CANNOT JAM is ALREADY KNOWN in the solver's
   own repository (PAPER_STF_CLAIMS_2026-08-02: 'Wyart-Cates cannot jam there at
   any fhat'), and that repo ALSO records cells ARRESTING in that regime anyway.
   So drop ARREST is a KINETIC criterion (the solver's own `jam_frac`, `u_max <
   0.02 U0`) and is NOT a readout of CONSTITUTIVE jamming (eta -> infinity).
   A finite Theta of 5-90x is perfectly capable of arresting a drop.
