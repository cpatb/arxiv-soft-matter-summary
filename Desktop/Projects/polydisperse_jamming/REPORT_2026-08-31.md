## 0. Summary

**The bench observation is reproduced in direction, its mechanism is identified, and the
mechanism is not the one the project was set up to test.**

1. **M1 (permeability) is excluded over most of the range but not by a comfortable margin.**
   Raising CV at fixed number mean raises the Sauter mean and hence `ID ∝ d₃₂²`, in the
   observed direction, but only from 7.2e-5 to 1.8e-3 against a boundary at 1. It cannot
   produce a transition. ⚠ Against `ID`'s *own empirical* boundary (6.6e-3) the top corner is
   within 3.6×.
2. **M2 (`φ_J` shift) is real, and insufficient where the lab works.** `φ_J` rises +0.062
   over CV = 0 → 0.5 at `μ_p` = 0.3 (≈13× seed scatter). ⚠ It rises monotonically **on the
   lognormal family only**: on a zero-skew family the increment collapses above CV ≈ 0.3
   (+0.0093 then +0.0001), which is *consistent with* rather than contrary to the 2D
   non-monotonicity of Singh et al., arXiv:2510.16464, whose own distributions have skewness
   0. An earlier draft of this report claimed the opposite by comparing the wrong families;
   see §5. At `φ` = 0.44 the shift buys only **0.65–0.77×** in viscosity, not the
   pre-registered decade.
3. **M3 (structure) survives, and a finite-size check now backs it.** At matched mechanical
   state the rattler fraction rises **0.040 → 0.234** (20σ), the largest backbone component
   is identically 1.0000 at every CV, and `Z` over the backbone moves far less
   (−0.116 across the whole CV range against −2.008 for `Z` over all grains).
   **Polydispersity does not restructure the force network, it removes grains from it.**
   An N = 1500 → 3000 ladder confirms all six trends at the same sign and within 22%,
   though the *absolute* values are N-sensitive and the code's own `d_max/L` flag fires on
   50–57% of CV ≥ 0.4 cells. See §6c.
4. **Matched moments determine the packing, and that isolates skewness as the cause.** A
   third family (bidisperse, 50:50 at `d₁₀(1±CV)`) matches the uniform one on mean, CV,
   skewness and `d₃₂` to four decimals while being a completely different shape, and their
   `φ_J` agree at **6/6 CV values within 2σ**. So the lognormal's much larger shift really
   is its tail. See §6b.
5. **CV and skewness separate cleanly.** A zero-skewness uniform family
   agrees with the lognormal to CV ≈ 0.2 and then diverges (3.2σ, 8.8σ, 7.6σ), saturating at
   `φ_J` ≈ 0.596 while the lognormal climbs on its tail. **The rattler mechanism is CV-driven
   (17/17 comparisons agree); `φ_J` above CV ≈ 0.2 is a tail effect.** So above CV ≈ 0.2,
   `φ_J` cannot be predicted from a polydispersity index, the distribution shape must be
   measured.
6. **A pre-registered prediction was falsified and the failure is physical.** Capillary
   confinement was predicted to be irrelevant during impact (`Λ` = We/4 = 12.5–200). It is
   not: capillary aggregates behave like *caged* ones and nothing like free ones. Re-checked
   after the confinement error (`audit_claims.py`): free spread **3.317 ± 0.384** against
   capillary **1.070 ± 0.158**, a **21.6σ** separation, and **22.0σ** when the confined arm
   is restricted to cells that were still jammed. **A cohesionless aggregate has zero tensile
   strength, so the free limit is singular and a stress-ratio argument cannot see it.**
   ⚠ Scope narrowed: the confinement was too weak to keep those aggregates *jammed*, but
   strong enough to hold the grains *spatially together*. So this is a statement about the
   cohesion of the body, not about a jammed drop. The gate is deliberately **not** applied to
   the free arm, because a free aggregate disintegrating is the finding, not a defect.
7. ⚠ **"Nothing rebounds" is RETRACTED as a statement about jammed drops, and replaced.**
   The Phase 4/5 aggregates were losing **50–100% of their coordination before impact**,
   because the confinement holding them was ~100× weaker than the pressure they were
   carved at. Those runs measured an *unjammed* cloud. Two things repair it, both new
   today: `test_cor_anchor.py` shows the measurement itself is sound (an elastic sphere
   returns COR = 0.819 through the same path at the production damping, so a rebound was
   detectable 23× over, and it also caught an inverted sign convention); and
   `phase7_confined.py` repeats the experiment with `p_cap = p_carve` plus a gate on
   `Z_launch/Z_carved`. See §7a–7b.
8. **The stiffness bracket is now satisfied, after two bugs were found chasing it.**
   SPEC §2.3's sweep had never been run for Phases 4–7. Running it exposed (a) a reduced
   pressure used as an absolute stress, and (b) a **net-force bug that made the confinement
   a thruster** — a CV = 0 aggregate accelerated away from the wall with no wall contact,
   COR climbing to +0.339. Both fixed. The apparent stiffness dependence was the thrust:
   the E = 1 vs E = 10 difference at CV = 0.5 falls from **0.113 to 0.0008**. See §7d–7e.
9. **Cohesion is what a rebound needs, and polydispersity suppresses it.** Bonding the
   contacts at `C = σ_coh/ρU₀² ≳ 100` makes a monodisperse aggregate rebound (COR **+0.519 ±

**What this does not do:** it does not explain the *magnitude* of the bench effect at the

## 1. The question, and why it needed a null killed first

The author observes on the bench that at *similar particle size*, raising the coefficient of
variation `CV` of the size distribution moves an impacting dense-suspension drop from purely
dilatant to spreading and inertial-dominated. "Similar size" means matched **number mean**,
to within about 100 nm.

⚠ **That dataset is not on disk.** A search of `Desktop\Projects`, `Desktop\Papers` and the
Obsidian `Status/` tree on 2026-08-31 found no drop-impact CV data. Every `CV` in this report
is a bench-note figure. **Nothing here may be quoted as agreeing with experiment until the
raw distributions are produced.**

Matching two samples on the number mean does not match them on the moment the physics reads.
Carman–Kozeny permeability uses the **Sauter mean** `d₃₂ = ⟨d³⟩/⟨d²⟩`, and for a lognormal at
fixed number mean the ratio is exact:

```
d32/d10 = (1 + CV²)²          kappa ratio = (1 + CV²)⁴
```

verified analytically and against a 2×10⁷-draw Monte-Carlo (agrees to 4 decimals to CV = 0.8;
drifts to 3.957 vs 4.000 at CV = 1 as the third-moment tail converges slowly). So raising CV
raises permeability, and the lab's own inertial–dilatant number `ID ∝ κ ∝ d₃₂²` with it, **in
exactly the observed direction, with no packing physics at all.** That is mechanism **M1**,
and it had to die before a grain-scale model could be justified.

## 4. Method, and the four traps found while building it

Model: frictional hard spheres, Yade DEM, **no interstitial fluid at all**. Dimensionless
throughout (`d10 = 1`, `ρ = 1`, `E = 1`), with the reduced pressure `p_hat = p/E` as the
control parameter. `φ_J` is never read off a single run, it is the `p_hat → 0` extrapolation
of a pressure ladder.

**Why dimensionless.** At the physical scale (2 µm, `E` = 1e8 Pa) `PWaveTimeStep` gives
dt ≈ 1.4e-9 s and one N = 300 cell took over 3 minutes; a 150-cell sweep would be days.
Packing observables are unit-invariant, so the physics is unchanged and the stiffness sweep
the spec demanded becomes a one-line sweep of `p_hat`.

**Scale invariance also separates M1 from M3 by construction.** A DEM packing cannot see an
absolute length, so at fixed distribution shape, matching on `d10` and matching on `d32` are
the *same experiment*. M1 lives in the fluid; **everything the DEM reports is free of M1
without any matching being done** (spec §11.7). This is why Phase 3 needed no new runs.

### The four traps

1. **The rattler threshold is a definition that changes the answer.** Iterated removal of
   grains with `< k` contacts is k-core decomposition. At frictional mean `Z` = 4.86 the
   **k = 4 core is empty**, the entire pack reads as rattlers, while a single non-iterated
   pass removes 15%. The frictionless case is unaffected (`Z` = 6.92, rattlers 0.001 at every
   k). All of k = 2/3/4 are recorded; k = 2 is primary.
2. **The exponent anchor was measuring noise, and the fix was a stricter gate.** A harmonic
   contact law predicts `φ − φ_J ∝ p^1`. The first complete ladder returned `b` = 0.248. The
   raw numbers explain why: the frictionless ladder is 0.6434, 0.6319, 0.6352, 0.6313 over
   1.5 decades, **non-monotonic**. A range gate alone would have passed it (range 0.012
   against a seed scatter of 0.00111), so the gate requires range **and** monotonicity, since
   `φ_J + c p^b` is monotonic by construction and cannot describe such a series at any `b`.
   The frictional ladders are cleanly monotonic (0.5949, 0.5827, 0.5748). The excluded
   ladder's `b` is still printed beside the verdict.
3. **Yade body forces must come from a `PyRunner`.** `ForceResetter` zeroes `O.forces` at the
   start of every timestep, so the capillary pull applied from Python between `O.run(400)`
   calls acted for one step in 400. Combined with overlaps from random insertion, the first
   Phase-4 version returned COR = 2.6e37. The fix was to carve aggregates out of an
   already-validated compressed periodic pack rather than compacting them bespoke.
4. **`N` is bounded below by the periodic collider, and the bound binds at the final
   density.** The cell shrinks under compression, so sizing the insertion cell is not enough:
   N = 200 at CV = 0.5 passed at φ = 0.30 and died on the way to 0.64. At CV = 0.5 with
   `truncate = (0.2, 5.0)` the requirement is **N ≥ 1299**; production runs at N = 1500.

## 5. Phase 2: `φ_J(CV)` and mechanism M2

The final dataset is **388 cells, of which 67 (17.3%) did not converge** and are dropped. (Earlier drafts of this section quoted 149/34 and 316/249, which were the counts at the time each was written; the analysis scripts always load the full set.) **The drops are not a
random sample**, non-convergence rises from 5% at CV = 0 to 46% at CV = 0.5, and is 37% at
`μ_p` = 0 against 5.7% at `μ_p` = 0.3, so the bias was measured rather than assumed. All 34
failures hit the 200 000-iteration cap with unbalanced force median 0.043 against a 0.02
bar: they are *slow*, not *wrong*. Dropping them moves `φ` by at most **0.0042**, median
**0.0006**. `analyse_phase2.py` now runs this check automatically on every invocation.

**The exponent anchor FAILS, and the bar is not being moved.** A harmonic contact law
predicts `φ − φ_J ∝ p̂¹`; on the final 316-cell dataset the free fit gives median
`b` = **0.549** over 17 informative ladders against a pre-registered bar of [0.6, 1.6], IQR
[0.352, 0.668]. So the exponent is both low and poorly determined.

⚠ The verdict file also prints that the **excluded** (non-monotonic) ladders would have
given median `b` = 0.993, suspiciously close to the predicted 1, which would look like an
argument that the gate is wrong. It is not, and the reason was tested directly rather than
asserted: fitting `φ_J + c·p̂^b` to one of the actual excluded ladders **does not converge**,
returning `b` = 9.7 / 10.1 / 13162 from starting guesses 0.3 / 1.0 / 2.0 with the covariance
unestimable. The individual values are arbitrary, so their median is arbitrary too. (My first
explanation, that the fit returns its initial guess, was wrong, and testing it is what
showed the values diverge instead.)

Ladders are admitted only if their `φ` range exceeds 2× the seed scatter **and** they are
monotone in `p̂`, `φ_J + c·p̂^b` is monotonic by construction and cannot describe a series
that goes down, up, down at any `b`. The frictionless ladders are exactly that
(0.6434, 0.6319, 0.6352, 0.6313 over 1.5 decades) and one of them returned `b` = 0.248; a
range gate alone would have admitted it. The excluded ladders' median `b` is printed beside
the verdict so the exclusion stays visible.

**What the failure costs.** `b` systematically below 1 means the linear extrapolation
slightly over-corrects, so `φ_J` may be marginally **under**estimated. Two reasons are
plausible and neither is fixed here: the ladder spans only 1.5 decades, and the
lowest-pressure rung is where non-convergence concentrates (60% at `p̂` = 3e-5), so the end
that most constrains the exponent is the least populated. **Every comparison in this report
is a difference at fixed protocol, so the ordering and the effect sizes are unaffected; an
absolute `φ_J` quoted from here carries this caveat.**

### `φ_J` rises monotonically with CV, in both friction cases

| `μ_p` | CV = 0 | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.30 | 0.40 | 0.50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 0.6332 | 0.6375 | 0.6454 | 0.6583 | 0.6729 | 0.6833 |
| 0.3 | 0.5745 | 0.5781 | 0.5881 | 0.6040 | 0.6196 | 0.6369 |

Seed scatter is 0.0002–0.0048, so the CV = 0 → 0.5 shift is **+0.062 at `μ_p` = 0.3, about
13× the seed scatter**. (The `μ_p` = 0 column is weaker at the top: CV = 0.5 survived on one
seed, because that corner is where non-convergence concentrates.)

⚠ **CORRECTED after reading arXiv:2510.16464 in full (2026-09-01).** An earlier version of
this section claimed the monotonic 3D result "answers a question left non-monotonic in 2D".
**That compared the wrong families.** Singh, Tyagi & Santra measure their non-monotonicity on
a **normal** size distribution with skewness *exactly* 0 (their Table 2: five normals at
`s.d./⟨a⟩` = 0.072 to 0.320, plus a single lognormal at 0.374 with `s` = 0.694). The
like-for-like comparison is against the **uniform** family here, not the lognormal.

Made like-for-like, the two results agree rather than conflict. What separates the families
is the *increment*, not the sign:

| `μ_p` = 0.3, steps in `φ_J` across the CV ladder | | | | | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| uniform, skewness 0 | +0.0038 | +0.0077 | +0.0093 | **+0.0001** | **+0.0019** |
| lognormal, skewness (3+CV²)·CV | +0.0036 | +0.0099 | +0.0159 | +0.0156 | +0.0173 |

**On the zero-skew family `dφ_J/dCV` peaks near CV ≈ 0.3 and then collapses to within seed
scatter; on the lognormal it keeps growing.** The same rollover appears at `μ_p` = 0, where the
uniform steps run +0.0035, +0.0082, +0.0074, +0.0050 (peaking at CV = 0.2) while the lognormal
runs +0.0043, +0.0079, +0.0129, +0.0145.

Their `α` range stops at 0.320, which is exactly where the zero-skew rollover sets in here. So
the honest reading is: **the non-monotonicity they report is plausibly a property of
zero-skew distributions, and the distribution tail is what keeps `φ_J` climbing.** In 3D the
zero-skew family saturates rather than turning over; whether it would actually reverse is not
settled by this data and would need CV > 0.5, which the periodic-cell constraint blocks at
this `N`.

This makes the skewness result of §6a and their non-monotonicity the *same* finding seen from
two directions, which is a better outcome than the contradiction I first wrote down.

### M2 verdict: real, well-resolved, and insufficient where the lab works

Because `φ_J` *rises* with CV, a polydisperse suspension at fixed `φ` sits **further** from
jamming and is therefore **less** viscous, which is the direction of the bench observation.
Under `η ∝ (φ_J − φ)⁻²` the ratio `η(high CV)/η(low CV)` is:

| `φ` | 0.40 | 0.44 | 0.50 | 0.55 | 0.58 | 0.60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ratio (`μ_p` = 0) | 0.805 | **0.771** | 0.693 | 0.572 | 0.442 | 0.306 |

**At the lab's `φ` = 0.44 the shift is worth 0.77× (`μ_p` = 0) or 0.65× (`μ_p` = 0.3), a few
tens of percent, not a decade.** One decade of thinning needs `φ ≳ 0.62`, which is close to
`φ_J` itself. So **M2 is insufficient at the bulk operating point.**

⚠ The pre-registered test as first written solved for the *opposite* direction, a factor of
+10, and returned `φ` = 0.672, a value above both `φ_J` values and therefore unreachable by
construction. **The sign of the test was wrong, not the data**; it is fixed and both the
corrected threshold and the effect at `φ` = 0.44 are printed.

⚠ **What this does not settle.** The *local* packing fraction inside an impacting drop is not
the bulk 0.44, the material is dynamically compacted at the contact. M2's sufficiency
depends on the `φ` the impact actually reaches, which this model does not measure and the
experiment does not know. **That is the measurement that would decide it.**

## 6a. Is it polydispersity, or is it skewness?

Desmond & Weeks (*PRE* **90**, 022204) show skewness can shift `φ_RCP` more than
polydispersity does. A lognormal's skewness is fixed by its CV, `S = (3 + CV²)·CV`, so
CV = 0.5 gives S = 1.63, so **the lognormal ladder moves CV and skewness together**. A
`UniformPSD` family (skewness ≡ 0 at every CV, verified numerically to |S| < 0.002) breaks
the degeneracy at matched CV.

**Final dataset: 388 packing cells, 321 converged.** The dissociation was then confirmed at
a **second friction value** by running the uniform family at `μ_p` = 0 as well, so it is not
a property of one friction coefficient:

| family, `μ_p` | CV range | Δ`φ_J` |
|---|---|---|
| lognormal, 0.0 | 0 → 0.479 | **+0.0502** |
| uniform, 0.0 | 0 → 0.400 | **+0.0241** |
| lognormal, 0.3 | 0 → 0.496 | **+0.0623** |
| uniform, 0.3 | 0 → 0.499 | **+0.0228** |

**At both friction values the zero-skewness family gives roughly a third to a half of the
lognormal's shift.** Cell by cell, `μ_p` = 0.30:

| CV | lognormal | uniform | diff | n_sd | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 0.5745 | 0.5751 | +0.0006 | 0.59 | agree |
| 0.10 | 0.5781 | 0.5790 | +0.0008 | 0.56 | agree |
| 0.20 | 0.5881 | 0.5866 | −0.0014 | 0.90 | agree |

**The two families agree up to CV ≈ 0.2 and then separate, and the bounded uniform family
SATURATES** at `φ_J` ≈ 0.596 while the lognormal keeps climbing. Above CV ≈ 0.2 the rise in
`φ_J` is therefore **a property of the lognormal's tail, not of the width of the
distribution**.

Per observable, over 17 matched comparisons:

- **CV-driven** (families agree): rattler fraction k=2 **17/17**, rattler fraction k=3
  **17/17**, largest backbone component 17/17, `Z` over all grains 16/17
- **Skewness-sensitive**: packing fraction at fixed `p̂` (8/17), contact-force tail slope
  (9/17), contact-force CV (11/17), `Z` over the backbone (12/17)

**This is a clean dissociation, and it is the most useful result of the session.** The M3
mechanism, grains dropping out of the load-bearing network, is **purely CV-driven and
family-independent**. The M2 mechanism, the `φ_J` shift, is CV-driven only up to CV ≈ 0.2
and becomes a tail effect above it.

**Consequence for the experiment: above CV ≈ 0.2, `φ_J` cannot be predicted from a
polydispersity index alone. The distribution shape must be measured.** That is Desmond &
Weeks' warning, reproduced here for *frictional* shear-jamming rather than frictionless RCP,
and it is directly actionable for the bench.

## 6c. Finite size: two different questions, two different answers

Everything here was produced at N = 1500 grains, the smallest N clearing the
periodic-collider constraint at CV = 0.5, which leaves system size untested under every
result. Two things now check it.

**The code's own finite-size flag, which the report had never surfaced.** `packing.py`
computes `d_max_over_L` and flags any cell where the largest grain exceeds a quarter of the
cell at its densest. Across the full dataset the flag rate is **0/115 at CV = 0, 0/60 at CV = 0.1, 0/60 at
CV = 0.2, 8/72 (11%) at CV = 0.3, 36/72 (50%) at CV = 0.4, 48/84 (57%) at CV = 0.5.**
The risk is concentrated exactly at the high-CV end, which is where every headline effect
lives. The flag was computed on every run and reported by none of them until a referee
pass looked for it.

(Counted over all non-failed cells. Restricting to *converged* cells gives a lower
11 / 32 / 39% — the two corners overlap, because a cell with a grain large enough to trip
the flag is also a cell that struggles to reach the force criterion. The inclusive numbers
are quoted here because they describe the data as generated.)

**An N = 1500 → 3000 ladder** on the load-bearing cells (`analyse_finite_size.py`, 27
matched cells at identical CV, friction, reduced pressure and seed). The answer depends on
which question is asked, and the distinction matters:

*Does the absolute value move?* Yes, for everything — by 3 to 5 times the seed scatter. On
that basis the "backbone `Z` is unchanged across CV" claim looks unsupported: its CV effect
is 0.082 while its worst N shift is 0.124.

*Does the CV trend move?* **No.** A finite-size offset common to every CV cancels in the
difference a CV claim actually uses, and the trends measured independently at each size
agree:

| observable | trend @ N=1500 | trend @ N=3000 | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| packing fraction at fixed `p̂` | +0.0609 | +0.0600 | 0.98 |
| `Z` over all grains | −2.0076 | −2.0533 | 1.02 |
| rattler fraction (k=2) | +0.4052 | +0.4140 | 1.02 |
| `Z` over backbone (k=2) | −0.1158 | −0.0970 | 0.84 |

**All six trends hold: same sign, within 22% at double the system size.** So the CV claims

⚠ Both checks are needed and they say different things. Quoting an absolute `φ_J` or an

⚠ Two seeds per size, three CV values, one doubling. This bounds the N dependence; it does

## 7. Phase 4: the drop-like boundary

60 cells: five CV values × five boundary conditions × up to three seeds, all carved from
identical parent packings so the comparison is within-configuration. `N_grains` ≈ 620–710
after carving.

### The pre-registration is falsified, and that is the result

SPEC §5 recorded, **before the runs**:

> At `Λ` = 12.5–200 the capillary case is expected to sit close to the free case. If it
> does, that IS the finding, capillary confinement is irrelevant during impact, and it
> must be reported rather than tuned away.

It does not. Lateral spread, pooled over CV:

| boundary | `Λ` | spread |
|---|---|---|
| caged | 0 | 1.01 – 1.24 |
| capillary | 12.5 | 0.95 – 0.99 |
| capillary | 50 | 1.04 – 1.06 |
| capillary | 200 | 1.31 – 1.53 |
| **free** | ∞ | **3.03 – 3.99** |

**The capillary cases sit with the CAGED case, not the free one**, at 13–45 seed standard
deviations from free. The prediction in §2.1, that `Λ` = 12.5–200 makes surface tension "one
to two orders of magnitude too weak to confine the grains", was wrong, and the reason is
instructive.

**Why the Weber argument fails.** `Λ = We/4` compares capillary pressure to the *bulk
inertial stress*. But a cohesionless granular aggregate has **zero tensile strength**, so the
question is not whether capillary pressure exceeds the inertial stress, it is whether it
exceeds the material's cohesion, which is zero. **The free limit is singular, not merely
extreme.** Any nonzero confinement is qualitatively different from none, and a stress-ratio
argument cannot see that.

This matters beyond the model: it says the free surface of a real drop is **not** dynamically
equivalent to an unconfined granular aggregate, even at `We` = 800.

### What `Λ` does do

Within the capillary series the trend is clean and monotonic, the boundary is not a
switch:

| `Λ` | spread | `Z` at peak | rattler fraction at peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12.5 | 0.990 | 4.298 | 0.050 |
| 50 | 1.063 | 2.284 | 0.403 |
| 200 | 1.309 | 1.306 | 0.777 |
| free | 3.035 | 0.985 | 0.890 |

(CV = 0 column.) Weaker confinement gives more spread, a sparser contact network at peak,
and a larger spectator population, monotonically in `Λ`, then a jump to the free case.

### The CV effect survives inside every boundary condition

At fixed boundary, raising CV raises the peak rattler fraction monotonically, e.g. at
`Λ` = 12.5: **0.050 → 0.107 → 0.326 → 0.563** for CV = 0, 0.2, 0.35, 0.5. This is the same
M3 signature Phase 3 found in quasi-static compression, now surviving a dynamic impact.

And in the **free** case the lateral spread rises with CV: 3.03, 3.14, 3.29, 3.21, 3.99 for
CV = 0, 0.2, 0.3, 0.35, 0.5, **more polydisperse spreads more**, which is the direction of
the author's bench observation.

confinement holding it afterwards is `p_cap = p_imp/Λ` = 4e-4/50 = **8e-6**, roughly
**100× weaker** than a `p_carve` of 1e-3. Released from carving, such an aggregate
expands until its contacts open.

`Z_launch/Z_carved` is **bimodal** (`figures/F6_jamming_gate`): a spike near 0 and a
cluster at 0.85–1.10. Median 0.877. So the sweep is a *mixture*, not a write-off.
`analyse_phase5.py` now gates on retention ≥ 0.80 before interpreting anything:
**47 of 79 cells pass; the other 32 are excluded and the exclusion is printed.**

and the histogram is what showed so.

**On the 47 jammed cells the conclusion is unchanged: 0 of 25 groups rebound

- **The measurement is anchored.** `test_cor_anchor.py` drops one elastic sphere through

- **The design is repaired.** `phase7_confined.py` confines at `p_cap = p_carve`, which

### The rebound result, as gated


**Nothing rebounds.** The coefficient of restitution is between −0.035 and +0.005 in all 60

rebound, the mechanism is **not** in the grain contact network alone, it needs either the
fluid, cohesion, or the stress-activated friction this model deliberately omits.

more inertial per grain than the experiment and no affordable N fixes that.** Phase 4
compares boundary conditions at matched `I`; it predicts no experimental regime boundary.

## 7c. What a rebound actually needs, and why polydispersity forbids it

`phase6_cohesion.py` bonds the carved contacts with `CohFrictMat` and sweeps the bond
strength in units of the impact stress, `C = σ_coh/ρU₀²`, over four decades. Both
pre-registered checks pass:

- **Discriminating negative:** at `C` = 0 the cohesive model must reproduce the
  cohesionless result. Worst |COR| at `C` = 0 is **0.0262**, against a bar of 0.05. Pass.
- **Positive control:** somewhere on the ladder COR must lift clearly off the floor. It
  does. Pass.

| CV | `Z` at launch | rebound threshold | COR at `C` = 3000 | spread at `C` = 3000 | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 4.17 | `C` = 100–300 | **+0.519 ± 0.020** | 1.013 | 3 |
| 0.25 | 3.54 | `C` = 100–300 | **+0.313 ± 0.104** | 1.125 | 3 |
| 0.50 | 2.33 | **none in the ladder** | +0.171 ± 0.144 | 2.649 | **6** |

⚠ **Two corrections since the first draft, both from replication.** The CV = 0.5 row was
originally quoted from a **single seed** (COR +0.060) because replicate files existed on
disk but had never been folded into the verdict; a referee pass caught it. Deepening that
cell to **n = 6** then raised the C = 3000 mean to **+0.171**, which *crosses* the 0.15
recoil bar the first version tested against.

It is still not a rebound, and making the criterion consistent across phases is what shows
why. Phases 5 and 7 both require recoil **and** coherence; `analyse_phase6.py` originally
tested recoil alone. On the same two-part test, plus a resolution requirement:

- **spread 2.649** against a coherence bar of 1.5 — the aggregate is in pieces, so the
  centre-of-mass COR is fragments departing, not a body returning;
- **mean 0.171 against its own sd 0.144** — not resolved even at n = 6.

So CV = 0.5 has **no crossing anywhere in the ladder**, while CV = 0 and CV = 0.25 cross
cleanly between `C` = 100 and 300 with spreads of 1.01 and 1.13.

Two things follow, and the second is the interesting one.

**A cohesionless jammed aggregate does not rebound because it has no tensile strength,
not because the model cannot represent a rebound.** Give the same contacts a bond
strength of a few hundred times the impact stress and the monodisperse aggregate comes
back with COR ≈ 0.5 while staying coherent (spread 1.01). That is a positive control the
Phase 4/5 negative badly needed.

**Polydispersity does not merely raise that threshold — inside this sweep it removes the
possibility.** At CV = 0.5 the aggregate never coheres at any `C` up to 3000, on six seeds.
The reason is in the same table: it launches with **2.33 contacts per grain** against 4.17
at CV = 0. There are not enough load-bearing contacts to build a solid out
of, so multiplying the strength of each one does not help.

This is the M3 result of §6 appearing dynamically. Polydispersity removes grains from the
force network; a network with too few members cannot be made rigid by strengthening its
bonds. **In the model's terms, that is why a monodisperse ball rebounds and a
polydisperse one spreads.**

⚠ Scope. `C` is a *per-contact* bond strength, so "same `C`" is not "same aggregate
strength" — the two differ by exactly the contact-number factor that is doing the work
here. Both readings are legitimate and the table states which is held fixed. And a real
suspension's tension comes from capillary bridges or adhesion, not from the bonded-contact
model used here; `C ≳ 100` is a *requirement* this model places on whatever supplies it,
not a measurement of the lab's material.

## 7d. The stiffness bracket SPEC §2.3 required, finally run — and it bites

SPEC §2.3 pre-registered a stiffness sweep and §4 then argued it was unnecessary, because
packing observables are unit-invariant under the reduced-pressure formulation. **That
argument is sound for the STATIC phases and was silently extended to the dynamic ones.**
`young = 1.0` was the only stiffness any Phase 4–7 cell ever ran at. A referee pass caught
that the check had been skipped rather than passed.

`test_scale_invariance.py` runs the same dimensionless cell at E = 0.1, 1 and 10 with `U₀`
scaled as √E so `p_imp/E` is held fixed. Invariance then requires every observable to agree.

**First run: FAIL, and it located a real bug.** `z_carved` was invariant (0.63–1.00 seed
scatters) exactly as the static argument predicts, while every dynamic observable varied
enormously — `z_launch` by 70 scatters, `z_retention` by 24–68, and `ke_ratio` reaching
**46×** at E = 0.1. The cause: `phase7_confined.py` set `p_cap = carve_p_hat`, but
`carve_p_hat` is *reduced* pressure `p/E` being used as an absolute stress. At E = 1 the
two coincide, which is why every production run was self-consistent and the error
invisible. The same class of error was in `phase4_impact.py`'s capillary force. Both fixed.

**Second run, after the fix: mostly invariant, but not everywhere.** Almost everything
collapsed to within 1–2 seed scatters. Judged on the question that matters — *does the
variation change a verdict the observable feeds?* —

| | CV = 0 | CV = 0.5 |
|---|---|---|
| jamming gate (`z_retention` ≥ 0.80) | stable | stable |
| rebound bar (COR > 0.05) | stable | **FLIPS**: 0.113 (E=1) vs 0.0009 (E=10) |
| admissibility (`ke_ratio` ≤ 1) | stable | **FLIPS**: 1.74 at E = 10 |

⚠ **This verdict is SUPERSEDED by §7e.** The flip turned out to be a second bug — the
confining force carried a non-zero net component and acted as a thruster, and its magnitude
depends on stiffness through the force-to-mass ratio. With that fixed the E = 1 vs E = 10
difference at CV = 0.5 falls from 0.113 to **0.0008** and the dynamic phases are invariant.
The paragraph below is kept because the reasoning was right even though the cause was not
the one named: an unexplained stiffness dependence *should* be treated as disqualifying
until it is explained, and treating it that way is what found the thruster.

Two consequences, as they stood before §7e resolved them:

- **Every Phase 4–7 number is an E = 1 result.** The internal comparisons are consistent
  (all production ran at one stiffness), but "at CV = 0.5 the aggregate never rebounds at
  any `C` up to 3000" is demonstrated **at E = 1**, not in general. Bracketing it is the
  work SPEC §2.3 asked for and it is still outstanding.
- **The confining `PyRunner` still does work on the aggregate**, and more of it at high
  stiffness: `ke_ratio` = 1.74 at E = 10 exceeds the physical bound. The admissibility gate
  of §7b catches this, but the artefact is not eliminated, only detected.

The static results — `φ_J`, the rattler mechanism, the moment-matching and skewness
comparisons of §5, §6, §6a and §6b — are unaffected. `z_carved` is invariant across two
decades of E, which is the direct confirmation of the argument §4 makes.

## 7e. The confinement was a thruster: a net-force bug under every impact COR

Chasing the §7d stiffness flip led to a bigger defect than the one being chased.

**The diagnosis.** `diag_cor_timing.py` traces the centre-of-mass velocity, wall-contact
count and kinetic energy across the whole probe loop. Two things were immediately wrong:

- At CV = 0.5 **wall contact never ended** — 9 contacts still live at the last sample. COR
  was being read off a body still resting on the wall, not a body that had left it.
- At CV = 0 the aggregate was **accelerating away from the wall with zero wall contacts**.
  COR climbed monotonically +0.031 → +0.339 across the loop while `ke_ratio` rose
  0.0013 → 0.115. Nothing was touching it.

**The cause.** The confining `PyRunner` applied an inward force to each shell grain. That
is self-equilibrated only while the shell is symmetric, and the shell stops being symmetric
the moment the aggregate flattens against the wall. The residual **Σf ≠ 0 acted as
continuous thrust.** A real surface tension cannot do this; the implementation could.

**The fix**, one line of physics: subtract the mean of the shell forces, so the set can
compress the aggregate but cannot translate it.

**What it changes.**

| | COR before | COR after | drift over last 20% of loop |
|---|---|---|---|
| CV = 0, E = 1 | +0.339, still climbing | **+0.0001** | 0.0001 |
| CV = 0.5, E = 1 | −0.0016 | **+0.0005** | 0.0016 |
| CV = 0.5, E = 10 | +0.0049 | **−0.0003** | 0.0016 |

Three consequences, and the first supersedes a section of this report.

**§7d's stiffness flip was this artefact.** The E = 1 vs E = 10 difference at CV = 0.5 goes
from **0.113 to 0.0008**. The thrust magnitude depends on stiffness through the force-to-mass
ratio, so it masqueraded as a scale-invariance failure. With the force made self-equilibrated
the dynamic phases are invariant, and **SPEC §2.3's stiffness bracket is now genuinely
satisfied rather than merely unrun.** The §7d limitation is withdrawn.

**The no-rebound conclusion is strengthened, not weakened.** The artefact could only ever
*add* spurious positive COR. Removing it leaves **COR ≈ 0.000 ± 0.002** against a measured
elastic ceiling of 0.819 — a null by a factor of about 500 rather than the factor of 5 the
contaminated data supported. The §7b "physically inadmissible" cells (COR +2.15, `ke_ratio`
175) were not a separate pathology; they were this one at its most visible.

**Phase 6 is untouched.** The cohesion runs hold the aggregate together with bonded
contacts and never instantiate the confining force, so §7c's numbers and the cohesion
threshold stand as reported.

⚠ What this cost: every Phase 4, 5 and 7 COR published before this correction was
contaminated. Phase 7 has been re-run in full on the fixed force; the superseded records
are kept under `out/superseded_thrust_bug/` rather than deleted. Phase 4 and 5's COR
columns should be read as upper bounds only — their spread, retention and structural
columns are unaffected, because the thrust translates the aggregate without changing its
internal packing.

## 7f. The confinement audit: a second hypothesis, well-posed and refuted

The referee's diagnostic (`diag_confine.py`) named **two** candidate mechanisms for the
impossible kinetic energy of §7d, not one. §7e fixed the first. The second deserves its own
entry because it was the more dangerous of the pair and because the answer is no.

**H2, equal-share flinging.** The confining force was divided *equally* among shell grains:
`f = p_cap * 4*pi*rmax^2 / n_shell`, so every shell grain felt the same force regardless of
its size. A surface pressure should instead give each grain a force proportional to its share
of the surface, `∝ r_i^2`. The two rules coincide exactly at CV = 0 and diverge as CV grows,
and the divergence is in the worst possible direction:

| rule | force per grain | force per unit **mass** |
|---|---|---|
| equal share | `const` | `∝ r^-3` |
| area weighted | `∝ r^2` | `∝ r^-1` |

At CV = 0.5 the smallest grains are roughly a third the size of the largest, so equal-share
over-accelerates them by about **9×** relative to the physically correct rule. That is a
CV-dependent artefact sitting directly on top of the polydispersity contrast this entire
project exists to measure — and it would have been invisible in every monodisperse control,
because at CV = 0 the two rules are identical.

**The test.** Run both weightings at both CVs and split the kinetic energy by radius quartile,
normalised by each quartile's mass share. If H2 is real, the smallest quartile is hot at
CV = 0.5 and only there.

| CV | weighting | specific KE, q1 (smallest) → q4 (largest) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | equal | — (single radius, no quartiles) |
| 0.5 | equal | 1.005 / 1.002 / 0.996 / 1.000 |
| 0.5 | area | 1.008 / 0.999 / 0.986 / 1.003 |

**Flat, under both rules.** The energy is shared uniformly per unit mass to within 1.4%, so
the small grains are not being flung and the CV contrast is not biased this way. H2 is
refuted, and the production code's equal-share rule is safe for the observables reported here.

Two things are worth saying about how this went. First, the hypothesis was **well-posed and
worth the compute**: it made a specific, falsifiable prediction about where the energy would
sit, the prediction was cheap to measure, and measuring it settled in one run what arguing
from the force law would not have settled at all. Second, it is a reminder that a defect can
be real in the *code* and absent in the *data* — the equal-share rule genuinely is not what a
surface pressure does, and it genuinely does not matter here. Both halves of that sentence
needed measuring.

## 7g. H1, geometric pumping: the fix removes the injection, not the work

§7f refuted the referee's H2. Its H1 is the other half, and it is the one that survives.

The confining force is recomputed every step as `f_tot = p_cap * 4*pi*rmax^2` from the
**current** aggregate radius. When the aggregate pancakes on the wall `rmax` grows, so the
inward force grows, and the re-formed ball is squeezed harder than it was released. That is
a non-conservative loop: it can pump energy in over a cycle. §7e fixed the **net force**, and
the argument for that fix is about *translation* -- but a self-equilibrated force set can
still do *work* on a deforming body, so §7e did not settle H1. This did.

`diag_confine.py` integrates `W_conf = sum(F . v) dt` over the shell grains, under the old
equal-share rule and under the production net-zero rule, at CV = 0.5 (the cell the whole
polydispersity contrast rests on, and the worst case for the old code).

| rule | `W_conf / ke0` | `ke_ratio` at end | `COR` | `spread` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| equal share (pre-fix) | **9.135** | **1.885** | +0.0296 | 0.941 |
| net-zero (production) | **2.906** | **0.159** | +0.0627 | 1.867 |

Two things to read off it, and they point in opposite directions.

**The fix works, on the thing that matters most.** `ke_ratio` goes from **1.885 to 0.159** --
from physically impossible (a passive body leaving with more energy than it arrived with) to
comfortably admissible. That is independent confirmation that the §7e null is clean, and it
agrees with the Phase 7 gate, where 0 of 18 cells are now inadmissible against 3 before. The
work itself falls by **3.1x**.

**But the confinement is not a passive boundary.** `W_conf/ke0 = 2.906` means the confining
pressure does roughly **three times the impact kinetic energy** in work over the probe loop.
Energy balance closes on dissipation: `ke_end = ke0 + W_conf - D` gives `D = 3.75 ke0`, so
friction and Cundall damping absorb more than the impact brought in. Nothing here is
non-physical -- a real surface tension does work on a spreading drop too, and this pressure is
set to `p_carve` precisely because that is what holds the pack jammed. The honest statement is
about **dominance**, not correctness: during the probe loop the aggregate's dynamics are
driven substantially by the boundary condition rather than by the impact.

**What that licenses and what it does not.** It does not touch the null. "Nothing rebounds" is
a statement that the aggregate fails to come back *even with* an energetically dominant
confinement actively squeezing it, which if anything makes the null stronger -- the boundary
is helping, and it still is not enough. It does mean any *quantitative* COR from this model is
a property of the aggregate-plus-confinement system, not of the aggregate alone. A COR quoted
to two figures from these runs would be over-reading them; the ordering across CV, and the
distance to the 0.819 elastic ceiling, are what the runs support.

⚠ Note also that `spread` moves 0.941 -> 1.867 between the two rules: the old thrust was
holding the aggregate together as well as pushing it. Any pre-fix spread number is affected in
the same way the CORs were.

⏳ Measured at CV = 0.5 only, on n = 2 seeds per arm. The CV = 0 arm was still running at
session end.

## 8. What would make this wrong

Stated in the spec before the runs, repeated here so it can be checked rather than
rationalised:

1. `I` never separates the regimes at any defensible `γ̇`, then the inertial framing is wrong.
2. The residual CV effect at matched mechanical state is inside seed scatter, M3 refuted.
3. Conclusions move with `E*` across the swept decades, they are softness artifacts.
4. The capillary boundary is indistinguishable from free at all `Λ`, the drop-like boundary
   adds nothing over a free packing.
5. **The author's raw distributions show the samples were not matched on the number mean
   after all**, then §2 has to be re-run and M1 may revive.

Any of 1–4 is a publishable negative. None is a reason to adjust a parameter.

---

## 9. What to do next, in order

1. **Produce the raw size distributions for the two bench samples.** Everything here is
   conditioned on them being matched on the *number* mean (§1, and §8 item 5). If they were
   matched on a volume-weighted or DLS `z`-average instead, `d₃₂` moved much more than assumed
   and M1 may revive. This is the cheapest and highest-leverage action in the list.
2. **Ask a co-author to settle the `ID` boundary calibration.** Eq. 6 returns 6.6e-3 at the
   Regime-2/3 transition the paper itself draws, not 1. The M1 exclusion in §2 is only as
   strong as that number, and Driscoll is local and on the paper.
3. **Measure the LOCAL packing fraction reached during impact.** M2's sufficiency is decided
   by the `φ` the material actually reaches at the contact, not the bulk 0.44 (§5). At bulk
   `φ` the shift is worth 0.77×; a decade needs `φ ≳ 0.62`. **This is the measurement that
   decides whether M2 matters in the experiment at all.**
4. **Read arXiv:2510.16464 in full and compare `f_rig` against the backbone fraction here.**
   Only the abstract has been read. Their order parameter is close to this project's
   largest-backbone-fraction observable, and their 2D non-monotonicity should be checked
   against the 3D monotonicity found here.
5. **Extend the uniform family to `μ_p` = 0 and to a third distribution shape.** The CV/skewness
   dissociation currently rests on one friction value and two families. A bidisperse family
   would also test Singh et al.'s claim that polydisperse ≈ statistically equivalent bidisperse.
6. **If the rebound observation is to be modelled, this model is the wrong instrument.**
   §7 shows a cohesionless frictional aggregate does not rebound at all. Adding either the
   interstitial fluid or stress-activated friction is a different project, and the fracture
   threshold of Slutzky et al. (*PRE* **113**, 025410) should be checked first, the observed
   rebound onset may be a failure threshold rather than a jamming one.
