0. THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND BEFORE TOUCHING ANYTHING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The author's actual question is: how do a particle's SIZE, SHAPE, SIZE DISTRIBUTION,
SURFACE POTENTIAL and MATERIAL PROPERTIES change shear thickening / jamming in DROP
IMPACT -- answered numerically/analytically, backed by simulation, backed by experiment.
The standing instruction is "as simple as possible while maintaining rigor."

The Wyart-Cates closure the DNS solves is EXACTLY covariant under a common rescaling of
(phi, phi0, phim), so the whole thing is three numbers:

    x = phi/phi0   (position)    chi = phim/phi0   (contrast)    sigma*   (onset)

    x < chi       never jams -- continuous thickening only
    chi <= x < 1  THE SHEAR-JAMMING WINDOW, width 1-chi
    x >= 1        jammed at rest

    Theta = eta(fhat=1)/eta(fhat=0) = [(1-x)/(1-x/chi)]^MP_EXPO   -> inf at x = chi

chi decides WHETHER a suspension can shear-jam; x decides WHERE it sits; sigma* decides AT
WHAT STRESS, never whether. Every particle axis is classified by which knob it turns.

⚠ SCOPE: DIL_COEFF = 0. The dilatancy term uses a fixed PHI_REF = 0.49 that does not
rescale. Every preset on record uses the default, but say it whenever you say "exact".

1. THE HIGHEST-VALUE NEXT STEP, AND IT NEEDS NO NEW CELLS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Read out/three_knobs.txt section 6 in full before planning anything about sigma*.

⚠ A sigma* DEM CAMPAIGN IS NOT RUNNABLE AS THINGS STAND. Audited this session:
packing.py and shear.py carry ONLY Cundall-Strack friction and a cohesion law with
cohesion OFF -- there is no repulsive force law -- and the shear is RATE-controlled, not
stress-controlled. Measuring sigma* directly is a CAPABILITY BUILD.

But there is a cheap route. In WC, fhat is the FRACTION OF CONTACTS whose normal force
exceeds F_rep -- i.e. the SURVIVAL FUNCTION of the contact-force distribution, which
measure.force_moments ALREADY RECORDS on every record (`force`: mean, cv, tail_slope,
n_tail). So

    fhat(sigma) = S(F_rep/<f>) = S(sigma*/sigma)

and tail_slope sets the SHAPE of the activation curve -- which is what the solver's DELTA
parameterises. Measured, controlled (lognormal, N=1500, alpha=1, mu_p=0.3, converged,
n_tail >= 200): POLYDISPERSITY FLATTENS THE TAIL, monotonically, at every rung:

    rung      CV0.0    CV0.2    CV0.5   d(0->0.5)   within-cell sd
    1e-03    -2.516   -1.800   -1.133     +1.383            0.063
    3e-05    -1.509   -1.489   -0.968     +0.541            0.075

=> the size distribution may be a THIRD kind of knob: the SHARPNESS of the transition,
not the window and not the position. THAT WOULD BE A NEW CATEGORY IN THE AXIS MAP.
