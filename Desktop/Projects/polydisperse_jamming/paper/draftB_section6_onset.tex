\subsection{Polydispersity broadens the thickening onset but barely moves it}

The drop simulations above take the onset stress $\sigma^*$ and the activation exponent $\delta$ as inputs. In dry frictional
packings, a geometric chain built on the exact Love--Weber identity predicts that raising the size-distribution width from
$\CV=0.2$ to $0.5$ at fixed number-mean radius lowers $\sigma^*$ by a factor $0.77$, through the contact density $Z\phi$.
We tested that prediction in lubricated simulations (LF-DEM, electrostatic repulsion scaled by the reduced radius, Coulomb
friction $\mu=0.3$, $N=500$, three paired configurations per width, nine stresses; pre-registered), where contacts form only
once the stress overcomes the repulsion.

The prediction fails. The Wyart--Cates activation fitted to the frictional contact number gives
$\sigma^*(0.5)/\sigma^*(0.2)=0.955$, and the half-activation stress moves by the same amount ($0.96$). The dry-packing
value $0.77$ is excluded under every error model we could construct; whether the small remaining shift is resolved depends
on whether the seed pairing is allowed to cancel the fit uncertainty, and with the fit error included it is consistent with
no shift at all. Against monodisperse spheres the ratio is $0.89$ ($95\,\%$ band $0.79$--$1.00$). What does change is the
\emph{shape} of the activation: $\delta$ falls by a factor $0.735$ ($95\,\%$ band $0.62$--$0.87$), a larger broadening than
the dry packings predict. Because the curve broadens, a single ``onset stress'' is itself readout-dependent at the level of
$-19\,\%$ to $+11\,\%$, and we quote the midpoint.

The dry chain is not wrong; it describes a different network. Its geometric factor $G=(N_c/V)\langle a_ia_j\rangle_c$ falls
by $0.70$--$0.86$ with width once the network is fully activated, reproducing the dry $0.77$, but it is width-neutral
($0.94$) at each suspension's own onset. The first contacts to form are enriched in large pairs, as Guy \emph{et al.} found
for bidisperse suspensions with a size-independent contact threshold~\cite{guy2020testing}, and more so the wider the
distribution. The size-selected onset network therefore carries the same geometric factor whatever the width, and the
onset barely moves.

For the drop this sharpens the conclusion of Sec.~\ref{sec:dns}: size polydispersity acts on an impacting drop through the
jamming window $\chi$, not through its thickening onset. The only activation parameter it changes appreciably is $\delta$,
and the drop simulations are insensitive to it at the impact stresses of interest.
