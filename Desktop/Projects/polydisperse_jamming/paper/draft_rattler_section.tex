\paragraph{Which grains rattle.}
The rattler fraction's rise with $\CV$ has a simple size-resolved form.
Suppose the number of load-bearing contacts on a grain of radius $a$ is Poisson-distributed with a mean proportional
to its surface area, $\lambda(a)=Z\,a^2/\langle a^2\rangle$, where $Z$ is the mean contact number of all grains.
A grain rattles when it has fewer than $k=\rattlerK$ contacts, so
\begin{equation}
  P(\text{rattler}\mid a)=e^{-\lambda(a)}\bigl[1+\lambda(a)\bigr],
  \qquad
  \fr=\bigl\langle e^{-\lambda(a)}[1+\lambda(a)]\bigr\rangle_{a},
  \label{eq:rattler}
\end{equation}
with the average over the number distribution.
The model has no continuous parameter. It is not iterated, unlike the rattler removal, and a Poisson count is wider
than the real one, so its two leading biases have opposite signs.
Since $\Zall=(1-\fr)\Znr$ and $\Znr$ is nearly flat, the total $\fr$ largely follows from $Z$. The content of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:rattler}) is therefore \emph{which} grains rattle: the small ones, with a probability set by their area.

Among power laws $\lambda\propto a^{p}$ with $p\in\{0,\tfrac12,1,2,3,4\}$, $p=2$ reproduces the total $\fr$ of all
702 converged isotropic packs best (rms $0.016$, against $0.128$ for the size-blind $p=0$); that choice was made on
this set.
The test it never saw is size-resolved: in 135 binary packs (size ratio 1.25--5, $\mup=0.3$), Eq.~(\ref{eq:rattler})
predicts the rattler fraction of each species separately to rms $0.017$ (small) and $0.018$ (large), where the
size-blind count gives $0.133$ and $0.346$.
It is a moderate-polydispersity law: for discrete distributions spanning a size ratio of 4 with three to five
species it under-predicts rattling by about $0.09$, most for the species just above the smallest.

So polydispersity does not restructure the backbone; it sorts grains into it by size.
A grain's chance of being load-bearing is set by its surface area, and the distribution's width matters only through
how much of the population sits at small $a$.
