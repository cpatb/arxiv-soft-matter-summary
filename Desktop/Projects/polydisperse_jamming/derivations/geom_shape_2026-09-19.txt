E1. DEGREES OF FREEDOM -- Donev's singularity is a FRICTIONLESS statement
==============================================================================
   Generalise section 1 of geom_isostatic_backbone to a rigid body with n_f
   EFFECTIVE degrees of freedom and n_c constraints per contact:
     z_iso = 2 n_f / n_c = 2*n_f/n_c

   FRICTIONLESS (n_c = 1, only the normal gap is a constraint):
     sphere:            rotations are EXACT null modes (a smooth sphere can
                        spin with no contact sliding penalty), so n_f = 3
                        -> z_iso = 6  = 2d
     spheroid / 2-sphere clump: spin about the SYMMETRY AXIS is still a null
                        mode (the body maps to itself), the other two
                        rotations are not, so n_f = 5  -> z_iso = 10
     general ellipsoid: n_f = 6  -> z_iso = 12
   The jump 6 -> 10 at infinitesimal asphericity IS Donev's singularity
   (Science 303, 990 (2004), doi:10.1126/science.1093010): the DOF count
   changes discontinuously while the SHAPE changes continuously.

   FRICTIONAL (n_c = d = 3 at a non-sliding contact, 3 - x_m on average):
     sphere:  rotations are NOT null modes -- tangential friction resists
              rolling -- so n_f = 6 already.
     2-sphere clump: rotation about the long axis moves every off-axis
              contact point tangentially, so it is resisted too; n_f = 6.
     -> z_iso = 12/(3 - x_m) for BOTH.  The count is shape-blind.

   PREDICTION E1: at mu_p = 0.3 there is NO DOF-driven jump in z_iso from
   alpha = 1 to alpha = 2.  Any measured rise must come from contact
   MULTIPLICITY or surface corrugation, not from constraint counting.

E2. FREE VOLUME -- does the void available predict the 0.6x?

   In the Furnas ideal limit (derivations/furnas_ideal_limit.md) a binary
   mixture reaches phi_max = beta(2 - beta), so the FILLING GAIN over the
   monodisperse skeleton is
       phi_max - beta = -beta*(beta - 1)  = beta(1 - beta)
   If the CV effect is void filling and nothing else changes, its size

E4. AN EFFECTIVE SIZE RATIO -- the only candidate with the right sign
==============================================================================
   HEURISTIC, stated as one: whether a fine grain can enter a coarse pore is
   set by the pore THROAT, and for a two-sphere clump the throat is set by
   the MEMBER diameter d_s, not by the equivalent-sphere diameter d_eq.
   CLAIMS_SHAPE_2026-09-18.md reports d_s/d_eq = 0.7937 at alpha = 2, so a
   fine grain of size u*d_eq sees an EFFECTIVE size ratio
       u_eff = u * (d_eq/d_s) = u / 0.7937 = 1.2599 u,
   i.e. LESS size separation, hence less filling.  Right sign by
   construction; the question is the size.

   Evaluated on the BINARY family, where the linear packing model applies
   directly.  Bidisperse CV = 0.5 has atoms 0.5 and 1.5, so u = 1/3 and the
   fines VOLUME fraction is X_s = 0.5^3/(0.5^3+1.5^3) = 0.035714.
   Interaction exponents PA = 1.02, PB = 1.5 (paper/numbers.tex fillPa/fillPb).
     p=1e-03: u = 0.3333 at alpha 1 (beta 0.5978) gain +0.00906;  u_eff = 0.4200 at alpha 2 (beta 0.5696) gain +0.00715;  ratio 0.7892
     p=3e-04: u = 0.3333 at alpha 1 (beta 0.5903) gain +0.00895;  u_eff = 0.4200 at alpha 2 (beta 0.5663) gain +0.00711;  ratio 0.7945
     p=1e-04: u = 0.3333 at alpha 1 (beta 0.5822) gain +0.00882;  u_eff = 0.4200 at alpha 2 (beta 0.5638) gain +0.00708;  ratio 0.8021

   Measured bidisperse ratio 0.648 +- 0.132.
   WHAT THIS IS AND IS NOT.  The map u -> u * d_eq/d_s is ASSERTED, not
   derived: nothing here shows the throat of a clump pore scales with the
   member diameter.  It is the only one of the three accounts that predicts
   a REDUCTION at all, and it lands in the right region for the one family
   where the linear packing model is directly applicable.  That is a SIGN
   result with a plausible magnitude, not a prediction of 0.62.
