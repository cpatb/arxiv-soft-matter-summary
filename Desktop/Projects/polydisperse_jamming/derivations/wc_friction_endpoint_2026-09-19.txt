=== S3b  THE MECHANISM: the residual friction gap above mu_p = 0.3 GROWS WITH CV ===
   W(0.3-based) = W(1.0-based) - [phi_J(0.3) - phi_J(1.0)], identically. So if the
   0.3-based window drifts while the 1.0-based one does not, the drift must live
   in that residual gap. Measured, paired on seed:
      CV  phi_J(0.3) - phi_J(1.0)        se
     0.0                 +0.01550   0.00073
     0.2                 +0.01542   0.00068
     0.5                 +0.02136   0.00146

   growth of the gap, CV 0 -> 0.5, paired: +0.00586 +- 0.00077   |t| = 7.65 vs 4.303
   [PASS] the residual friction gap above mu_p = 0.3 GROWS with CV, which is what makes the 0.3-based window appear to deform  --  |t| = 7.65
   READING: a wider size distribution takes MORE friction to reach its frictional
   limit. That is a statement about the approach to the limit, not about the
   limit itself -- and it is why a campaign that stopped at mu_p = 0.3 sees a
   CV-dependent window where the converged one does not.
   ⚠ THREE CV RUNGS, ONE FAMILY. This identifies the mechanism; it does not
     measure its shape. A mu_p ladder at fixed CV would.
   ⚠ And the sign FLIPS between the two endpoint choices: dW/dCV is negative
     on the 0.3-based window and positive (unresolved) on the 1.0-based one.

   THE POINT: two errors were CANCELLING. The un-converged endpoint created a
   deformation in the window, and the over-lenient range yardstick hid it. Fix
   either one alone and the published page's conclusion changes; fix both and it
   comes back, for a better reason than it was first given.
