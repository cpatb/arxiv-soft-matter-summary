# F4 — does the confining boundary change the impact?

**What is plotted.** Lateral spread (left) and the largest load-bearing backbone fraction
at peak compression (right), against the confinement parameter
`Λ = p_imp / p_cap = We/4`, for aggregates carved from identical parent packings and driven
into a rigid wall. One series per CV.

**The x-axis is logarithmic and its two ends are limits, not measurements.** The *caged*
boundary is `Λ = 0` (infinitely strong confinement) and the *free* boundary is `Λ = ∞`;
neither can be drawn on a log axis, so they are placed at sentinel positions 1e−3 and 1e9.
The capillary points in between are real values of `Λ`.

**Why this comparison is the point.** The lab's own field map lists as *contested*:
"intrinsic bulk transition against boundary-mediated dilatancy, unresolved **for a free
surface**, where no rigid wall supplies confinement." Applying three boundary conditions to
the *same* carved aggregate is a direct test of how much the confinement matters. The lab's
drops sit at `Λ` = 12.5 (U₀ = 1 m/s) to 200 (U₀ = 4 m/s), so surface tension is one to two
decades too weak to confine grains during impact — and the pre-registered expectation,
written before the runs, was that the capillary case would therefore sit close to the free
case. **If it does, that is the finding, not a null result to be tuned away.**

⚠ **A limitation that bounds every number here.** The granular inertial number reduces to
`I ~ d/D_agg`, independent of `U₀`. An affordable aggregate has `D_agg ~ 12 d`, giving
`I ~ 0.08`, whereas a real 2.5 mm drop of 1 µm grains has `d/D₀ = 4×10⁻⁴`. **The model is
~200× more inertial per grain than the experiment, and no affordable particle count fixes
that.** This figure compares boundary conditions at matched `I`. It does not predict the
experiment's regime boundary, and no value from it may be quoted as one.
