# Which particle property decides whether an impacting dense-suspension drop JAMS or stays inertial-dilatant?

Synthesis written overnight 2026-09-22/23 from this project's DEM, its Wyart–Cates drop DNS and the
closed forms in `derivations/`. **No new bench data.** Every number cites the graded artifact it
came from; everything else is marked as reasoning or hypothesis.

## 1. The answer in one paragraph

The drop can only jam if its packing sits inside the **shear-jamming window**, `χ ≤ x < 1`
(`x = φ/φ₀`, `χ = φ_m/φ₀`); outside it the suspension can only thicken, however hard it is hit.
Inside it, a material point jams once the impact stress exceeds
`σ_jam = κ d^(m−2)/Q_{1−f_c}(ĝ)`, `f_c = (1−x)/(1−χ)`. So the particle properties divide into
three kinds: those that decide **whether** jamming is possible at all (they move χ: friction,
shape, and — the author's hypothesis — **aggregation of one primary into doublets and
multiplets**); those that decide **where** a weighed-out suspension sits (they move x: the size
distribution, most strongly); and those that decide only **at what impact stress** (size and
surface potential, through σ\*). Of the window-movers, aggregation is the one set by the
preparation of a given batch, and it moves the window, the onset and the activation sharpness
together.

## 3. The drop

- **Activation at impact behaves as STRESS-controlled** although the solver writes it in rate form:
  `jamwin` P2 (one informative pair) and `jamwin2` (censored bounds rising with δ by > ×3.16).
  Hypothesis: an impact fixes the momentum flux, so η↑ ⇒ γ̇↓ at ~fixed stress.
- **Outside the window a drop still stiffens**: Θ = [(1−x)/(1−x/χ)]^2 at x 0.85 with the CLEAN μ 0.3 χ
  goes 5.46 → 7.78 (w₂ 0 → 0.25) and → 97.7 at w₂ 1 (still outside). The ×15 (13.6 → 205) quoted
  earlier used a predicted χ 0.009 from x and is ill-conditioned. That this thickening is what
  lowers the spread before jamming is a hypothesis.
- **S-scope:** every σ_jam statement here assumes stress-controlled activation (the bullet above).
- **The regime map (A3, `kmermap`, all channels from the clean μ_p 0.3 DEM)**: at x 0.88, outside the
  singlets' window and inside the doublets', pure doublets JAM (J 0.058 / 0.196 at U₀ 2 / 4) where
  singlets cannot (R1 PASS). At x 0.92, inside both windows, doublets jam more (0.636 vs 0.138 at U₀ 4;
  the other pairs are censored by L9 deaths). R4 PASS on the 4 of 9 (x, U₀) pairs where both arms survived: doublets never spread further there.
  **The footprint does not discriminate**: the largest spread cut (−31 % in Rg) is at x 0.85, where
  nothing can jam. `PREREG_KMERMAP` RESULT, figure K3.

## 5. What only the bench can decide (no new bench data was used here)
1. **Count doublets at 0 and 100 mM salt.** Equal counts ⇒ the salt series is a pure σ\* (screening)
   lever; rising counts ⇒ it also opens the window, and the arrested-state constancy becomes a puzzle.
2. **Fractionate one batch and remix to known doublet fractions** at fixed φ: the drop should switch
   from spreading to arrest past `w*(x)` (closed form, `multiplet_knobs` §2), and the rheometer's
   χ = φ_m/φ₀ should fall by the ratio in §2 of this file — a scale-free prediction.
3. **Sintered vs salt-bridged doublets**: equal behaviour ⇒ no breakage during impact.

## 6. Caveats that travel with every number
The DEM φ axis is not the bench's (lab φ 0.44 sits below every DEM window); only ratios and x
transfer. μ_p 3 clump packs lock in interpenetration (excluded). The DNS runs at L9, where deep jams
die at the viscosity floor. The σ\*/δ transfer from sheared DEM packs to the DNS assumes the drop's
contacts are sheared at the steady state the packs reach.
