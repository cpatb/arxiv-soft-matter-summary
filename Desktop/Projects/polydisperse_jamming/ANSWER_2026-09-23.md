# Which particle property decides whether an impacting dense-suspension drop JAMS or stays inertial-dilatant?

Synthesis written overnight 2026-09-22/23 from this project's DEM, its Wyart–Cates drop DNS and the
closed forms in `derivations/`. **No new bench data.** Every number cites the graded artifact it
came from; everything else is marked as reasoning or hypothesis.

## 1. The answer in one paragraph

The drop can only jam if its packing sits inside the **shear-jamming window**, `χ ≤ x < 1`
(`x = φ/φ₀`, `χ = φ_m/φ₀`); outside it the suspension can only thicken, however hard it is hit.
Inside it, a material point jams once the impact stress exceeds
`σ_jam = κ d^(m−2)/Q_{1−f_c}(ĝ)`, `f_c = (1−x)/(1−χ)`. So the particle properties divide into
three kinds: those that decide **whether** jamming is possible at all (they move χ: friction,
shape, and — the author's hypothesis — **aggregation of one primary into doublets and
multiplets**); those that decide **where** a weighed-out suspension sits (they move x: the size
distribution, most strongly); and those that decide only **at what impact stress** (size and
surface potential, through σ\*). Of the window-movers, aggregation is the one set by the
preparation of a given batch. In the DEM it moves the window, the onset and the activation
sharpness together. **In the DNS drop it acts almost entirely through the window.** At realistic
friction (μ_p 0.3, clean) pure doublets widen the window ×1.49, which switches a fixed-φ drop at
x 0.88 from "cannot jam" to jammed (`kmermap` R1). The doublets' χ ALONE reproduces that and the
whole footprint change (`kmerdecomp`), while their lower onset and softer activation move the
footprint by ≤ 3 %. Within the model, then, the answer to "which knob decides jamming vs
inertial-dilatant" is **whichever knob moves χ relative to x**, and one-primary aggregation is the
one a preparation protocol controls. Stress-controlled scope, L9, DEM → DNS transfer, no bench data.

## 3. The drop

- **Activation at impact behaves as STRESS-controlled** although the solver writes it in rate form:
  `jamwin` P2 (one informative pair; registered PASS). `jamwin2`'s registered verdict is
  UNDETERMINED; its bounds rising with δ by > ×3.16 are the source's own DESCRIPTIVE, non-registered
  reading and are not on the same footing.
  Hypothesis: an impact fixes the momentum flux, so η↑ ⇒ γ̇↓ at ~fixed stress.
- **Outside the window a drop still stiffens**: Θ = [(1−x)/(1−x/χ)]^2 at x 0.85 with the CLEAN μ 0.3 χ
  goes 5.46 → 7.78 (w₂ 0 → 0.25) and → 97.7 at w₂ 1 (still outside). The ×15 (13.6 → 205) quoted
  earlier used a predicted χ 0.009 from x and is ill-conditioned. That this thickening, not the
  onset, is what lowers the spread outside the window is now TESTED: `kmerdecomp` D3 puts the whole
  x 0.85 footprint cut on the window channel (ln −0.372 of −0.371).
- **S-scope:** every σ_jam statement here assumes stress-controlled activation (the bullet above).
- **The regime map (A3, `kmermap`, all channels from the clean μ_p 0.3 DEM)**: at x 0.88, outside the
  singlets' window and inside the doublets', pure doublets JAM (J 0.058 / 0.196 at U₀ 2 / 4) where
  singlets cannot (R1 PASS). At x 0.92, inside both windows, doublets jam more (0.636 vs 0.138 at U₀ 4;
  the other pairs are censored by L9 deaths at the default cap; at EPS_MIN 0.03 all 18 cells live and
  doublet > singlet holds at every U₀, R2′ 3/3). R4 PASS on the 4 of 9 (x, U₀) pairs where both arms survived: doublets never spread further there.
  **The footprint does not discriminate**: the largest spread cut (−31 % in Rg) is at x 0.85, where
  nothing can jam. `PREREG_KMERMAP` RESULT, figure K3.

## 5. What only the bench can decide (no new bench data was used here)
1. **Count doublets at 0 and 100 mM salt.** Equal counts ⇒ the salt series is a pure σ\* (screening)
   lever; rising counts ⇒ it also opens the window, and the arrested-state constancy becomes a puzzle.
2. **Fractionate one batch and remix to known doublet fractions** at fixed φ. The rheometer's
   χ = φ_m/φ₀ should fall by ×0.950 from singlets to pure doublets (clean μ_p 0.3; `kmer`), a
   scale-free prediction. The drop should switch to JAMMING past `w*(x)` (`multiplet_knobs` §2).
   ⚠ **That switch cannot be read from the footprint**: in the DNS the largest spread reduction
   occurs where nothing jams (`kmermap`). The bench needs an observable that separates a jammed
   solid from a thickened liquid. **The DNS offers one candidate: apex recoil** (`PREREG_JAMSIG`).
   After the apex height reaches its minimum, drops that cannot jam rise again by h/D0 ≥ 0.125, and
   jammed drops by ≤ 0.078 (0 = no rise of one grid quantum), on all 26 healthy cells seen. `PREREG_EPSSIG` shows what carries it: the
   separation holds at a 10× stiffer jammed state and falls to a quarter (0.125 → 0.031) at a 10×
   softer one (G1, G2 PASS),
   so recoil reads the jammed-vs-thickened viscosity contrast, and a jammed core ≥ 10⁴ η_s stiff
   does not retract. Its out-of-sample and L10 tests were blocked by solver deaths, so it is a
   well-understood candidate, not a validated signature. A high-speed side view would
   read it directly.
3. **Sintered vs salt-bridged doublets**: equal behaviour ⇒ no breakage during impact.

## 6. Caveats that travel with every number
The DEM φ axis is not the bench's (lab φ 0.44 sits below every DEM window); only ratios and x
transfer. μ_p 3 clump packs lock in interpenetration (excluded). The DNS runs at L9, where deep jams
die at the viscosity floor. The σ\*/δ transfer from sheared DEM packs to the DNS assumes the drop's
contacts are sheared at the steady state the packs reach.
**In the DNS a jammed region's viscosity IS the regularisation cap** (`wc_drop_impact.c`: ε = 1 − φ/φ_J
floored at EPS_MIN 0.01, η_max = 10⁴ η_s). A fully activated but unjammed region can sit within a
factor ~1 of that cap (w₂ 0.25 at x 0.85: ε 0.0105). So J (f̂ > f_c, the closure's own criterion)
does not involve the cap, but the dynamics do. **Measured (`PREREG_EPSSIG`):** J's MAGNITUDE moves ×5.9
over one decade of EPS_MIN (0.096 / 0.220 / 0.569 at 0.003 / 0.01 / 0.03, w₂ 0.75); the J+/J0 class
held. So every quoted DNS J is a default-cap number. **`PREREG_KMERMAPE03` then re-ran the whole regime
map at EPS_MIN 0.03: 18/18 healthy, R1′–R4′ all PASS (incl. R2 3/3 and R3 3/3, which deaths had left
incomplete at the default), and all 12 cells healthy at both caps keep their class.** Classes,
kmermap's orderings and the kmerdecomp channel split (Amendment 3: D1″, D3″ PASS, additivity
residual 0.0001) are cap-robust over 1.1e3–1e4 η_s; only the kmerdrop ladder (μ_m 3 predicted χ) was
not re-run. The
x 0.88 kmermap class was then TESTED under the cap: doublets jam there at EPS_MIN 0.003, 0.01 and
0.03 (the 0.003 cell as a clean lower bound 0.082 before an L9 death), so R1 is cap-robust; J's
magnitude is not, and not even in a consistent direction.
