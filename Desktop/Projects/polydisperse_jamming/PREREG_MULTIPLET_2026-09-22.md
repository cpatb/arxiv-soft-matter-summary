# PREREG — `multiplet`: does aggregation pack the way the closure says?

Written 2026-09-22, **before any multiplet cell exists**. Blind predictions are in
`out/predict_multiplet.txt`, generated by `predict_multiplet.py` and committed with this
file. **No number in §3 may be regenerated after cells land.**

Derivation: `derivations/multiplet_psd_2026-09-22.md`.
Implementation: `psd.MultipletPSD`, family `multiplet:f<D_F>:n<N1>/<N2>/...`.

---

## 1. Why this campaign, and what it is NOT

The multiplet family is the one a real suspension has: one primary particle, and a width
that is a **readout of the aggregation state** rather than a knob. The derivation splits
aggregation's effect on jamming into two levers — a closed-form trapped-fluid factor `Λ`
and a measured polydispersity gain `G` — and concludes that for realistic aggregates `Λ`
wins. **This campaign tests the `G` half only.**

> ⚠ **`Λ` IS NOT TESTABLE BY THIS CAMPAIGN AND NO GATE HERE CLAIMS TO TEST IT.** The DEM
> packs **effective spheres**, so it never sees the solid-vs-excluded volume distinction
> `Λ` is built from. A sphere pack cannot distinguish a `k`-mer from a big monomer. Only
> a **clump** campaign can, and that is §6.

What *is* tested: whether `mixture_model`'s calibrated filling functional — fitted on 16
binary compositions at size ratios 2 and 3, and already shown to transfer to three
continuous families — also predicts a **fourth** PSD shape it has never seen.

**Grid.** `mu_p = 0.3`, `N = 1500`, seeds {1,2,3}, the standard four-rung `p̂` ladder,
`PJ_MAX_ITER = 1000000`. Twelve compositions (blocks A, B, C below) × 3 seeds × 4 rungs =
**144 records**, tag `multiplet`.

> The raised cap is **not** optional and is not a change of protocol to be argued about
> later: this session established that `MAX_ITER = 200000` censors exactly the CV > 0
> cells (`PREREG_MUSTARCAP_2026-09-22.md`), and `wcbidicap`'s C1 identity gate has already
> shown 58/58 that raising it cannot move a cell that converged.

## 2. Gates

### M0 — WIRING. **Primary; gates everything.**

The family string must reach the packing unchanged, and the recorded `cv_realised` must
equal the closed form.

> **REGISTERED:** for every multiplet record, `|cv_realised − CV_closed_form| < 1e-6`,
> where the closed form is `psd.MultipletPSD(...).cv` and `cv_realised` is what
> `packing.measure` reports from the **grains actually placed**.
>
> ⚠ This gate can fail for a real reason: `DiscretePSD.class_counts` rounds to whole
> grains, so at `N = 1500` a class with number fraction 0.003 gets 4 or 5 grains, not
> 4.5. **If it fails, the tolerance is not widened** — the realised composition is
> reported and every prediction is recomputed at the realised `n_k` before any other
> gate is read.

Also registered: **no two compositions may collide on one output filename**
(`phase2_phiJ.fs_safe` is injective on this grid — pinned by `test_multiplet.py`).

### M1 — LADDER HEALTH.

> **REGISTERED: 144 of 144 converge at `1e6`**, and `SPAN/SD ≥ 2` on every
> `(composition)` group. Any group below 2 has its `φ_J` **withdrawn**, not flagged —
> the 2026-09-19 finding is that an extrapolated `φ_J` from a ladder that barely moves is
> meaningless rather than imprecise. The fit-free matched-rung channel is quoted instead.

### M2 — BLOCK A: the `d_f` ladder, composition fixed at 70/20/10.

Opening the aggregates at fixed `⟨k⟩` widens the PSD: `CV = 0.1408 → 0.2448` as
`d_f = 3.0 → 1.8`.

> **REGISTERED, blind:** `φ` at `p̂ = 1e-3` is **monotone increasing as `d_f` falls**,
> and the span across the block is `0.0156 ± ?` — predicted **7.4 × PHI_SCALE**, so it is
> comfortably resolvable. Individual predictions (refit closure):
>
> | `d_f` | CV | predicted `φ` | textbook `φ` |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 1.8 | 0.2448 | **0.6170** | 0.6351 |
> | 2.1 | 0.2068 | **0.6102** | 0.6249 |
> | 2.5 | 0.1712 | **0.6050** | 0.6165 |
> | 3.0 | 0.1408 | **0.6014** | 0.6103 |
>
> **Tolerance: ±2 × PHI_SCALE = ±0.0042 per point.** A miss is a miss and the tolerance
> is not widened. Both calibrations are tabulated **because the refit and the textbook
> exponents differ by up to 8.6 × PHI_SCALE here**, so this block also discriminates
> between them — which the three continuous families already did, in the same direction.

### M3 — BLOCK B: the `⟨k⟩` ladder at fixed `d_f = 2.1`. **The informative one.**

> **REGISTERED, blind, and NON-MONOTONE:** `φ` rises then **falls** —
> `0.5985 → 0.6102 → 0.6165 → 0.6090` at `⟨k⟩ = 1.10, 1.40, 1.70, 2.10`.
>
> The turnover is the prediction. A monotone closure, a CV-only closure, or a closure
> that simply tracked `⟨k⟩` would all get this wrong: note that `CV` is **also**
> non-monotone here (0.1129, 0.2068, 0.2189, 0.2044) while `Λ` rises throughout, so
> **`Λ` and `φ` move in opposite directions on the last step** — which is exactly the
> two-lever competition, visible inside one block.
>
> **Registered outcome (T): the argmax of `φ(⟨k⟩)` is at `⟨k⟩ = 1.70.`**
> Registered alternative: a monotone rise, which would mean the closure's filling optimum
> is in the wrong place for this family.
> **Tolerance ±2 × PHI_SCALE per point**; the argmax claim needs the peak to clear its
> neighbours by more than the seed-paired s.e. **and** more than `√2·PHI_SCALE/√3`.

### M4 — BLOCK C: MATCHED-CV PAIRS. **The discriminator.**

Four pairs matched in number `CV` to ≤ 4 × 10⁻⁴ with `d_f` differing by ≥ 0.5.

> **A closure written in `CV` alone predicts exactly ZERO for every row.** The calibrated
> closure predicts, blind:
>
> | pair | CV | `d_f` (a → b) | predicted `Δφ` | in PHI_SCALE | textbook |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | 0.1543 | 1.6 → 2.5 | **−0.0049** | −2.3 | −1.9 |
> | 2 | 0.1567 | 1.8 → 2.5 | **−0.0065** | −3.1 | −0.4 |
> | 3 | 0.1718 | 1.8 → 2.5 | **−0.0058** | −2.7 | −0.1 |
> | 4 | 0.2465 | 1.6 → 2.1 | **−0.0080** | −3.8 | −8.2 |
>
> **REGISTERED: all four `Δφ` are NEGATIVE and at least 2 × PHI_SCALE in magnitude.**
> The sign is uniform and is the falsifiable part; a single positive pair refutes it.
>
> ⚠ **The pairs were selected on CV agreement and `d_f` separation ONLY**, never on the
> size of the predicted difference — selecting on the response would be choosing the
> design from the answer. Pairs whose predicted `|Δφ| < 2 × PHI_SCALE` were dropped as
> **untestable by this protocol**, which is a statement about the protocol, and that rule
> was fixed before the search ran (`predict_multiplet.matched_cv_pairs`).

## 2b. AMENDMENT 1 — two registered CLAIMS are wrong; the PREDICTIONS are unchanged

Written 2026-09-22 after referee pass 2, **before any cell runs**. Neither the grid, the
tolerances, nor a single predicted number in §2 is moved. What is withdrawn is what two
of the gates were said to *discriminate*.

### 🔴 A1.1 — M3's claim that Block B discriminates a CV-only closure is FALSE

M3 says the non-monotone turnover would be got wrong by "a monotone closure, a CV-only
closure, or a closure that simply tracked `⟨k⟩`". **The CV-only part is false, and Block
B's own numbers say so.** Ordering its four points by `CV` and by predicted `φ`:

| `⟨k⟩` | 1.10 | 1.40 | 1.70 | 2.10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CV | 0.1129 | 0.2068 | **0.2189** | 0.2044 |
| predicted `φ` | 0.5985 | 0.6102 | **0.6165** | 0.6090 |

Both orderings are `1.10 < 2.10 < 1.40 < 1.70` — **identical**. So *any* strictly
increasing function of `CV` alone reproduces Block B's whole shape, **including the
turnover at `⟨k⟩ = 1.70`**. `CV` is itself non-monotone in `⟨k⟩` here, which is what the
turnover is made of.

> **M3's per-point predictions stand and its tolerance is unchanged.** What is withdrawn
> is the interpretive claim: **Block B does not discriminate a CV-only surrogate.** That
> job belongs to Block C alone, which is exactly what Block C was designed for. This is
> the repository's own "a discriminator that passes BOTH candidates has discriminated
> nothing" lesson, caught here before the cells ran rather than after.

### 🔴 A1.2 — M4's uniform negative sign is a property of the SELECTED SUBGROUP

M4 says "all four `Δφ` are NEGATIVE … the sign is uniform and is the falsifiable part; a
single positive pair refutes it." Regenerating the **full candidate population** that
`matched_cv_pairs` searches, rather than only the four it keeps:

| | count |
|---|---|
| CV-matched pairs with `d_f` gap ≥ 0.5 | **80** |
| of those, **positive** `Δφ` | **29 (36 %)** |
| clearing the 2 × `PHI_SCALE` floor | 13 (**9 negative, 4 positive**) |
| `d_f` gap of the 4 positive resolvable pairs | **all 0.5** |
| `d_f` gap of the kept 4 | 0.7 and 0.9 |

**The sign is genuinely mixed in the population, and the selection rule sorts by WIDEST
`d_f` gap first** — which in this grid picks only from the negative subgroup. The rule was
fixed before the search and is not tuned to the response, so this is not p-hacking; but it
**is** selection on a variable (gap width) that turns out to correlate with the sign, which
is precisely what §2 M4 claims to have avoided.

> **The four registered `Δφ` values and their ±tolerances are UNCHANGED and still blind.**
> What is withdrawn is the framing. The correct registered statement is:
>
> **REGISTERED (amended): the four selected pairs' `Δφ` are as tabulated in §2 M4, all
> negative. A positive result on any of the four refutes the closure's prediction FOR
> THAT PAIR.** It does **not** establish that `Δφ < 0` is a general property of the
> aggregation axis — the model itself says it is not, at 36 % positive over the
> population — and no such claim may be made from this block.
>
> ⚠ The *discriminating* content of Block C is undamaged and is the reason it exists: a
> **CV-only closure predicts exactly ZERO for every one of these pairs**, positive or
> negative, so any resolved non-zero `Δφ` of either sign refutes a CV-only description.
> The sign was never load-bearing for that; it was load-bearing only for the stronger
> claim, which is now withdrawn.

## 3. What would make this campaign worthless

- **M0 fails** → the family string is not reaching the packing and nothing may be read.
- **The realised `n_k` differs from the requested one** on any cell by more than one
  grain per class → the control variable is not controlled; report the realised
  composition and recompute every prediction against it before reading M2–M4.
- **Blocks A and B both pass while C fails** → the closure tracks width and nothing else,
  which is a *result about the closure*, not a failure of the campaign.
- **`SPAN/SD < 2` on the low-CV compositions** → the same ladder degeneracy that bit the
  frictional cells; the fit-free channel is then the only one quoted, and the `φ_J`
  column is withdrawn rather than flagged.

## 4. What is NOT being run, and why

- **No `μ_p` ladder.** One friction, `0.3`, because the closure is calibrated there and
  because this session has just shown the higher frictions cap. Whether `G` depends on
  friction is `multiplet_wc.py` §2's question and `mustarcap`'s cells, not this one's.
- **No new seeds.** `n = 3` is the design.
- **No `Λ` gate** (§1).
- **No `σ*` claim.** Not this campaign's variable; see `multiplet_wc.py` §4.

## 5. Cost

144 records at `PJ_MAX_ITER = 1e6`, 12 workers. The comparable `wcbidicap` block was 72
records at the same cap. ⚠ **Cells at the low-`d_f` end have size ratio up to 2.0 and are
the slower ones**, so this is expected to be longer than `wcbidicap`, not the same.

## 6. REGISTERED SEPARATELY, NOT RUN HERE: the clump control that tests assumption A1

The whole campaign above replaces each multiplet by **one sphere**. A real doublet is a
dumbbell. The control that separates the SIZE consequence of aggregation from its SHAPE
consequence is the **same `n_k` run as rigid clumps of `k` touching monomers**, which
`shape.py` already has the machinery for.

⚠ Two known obstacles, both recorded in this repository and neither solved:
`utils.porosity()` double-counts clump overlap (alpha-dependent, up to a factor 2), and
`packing.measure` supplies per-grain volumes for **sphere packs only**. So the clump arm
needs the volume bookkeeping fixed first. **It is registered as the next question and
explicitly not attempted here**, because a clump campaign run on a porosity that
double-counts would produce a confident wrong answer rather than an error.
