1. The range the bracket has to cover

   From the 15 Herschel-Bulkley fits in hb_fits_etd2020.csv (Biermann &
   Mesecke 2024, ETD2020, 0.1-0.9 wt% x 30-50 uL TEA):
     tau_y spans 0.80 to 41.11 Pa, median 16.80
     and it is NOT monotone in wt%: 0.3 wt% at 50 uL TEA gives 39.1 Pa
     against 26.5 Pa at 0.9 wt%.
   That non-monotonicity is the whole argument for measuring rather than
   labelling: the neutraliser dose matters as much as the carbomer, and the
   11AUG26 recordings record only the carbomer.

   A bath made to any of those recipes therefore needs a bracket that spans
   at least 0.8 to 41.1 Pa -- a factor of 51.

3. A ladder, and what it costs

   Sort every (material, diameter) by the tau_y it marks, then walk the list
   keeping only beads that advance the bracket by less than a factor of 1.5.
   The bracket a ladder returns is the RATIO between adjacent rungs, so the
   rung spacing IS the measurement's resolution -- there is no separate
   precision to quote.

   15 beads, 6 materials, covering 0.42 to 92.87 Pa.
   Worst-case bracket: a factor of 1.60, i.e. tau_y known to
   +/- 23% of its midpoint.
   The campaign document asks for 'within roughly a factor of 2'. This is
   better than that, and it is better because the ladder mixes DENSITIES --
   a single-material ladder is limited to the catalogue's diameter steps.

4. What this measurement cannot do, stated before it is run

   - [UNVERIFIED] Y_c = 0.143 is written from memory as Beris et al.'s
     creeping-flow result for a BINGHAM fluid, and is not resolved to a DOI.
     The project's own record says only 'near 0.14'. Resolve it first.
     Carbopol is Herschel-Bulkley with n = 0.40 to 0.63 in these very fits, and
     published Y_c for a sphere in an HB fluid moves with n. So the ABSOLUTE
     tau_y carries a systematic on top of the bracket above. Reported values of
     Y_c across the literature span roughly 0.12 to 0.20, which is +/- 25% on
     tau_y -- LARGER than the bracket the ladder gives. The bracket is not the
     dominant error and should not be quoted as if it were.
   - It is a static test. It says nothing about K or n, and the drop-impact
     problem needs both: at impact rates the HB stress is dominated by the
     K gamma_dot^n term, not by tau_y.
   - Thixotropy and ageing are not controlled. Carbopol's apparent yield stress
     depends on rest time, and a bead dropped into a freshly stirred bath and
     one dropped into a rested bath are not the same measurement. Fix a rest
     time and record it.
   - Wall effects: keep the bead diameter below about a tenth of the container
     diameter, or the bracket shifts.

   So this unblocks the 73 recordings by putting them on an axis with an
   honest +/- 25%, which is enough to ORDER them and enough to decide whether
   the arm earns a rheometer slot. It is not enough to publish a tau_y.

5. Protocol, for the half hour

   Fix the rest time and the container. Drop the ladder from the top of the
   list downward, one bead per rung, and record HELD or SANK after a fixed
   wait -- 10 minutes is longer than any settling time at these stresses and
   short enough to sit through. The bracket is [largest held, smallest sunk].
   A non-monotone result -- a bead that sinks while a larger one holds -- means
   the bath is not uniform or the rest time is not controlled, and is the one
   outcome that invalidates the run rather than widening it. Record every
   bead, including the ones that behave, so that check is possible at all.
