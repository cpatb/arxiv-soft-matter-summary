### 1b. The AIBN number — still open, and the bench card's fix does not transfer

This is the one that decides whether Tuesday is interpretable.

The bench card's **21 mg** was computed as 1 wt% of **2.0 mL** TPM (the Master Recipe).

| basis | for 0.5 mL TPM (0.52 g) |
|---|---|

which matters much less in a crosslinked organosilica network.

**Better, if you can find one more vessel:** after Gate 2, split the emulsion in two
and dose one half at the sheet's rate (0.5 mg / 50 mL) and one at 2 wt% (5 mg / 50 mL).
One emulsion, one pre-stir, one variable. Gate 3 then *answers* the AIBN question

### The original design note, kept for the record

### The observable

Per drop, on each length channel — contact **`Baseline w/d0`** and height
**`Center h/r0`**, both already in the side height CSVs — define the **unjamming
progress**

    Φ(t) = ( X(t) − X_jam ) / ( X_final − X_jam )

Φ = 0 at the jammed plateau, Φ = 1 at the final deposit. Both levels are already
measured (`d_jam`, `d_final`, and the h equivalents), so this adds no new fitting.

Then the landmarks are exactly what you described: **t25, t50, t75, t90** — the first
sustained crossing of each level. Each one **points at a frame you can open and look
at**, which puts them in tier (b) of your own scalar rule, not tier (c). They are not
fits. That distinction is what rescues t50/t95, which got cut earlier for being
un-inspectable — a *fitted* t50 is un-inspectable, a *crossing* t50 is a frame number.

### The one correction to your proposal

**Do not normalize by record length.** `t / t_total` is the trap: records here run
from ~90 µs to 6 s, and a drop whose recording ended early would read as "fully
recovered" — the same failure mode as the fitted-g QA gate and the common-time
statistic that a died-early cell moved. Normalize by an **internal, measured**
landmark: **t / t50.** And carry censoring explicitly — a record that ends before
Φ = 0.9 gives a t90 that is a **lower bound**, reported as one, never dropped.

### The two panels that answer her question

**Panel A — does salt just rescale the clock, or change the shape?**
Plot Φ vs t/t50 for every drop, colour = salt, marker = φ. If the curves overlie,
salt is a pure clock and the whole effect is one number per condition (a shift factor
a_salt — literally the time–concentration superposition a rheologist already knows how
to read). If they do **not** overlie, salt changes the *shape* of the unjamming, and
that is a statement no flow curve can make. Either outcome is a result.

**Panel B — the clock itself.** t25/t50/t75 vs salt at matched u₀, band B or C.

**The shape index, model-free:** `log10(t75/t25)` — the width in decades of the
recovery. This is what KWW's β was trying to measure, without an optimizer to rail
against its bound.

### Why this is the strongest version of the "rheometers can't do this" claim

You already have the sharpest form of it and it is under-used: **the contact channel
and the height channel relax on different clocks.** On the 100 mM drops the surface
caps in ~2–3 s while the contact line stays pinned past 6 s — more than a decade apart,
on the same drop, at the same instant. A rheometer returns one number for the whole
sample. Two lengths on one drop disagreeing by a decade *is* the spatiotemporal
argument, and Φ(t) makes it a single figure: two curves per drop, and the gap between
them is the observable.

Anchor it with the Newtonian control — it has no jam, so Φ is undefined for it. That
is the discriminator, and per §2g the control is already on disk and needs no hand
annotation.

## 3. The 50 mM LiCl session

Your read is right, and it is more specific than "reprocess it." The 50 mM point is
non-monotone in **two** places — `d_jam` (0.544, below 100 mM's 0.612) and the fitted
onset (471 s⁻¹, tied with 10 mM's 479) — while `u_front` itself is perfectly monotone
(8990 mm/s, between 10 and 100 mM). So the raw front tracking is fine; what is off is
the quantities that divide by a segmented length.

Two candidate causes, and they are separable by construction:

- **Segmentation threshold.** 50 mM is the only session in band B/C thresholded at
  **120**; 0 and 100 mM are at 100. A threshold shift moves every segmented length,
  which moves `d_jam` and `d₀` — and γ̇ = u₀/d₀, so it moves the onset too.
- **Calibration.** JUL is 37 px/mm vs JUN's 28. But this **cannot** be the cause of
  the onset tie: a px/mm error rescales y and leaves an x-intercept alone. It *can*
  explain the 16% steeper slope (though 37/28 = 1.32 does not fully account for +17%).

**So: reprocess 50 mM at threshold 100 to match 0 and 100 mM, and re-derive.** That is
the direct test and it discriminates cleanly — if the tie resolves, it was the
threshold; if it survives, the calibration or the physics owns it. It is unattended
compute; start it Monday morning and read it Friday. Separately, re-verify the 37 px/mm
value against the session's calibration frame — it is cheap and it is load-bearing for
every slope in that session.

🆕 **The ladder built on 09 AUG sharpens this into a prediction.** On the crossing
*times*, 50 mM sits correctly between 10 and 100 mM at every robust level — it is
monotone on the clock. It is non-monotone only in `d_jam` and in γ̇_c, which are a
level and a level-derived quantity. **The anomaly is in the measured widths, not in
the timing**, which is what a segmentation-threshold difference produces and what a
px/mm error provably cannot (a calibration error cannot move an x-intercept). So:
if the threshold is the cause, reprocessing should move `d_jam` and γ̇_c into order
while leaving these crossing times almost unchanged. That is a falsifiable outcome
rather than a fishing trip.

Supporting hint that it is processing and not physics: under the fixed-slope shift
construction the ladder **is** monotone — 632 / 520 / 340 / 190.

## 4. ζ on the salted aliquot

Worth doing, and three separate to-dos, not one: **instrument access**, **training
refresher**, **cells/consumables**. Email all three Monday morning — the lead time is
the whole risk.

Three things that will bite:

- **Dilute into matched LiCl, never into water.** ζ is measured dilute; diluting a
  100 mM sample into MilliQ changes the ionic strength you are trying to measure. Use
  the sample's own supernatant, or LiCl at the same concentration.
- **100 mM LiCl is ~11 mS/cm.** That is high enough to Joule-heat a standard folded
  capillary cell and degrade the electrodes. Drop the applied voltage, use a
  diffusion-barrier or dip-cell method, and expect the 100 mM point to be the noisiest.
- **Read the trend, not the absolute at 100 mM.** Screened ζ is small and imprecise.
  The 0/10/50/100 mM ladder is the measurement; the endpoint alone is not.

On gelation at high concentration: ζ won't tell you directly. **DLS on the same cell**
will — an apparent size well above 800 nm, or a rising PDI, is aggregation. Take the
size distribution while the sample is loaded; it is free.
