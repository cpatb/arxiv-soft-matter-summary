## 2. The highest-leverage Tier-B calls — review these first

Ranked by how much rides on you agreeing with them.

### ① chicago H2: crosswind σ_y → vertical σ_z  *(confidence: high diagnosis, medium replacement)*
The document correctly calls a highway a continuous line source, then fitted concentration fall-off along a *perpendicular transect* to a Gaussian in the **crosswind** coordinate. For an effectively infinite line source there is no crosswind fall-off — the source is translation-invariant along its own axis. A perpendicular transect measures decay with **downwind** distance, governed by **vertical** dispersion σ_z(x). The old fit would have returned a number that is neither σ_y nor σ_z and comparable to no Pasquill–Gifford curve.
**Your call:** I adopted the standard **infinite**-line form. The alternative — treat the road as a **finite** line and integrate along it — keeps a real crosswind dependence and would justify a two-parameter fit. Choose deliberately.
**Bonus finding:** the old pipeline also had two latent statistical bugs, now fixed — fitting a Gaussian to a monotonically decaying transect is ill-conditioned (`curve_fit` would return arbitrary widths), and a power law fitted without a background term biases the exponent low, corrupting the very PG comparison H2 exists to make.

### ② paper1: "orders of magnitude" → "a factor ~2–10"  *(confidence: high)*
The paper's own predicted values are τ_KWW = 300–600 ms (TPM) vs 50–200 ms (cornstarch) — a ratio of ~1.5× to ~12×. "Orders of magnitude" reads as ≥100×. Its own companion referee review flags exactly this. Softening makes the sub-claim falsifiable instead of unreachable.
**But note where I did *not* follow the audit:** sub-claim 1's "an order of magnitude *sooner*" for t_jam was **left alone**, because the paper's own Figure-4 key result states `t_jam^TPM/t_jam^cornstarch ~ 0.1`. There the phrase is exactly right. The June audit lumped the two together; only the τ_KWW claim was overstated.

### ③ bidet: retracted 16 mm → 26 mm, and 3.4 ms / 8 ms → 13 ms  *(confidence: high arithmetic, ambiguous input)*
Three numbers in the policy paper traced to three different cleaning radii. At L = 0.016 m and U = 2 m/s, t = L/U = **8 ms** — so the Conclusions value tracked the **retracted** 16 mm, while the body's 3.4 ms implies L ≈ 6.8 mm. All three now trace to one L = r_clean = 26 mm, the value the file's own revision header declares correct: Pe = 5.2×10⁸, t = 13 ms.
**Your call:** the audit flagged L as genuinely ambiguous between the *ideal* 26 mm and a *realistic* ~6.5 mm standoff-corrected radius. If realistic is intended, all three move together: L = 0.0065 m → Pe ≈ 1.3×10⁸ → t ≈ 3.3 ms — suspiciously close to the original 3.4 ms, which may be exactly where that number came from.
