# The downstream chain is regenerated, and the verdict survives it

**2026-08-29.** `P4_Coupling/landing_stats.npz` was dated 2026-07-26 03:19 against a
`monte_carlo.py` dated 14:19 the same day. Eleven hours older than the code that writes
it, it carried none of the schema `P4_Coupling/README.md` documents and, worst of all,
**no record of which gust model built it**, which mattered because GATE G1 had just
measured the older `temporal` model as a 74% overstatement of turbulence-induced
dispersion. `Q.npz` derived from that file, and every manuscript macro derives from `Q`.

It has been regenerated.

## What was run, and what was deliberately not

`SeanEarl_DiscFlight/rerunMonteCarloOnward.sh` runs `runDownstream.sh` steps 3-8 and
**skips steps 1-2**, so `P3_CFD/flow_fields.npz` stays byte-identical to the field both
the manuscript and GATE G1 were computed on. The Monte Carlo is then the only variable
that moves, which is the point of the exercise. Everything replaced was copied to
`_pre_rerun_2026-08-29/` first.

97,200 trajectories, 648 cells x 150 throws, 18 workers, about 110 minutes.

`n_delivered`, `n_airborne`, `schema_frames`, and a `prov_*` block carrying the git SHA,
library versions, integrator tolerances and the SHA-256 of the input field. And:

The question "which gust model is the manuscript's Q built on?" now has an answer that
lives in the artifact rather than in an inference from file timestamps.

## The conclusions did not move

This is the result. 17 of 58 results macros and 19 of 35 significance macros changed
value; **none were added or removed**, so the manuscript's `\input` wiring and its
`\MISSINGNUM` guard are intact. Every verdict is unchanged, and the surviving claim is
*better* supported than before:

| comparison | before | after | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90 vs 210 | p = 0.206, CI [−0.0095, +0.0018], 4/12 | p = 0.203, CI [−0.0092, +0.0021], 4/12 | **not resolved**, unchanged |
| 90 vs 270 | p = 0.031, 11/12 | **p = 0.022**, 10/12 | resolved, unchanged |
| 210 vs 270 | p = 0.026, 10/12 | **p = 0.0038**, 11/12 | resolved, unchanged |

`worst_supported: 270`, `best_supported: null`. The mean Q at 270° fell from 0.0356 to
0.0324, which is why both comparisons against it tightened. H1 stays SUPPORTED, H2 stays
SUPPORTED / CONTRADICTED, H3 stays PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, and the hammer stays NOT TESTABLE
for the same stated reason (its 12.1 m still-air range cannot reach a 25 m target, so its
0% success rate measures the target choice, not the wind).

That the ordering survives a full regeneration under a corrected gust model, a corrected
reference frame and a corrected throw-exclusion flag is the strongest evidence yet that
"270° is the worst of the three computed" is a property of the field rather than of the
pipeline.

## One defect this exposed

The manuscript hardcoded "the across-field standard deviation of Q is **9 to 14 times**
the Monte Carlo standard error", a number typed into prose in a project whose whole
convention is that manuscript numbers are generated. The regenerated values are 12, 13
and 13. The sentence now reads them from `\QsdOverSeH*`, so it cannot drift again.
