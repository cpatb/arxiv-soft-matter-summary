# Spec: three products in computational rheology

Written 2026-08-27. Covers ideas 1 to 3 from the 26 AUG monetization pass: `dst-fit`, the
measurement-to-solver bridge, and simulation-as-a-service. Direction and market landscape
are in memory as `monetization-direction` and `computational-rheology-software-landscape`.

---

## 0. The operating model, because it changes every spec below

Human budget is 5 to 10 hrs/week. Execution budget is overnight agentic sessions on a max
subscription. These are different resources and should not be spent on the same things.

Human hours buy decisions: gate verdicts, buyer calls, bench measurements, and the
judgement calls this document deliberately leaves open. Overnight hours buy execution,
which is why every unit of work below is written as an *overnight unit*, meaning a scoped
task with an acceptance gate defined before the run.

Five rules carried over from what has already gone wrong in past sessions:

1. The gate is written before the run and enforced in code. A criterion applied by hand at
   read-time gets skipped, and a criterion an agent invents at 3am is not a criterion.
2. Subagent budget is the limiter, not compute. Two or three concurrent, not five. Five
   reading-heavy subagents once burned a whole night in about 90 minutes.
3. Prefer agents that return a conclusion over agents that edit files. A killed reader
   loses nothing. A killed editor leaves a half-applied change. When an agent must edit,
   scope it to one file.
4. Launch long compute in the first few calls, then analyse around it.
5. Done, per unit, means tests green, the gate verdict recorded in the repo (pass or fail,
   since a fail is data), and one artifact a human can read in 90 seconds.

One note that matters for section 1: a failed gate is not a blocked night. It is the
result. Several gates below are more interesting when they fail.

## 1. `dst-fit`, constitutive identification for dense suspensions

### What it is

A Python package that identifies dense-suspension constitutive parameters from flow-curve
data and reports whether the data can identify them at all. The second half is the product.
The first half is table stakes.

### Why the slot is open

pyRheo (RSC *Digital Discovery* 2025), hermes-rheo (JOSS, Jan 2026) and RepTate all target
polymer viscoelasticity: creep, relaxation, SAOS, fractional models. None of them fit
phi-dependent thickening. Nothing packaged takes a flow curve and returns phi_m, phi_0,
sigma*, or a frictional fraction. Instrument vendors do not either.

### Scope, v1

Input is stress, shear-rate and viscosity flow curves, optionally as a phi-series. Models
are Wyart-Cates (steady), Maron-Pierce, Cross, Carreau, and Herschel-Bulkley with the
regularization made explicit rather than implied. Output is parameters, uncertainties, and
an identifiability verdict. Readers cover TA TRIOS `.xls` exports (six are already on disk)
and plain CSV.

Explicitly out of v1: transient and thixotropic fitting, LAOS, anything viscoelastic. That
is where the competition already lives.

### The differentiator, stated precisely

`diff_calibrate.py` currently recovers (phi, phi_m, sigma*) to three digits from data
generated by the same model it fits. That is the synthetic-omits-what-breaks-it trap in its
purest form. The recovery demonstrates that autodiff works and says nothing whatever about
real data. Two capabilities have to exist before the package makes any claim.

**Misspecification detection.** Generate with model A, fit with model B. The tool has to
report a poor or degenerate fit instead of returning confident wrong parameters.

**Practical identifiability.** Fit, compute the parameter covariance from the Hessian or
Fisher information, then report the condition number and all pairwise correlations. Flag any
pair with |r| > 0.95 as degenerate and say so in plain language. phi, phi_m and sigma* are
expected to correlate strongly on single flow curves, so the honest answer for much real
data is "your data constrains phi_m − phi, not phi_m and phi separately." Nobody else ships
that sentence.

### Data already on disk

`Desktop\Projects\Corn Starch Bulk Rheology AM\` holds six TA flow sweeps at phi = 40, 45,
47, 50, 52 and 54 % (rundate Aug 2023, with normal stress recorded, which is a DST
discriminator worth keeping). Each sheet has a title row, a column-name row, a units row,
then data, alongside a `Details` sheet.

Stress and normal stress are exported in **MPa**, so a corn-starch sweep sits around 1e-8 to
1e-4 in the stored column and prints as a row of zeros under any default formatting. The
values themselves are fine, carrying six significant figures. The reader converts to SI and
also recomputes stress as eta times gamma-dot, then compares the two, which is a real test
of the unit interpretation because misreading MPa as Pa would show up as a factor of 1e6.

*(Corrected 2026-08-27 during N1. An earlier draft of this section claimed the stress column
rounds to a literal 0.0 for 17 of 25 points and is unusable. That was a display artifact of
how the column was first printed, not a property of the file.)*

### Validation plan, thresholds fixed before the runs

| Gate | Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | Round-trip on synthetic data from the fitted model | Recovery within stated uncertainty (already passes; keep as regression) |
| G2 | Misspecification: fit HB data with WC and vice versa | Reports poor-fit or degenerate. Fails if it returns a confident number |
| G3 | Real cornstarch phi-series | phi_m consistent across at least 4 of 6 curves within 2 sigma |
| G4 | Literature cross-check, 2 or more published silica DST papers | Recovered phi_m within the paper's own value ±0.02 |
| G5 | Edges: 3-point curves, Newtonian data, monotone no-thickening data | **PASS 2026-08-27.** All six sub-gates, including the always-cries-wolf control. Extended during the run to cover whether the sweep brackets the frictional transition at all |

G3 is the real one. phi_m is a material property, so six independent fits of the same
material have to agree. If they do not, either the model or the fit is wrong, and finding
out which is worth more than the package.

### What N1 and N2 found

**The rate truncation is not a censoring problem, which reverses what N1 first concluded.**
phi = 0.52 and 0.54 stop at 214 1/s while the others reach about 995, and the obvious read
is that the two most informative curves were cut short. On the stress axis, where the model
is actually parameterised, the opposite holds: their thickening branches span 3.44 and 3.65
decades of stress, the widest of the six, against 2.66 to 3.47 for the four that ran to full
rate. Thickening makes stress climb superlinearly with rate, so those sweeps reach 1774 and
3698 Pa despite stopping at a fifth of the rate. What they actually have is fewer points on
the branch, 8 and 9 against 11 and 12, which is milder and different. Judging censoring on
the rate axis when the model lives on the stress axis was the error.

**Wyart-Cates has no thinning branch, and the corn starch thins before it thickens.** All
six sweeps fall by a decade or more before rising, so fitting the model to a whole sweep is
a category error. The fit is taken from the viscosity minimum onward, and because the
location of that cut moves the recovered sigma*, the cut is recorded on the fit result
rather than buried in it.

**The uncertainty estimate is calibrated.** G1 was run as a coverage test rather than a
single recovery: over 100 noise realisations per model the 2-sigma interval contains the
truth 93 to 97% of the time against a nominal 95.4%. That is the claim `diff_calibrate.py`
could not make, and it is what lets N4's real-data error bars be quoted at all.

**One pre-registered gate failed and was replaced.** G1c asked that truncation inflate the
sigma* standard error by 3x or more; measured 1.14x. The gate named the wrong parameter:
sigma* is set by where the frictional transition sits, which the retained data still
brackets, while the high-stress plateau is what constrains phi_m. Under cuts to 80, 60 and
50% of the stress range, phi_m's error inflates 1.31x, 8.52x and 154.70x while phi's moves
0.99x, 1.10x and 1.13x. The fitter was right and the gate was wrong. It is recorded as
failed rather than deleted, and replaced by a structural claim (phi_m must lose more
precision than phi at every cut) that cannot be tuned to make a run pass.

Also worth not doing: the stress at minimum viscosity rises with phi across the series
(0.11, 0.29, 0.36, 0.46, 0.65, 0.84 Pa), which invites reading it as sigma*. It is not. The
viscosity minimum sits where the thinning branch gives way to the thickening one, so it
moves with the thinning physics too. Whether sigma* is phi-independent here is a question
for the real fit in N4.

### The G3 decision is now live

G3 failed, so the question the spec reserved for you is on the table: **is Wyart-Cates wrong
for this material, or is this 2023 corn-starch data wrong?** Neither answer is available from
the numbers alone, which is why it was reserved.

What the run establishes. Every individual fit is good: residuals consistent with noise on
all six, rmse 0.075 to 0.253 in log viscosity. The fitter is not confused about the data,
because fitted phi reproduces the nominal label from curve shape alone at r2 = 0.99925,
slope 0.970, intercept +0.052, and that offset points the way corn-starch granules are known
to go since they swell. What fails is only the cross-curve consistency the model requires:
phi_m is supposed to be a particle property and it slides from 0.492 to 0.580. sigma* slides
too, 2.08 to 11.30 Pa, a factor of 5.4, where the model expects it set by the repulsive force
scale and therefore fixed.

Four candidates, none tested, ordered by how cheap they are to rule out:

1. **`phi_0` and `eta_s` are pinned** at 0.64 and the viscosity of water. Corn-starch granules
   are polydisperse, non-spherical and swollen, so 0.64 is likely wrong, and a wrong `phi_0`
   is absorbed straight into `phi_m`. Cheapest test: free `phi_0` in a joint fit across all
   six curves with one shared `phi_m`, and see whether the chi-squared collapses.
2. **Swelling is concentration-dependent.** Less water per granule at high phi means less
   swelling, which would make the effective packing genuinely vary and `phi_m` genuinely not
   be a constant for this material. That would be a real result, not a fitting artifact.
3. **The nominal figure may be a mass fraction** with a conversion to volume that itself
   depends on concentration.
4. **The branch cut** at the viscosity minimum is a choice, and `sigma*` moves with it. A cut
   sensitivity sweep is already supported through `thickening_branch(pad=...)`.

**Candidate (1) was run the same day and is dead.** The joint fit with a shared `phi_m` and
`phi_0` freed loses to six independent fits by 55 AIC, the residual nearly doubles, and the
one external check available (fitted phi against the nominal label) degrades from r2 =
0.99925 to 0.959. Freeing `phi_0` does not rescue a shared `phi_m`. Code in
`dst_fit/joint.py`, gate in `scripts/gate_n5.py`.

That leaves (2) and (3), which are two readings of the same physics: for swollen deformable
granules the effective volume fraction and the packing both depend on water availability, so
`phi_m` is genuinely not a constant for this material. (4) is cheap and worth running for
completeness but cannot produce a monotone drift of 0.492 to 0.580 on its own.

**The product decision this forces, and it is a good one.** Corn starch is a poor calibration
standard for a package that sells recovering material constants. `dst-fit` should be
validated against silica, where particles are hard, monodisperse and non-swelling, which
makes **N5 (literature digitization, gate G4) the critical path rather than a nice-to-have**.
The corn-starch series then ships as the worked example of the tool refusing a model, which
is a better demonstration than a clean fit: every individual fit looks good, residuals
consistent with noise on all six, and only the cross-curve consistency check catches it.

One gate-design note worth carrying forward. G6a asked only that the shared-parameter
standard errors be *finite*. They were, at `phi_0 = 0.691 +/- 924` and `phi_m = 0.595 +/-
796`, because with a shared `sigma*` the two packing fractions trade off almost perfectly.
Finite is not the same as meaningful, and the gate passed a fit that determines nothing. The
verdict did not depend on it, since G6b and G6d fail independently, but a future
"did it converge" gate should test that the error is small relative to the parameter, not
merely that it is a number.

### Decisions left to you, at the point they bind

Which models ship in v1, before N2. Whether the G3 threshold should stay at 2 sigma across
at least 4 of 6 or go stricter, before N4, and it has to be fixed before the run rather than
after seeing the spread. Then the one only you can answer: if G3 fails, is the model wrong
or is this cornstarch data wrong? That decides whether the package ships with Wyart-Cates at
all.

### What kills it, and the fallback decided in advance

If G3 fails badly because Wyart-Cates genuinely does not describe this cornstarch, the
product shrinks to HB, Cross and Maron-Pierce with identifiability. That is still unoccupied
and still worth shipping. Deciding now that you would ship it stops a failed gate from
reading as a failed project.

### Deliverable

A PyPI package and a JOSS paper. hermes-rheo published exactly this way in January 2026,
which is proof the path is fast and produces a citable artifact.

## 2. Measurement to model to solver bridge

### What it is

Takes an identified model out of `dst-fit` and emits a solver-ready configuration, with the
regularization chosen, documented, and its effect on the answer measured.

### The pain, precisely

Herschel-Bulkley has no unique numerical realization. Papanastasiou regularization,
bi-viscosity and simple clamping give different answers. Each carries a parameter (m,
eta_max, gamma-dot_c) that changes the result, and that parameter is almost never reported.
Two groups "using HB with tau_y = 10, k = 5, n = 0.5" get different answers and neither
knows why.

### Scope, v1, deliberately narrow

One fluid class: generalized Newtonian (HB, Cross, Carreau). Not viscoelastic, not WC. Two
targets: OpenFOAM (`transportProperties`) and Basilisk (a header), with Fluent only on
request. Per case it emits the config file plus a regularization sensitivity report, meaning
the same case at three regularization strengths with the spread in a stated quantity of
interest.

The sensitivity report is the product. Anyone can emit a config file. The defensible
artifact is the sentence "your regularization is moving this answer by 18%," which is the
same skill as the hoop-term finding and goes straight into an engineer's deck.

### Build gate, do not skip this

Five buyer conversations before any code. A yes is specific: someone describes the problem,
says what they currently do, and says why it is wrong, slow, or unreportable. Two of five
means build. Fewer means park it.

These calls are free, because they are the same calls as section 3's outreach with one extra
question appended.

### Overnight units, only after the gate passes

| # | Work | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | Three HB regularizations against an analytic case (Bingham Poiseuille has one) | All three converge to analytic in the limit and diverge measurably away from it |
| N2 | Config emitters | Round-trip: emit, run, recover the input parameters |
| N3 | Sensitivity report generator | Reproduces N1's spread from the report path alone |

### Risk, stated plainly

This may turn out to be a feature of `dst-fit` rather than a product of its own. That is an
acceptable outcome, and the buyer gate exists to find it out cheaply instead of after three
months of building.

## 3. Simulation-as-a-service

### What it is

Non-Newtonian CFD studies delivered as reports. $3,000 to $15,000 per study, fixed fee,
defined scope, one revision.

### Why it fits 5 to 10 hrs/week

You bill the deliverable, not the hours, and the overnight loop runs the sweep. A study that
costs a consultancy two engineer-weeks is one weekend of your decisions plus three overnight
runs. This is the only item here that produces revenue in 2026, and it is the reason the
other two are worth building.

### Positioning

Not "CFD consulting," which is a commodity and would put you against firms with sales teams.
Instead:

> Second opinions and validation for non-Newtonian simulations you already have, plus new
> studies.

The validation offer is unusual, cheap to deliver, and high trust, and it converts naturally
into the study. It is also the thing you have the strongest evidence for.

### The sales asset already exists

The axisymmetric hoop term computed wrong on every radial face in five solvers, `u_z/r`
where it should be `u_r/r`, confirmed from source, with the error *growing* 4.19x from L8 to
L9, so refinement ladders were measuring the bug and reading it as "not yet converged."

The bidet tau_c = 1 Pa cleaning radius that turned out to be a mesh boundary rather than a
result.

The datacenter containment sweep recomputed under the CFD recirculation model, which cut the
credited delta-PUE by 42%.

All three are your own work being corrected. Say that explicitly, because it is what makes
the claim credible. The through-line of the offer is that you find the error in the
simulation everyone else already shipped.

### What you need before selling, all cheap

A one-page site, since `cpatb.github.io` exists as an unpopulated Jekyll scaffold. Three
case write-ups, one page each, covering the three findings above, humanizer pass on each
before publishing. A rate card and scope template: fixed fee, defined deliverable, one
revision.

### Channels, ranked

Direct outreach to formulation and process R&D (coatings, inks, battery slurry, cement and
DIW inks, personal care), then Kolabtree, then expert networks, which are lumpy and better
in 2027 once the PhD is done.

### Overnight units

Site build. The three case write-ups, one per session. A reusable study template covering
mesh, run, convergence, sensitivity, report.

### The constraint that will actually break this

Outreach and calls are human hours and agents cannot supply them. Budget 2 of your 5 to 10
hrs/week to outreach or this does not happen. It is the most common failure mode for this
exact plan, and none of the overnight capacity substitutes for it.
