# The Rayleigh axis: how far this solver goes, and what the containment gap does on the way

**2026-09-02.** Status-card item 1 — *"re-measure the `eta` axis at hall-like Ra"* — which
the card calls the most decision-relevant open number in the project. Code:
`cfd/run_ra_ladder.py`; data: `cfd/cfd_ra_ladder.json`.

**This does not reach a hall, and it was never going to.** A hall is `Ra` ~ 1e9–1e12 and the
`cfd/aisle.py` solver is a two-dimensional laminar vorticity–streamfunction code. What it
does instead is establish where the solver stops being *allowed* to answer, and measure the
containment gap over the range where it is — which is a prerequisite for any statement about
high `Ra` and was written down nowhere.

## The admissibility criterion, fixed before the runs

In laminar convection the thermal boundary layer thins as `δ/H ~ Ra^(-1/4)`, so resolving it
needs `N ≥ 3·Ra^(1/4)`. Every rung picks its own `N` from that rule rather than inheriting
the calibration's `N = 40`, and every record carries `n_bl` — the cells the criterion says
are in the layer — so the ladder can be re-graded against a different bar without re-running.

⚠ **That criterion retroactively bounds the existing calibration.** `run_calibration.py`
runs at `N = 40`, which admits `Ra ≤ 3e4`. Its two points (4e3, 8e3) sit inside it with
`n_bl` = 5.0 and 4.2, so they are fine — but the same script at `Ra = 1e5` would not have
been, and nothing in the tree said so.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4e3 | 40 | 5.03 | 0.2411 | 0.0729 | **0.1682** | 0.1571 | 0.0016 |
| 8e3 | 40 | 4.23 | 0.2268 | 0.0704 | **0.1564** | 0.2051 | 0.0024 |
| 3e4 | 40 | 3.04 | 0.2239 | 0.0624 | **0.1615** | 0.2986 | 0.0021 |
| 1e5 | 54 | 3.04 | 0.2090 | 0.0457 | **0.1633** | 0.2823 | 0.0004 |

Only the two containment endpoints are swept, because the question the ROI asks is whether
the **gap** survives, not the shape of the curve between them.

## Three results, in order of how much they change

### 1. The containment gap is flat over 250× in Ra

`φ(0) − φ(0.95)` = 0.1682, 0.1564, 0.1615, 0.1633, 0.1580, 0.1575. **A 7 % spread with no
trend**, across a Rayleigh range of 250. Both endpoints fall individually — `φ(0)` by 24 %,
`φ(0.95)` by **66 %** — and they fall *together*, so the difference does not move. The
largest single step in the gap (0.1682 → 0.1564, between the two lowest rungs) is larger
than anything in the four decades above it.

That is the answer the containment ROI needed, as far as this solver can give it: **over the
range where the model is admissible, raising `Ra` does not erode the benefit of containment.**
It is an extrapolation of three to six decades to reach a hall and it must be labelled as one, but a
flat trend extrapolates better than a moving one, and the previous state of knowledge was two
points at one decade.

### 2. It independently corroborates the 31 AUG retraction of the "7 % floor"

The Tier-1 headline retracted on 2026-08-31 was a *"~7 % recirculation floor at perfect
containment"*, shown by a Lewis-number sweep to be **thermal diffusion rather than
recirculated air**. This ladder reaches the same conclusion down a completely different axis,
and it was not designed to:

- `φ(η = 0.95)` — the temperature channel, the one that produced the 7 % — reads **0.0729 at
  Ra = 4e3 and 0.0249 at Ra = 1e6**. It falls by **2.9×**, monotonically, at every rung.
- the passive tracer at the same `η` reads **0.0016 → 0.0000**: essentially zero everywhere,
  and falling faster than the temperature channel does.

Raising `Ra` strengthens advection against diffusion. A genuinely advective recirculation
would be roughly Ra-invariant once inertial; a diffusive one must shrink. **The floor
shrinks, and the advective channel says there was never anything there.** So "7 %" is not
even a fixed diffusive number — it is the diffusive floor *at this solver's Ra*, and it is
smaller at every step toward a real hall.

⚠ Do not read this as "the floor at hall Ra is 2.5 % or below" without saying it is an
extrapolation past the admissibility wall below.

### 3. The wall, and what it is made of

Two separate limits, and only one is about resolution:

- **Resolution.** `N ≥ 3·Ra^(1/4)` with the script's `N_MAX = 160` caps the ladder at
  `Ra = (160/3)⁴ ≈ 8.1e6`. Cost then grows as roughly `N³·√Ra`, so pushing to that cap is
  possible but the return is small: the gap is already flat. (The `Ra = 1e6` rung needs `N = 95`,
  is inside the cap, and completed: 25 000 and 16 000 steps, 4.6 and 4.0 minutes.)
- **Physics, and this one cannot be bought.** Above `Ra` ~ 1e8–1e9 the flow is not laminar
  and it is not two-dimensional. A finer grid on a 2-D laminar model does not approach a
  turbulent 3-D hall; it converges to the wrong answer more precisely. `run_ra_ladder.py`
  marks rungs above `TRANSITION_RA = 1e8` inadmissible **for that reason** and refuses to
  run them, rather than reporting a number and a caveat.

**So the honest statement of the project's position on Ra is: the gap is flat over
4e3 → 1e6, the diffusive floor falls 2.9× over the same range, and the remaining three to six decades to
a hall are not reachable by refining this model — they need a turbulence closure or a 3-D
LES, which is `DESIGN.md` §8's standing gap.** That is a smaller claim than "re-measured at
hall-like Ra" and it is one the code actually supports.

## What this does not do

- **`η` is sampled at two points, not swept.** The 48-cell `φ(η, p)` surface stays at its own
  Ra. If the *shape* of `φ(η)` matters — and for a partial-containment ROI it might — that is
  a separate ladder.
- **No through-flow.** `flux = 0` throughout, so this is the buoyancy-only limit. The 31 AUG
  provisioning work showed over-provisioning suppresses recirculation 5.5× between `p` = 0
  and 1; that axis is orthogonal to this one and is not crossed here.
- **The tracer's own diffusivity is at its historical `Le = 50` value**, not swept. The Lewis
  sweep is what established the diffusion diagnosis; this ladder is a second, independent
  line of evidence, not a repeat of it.
